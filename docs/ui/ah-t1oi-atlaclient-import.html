ah‑t1oi — importing a map exported by AtlaClient

Chosen by the navigator, 2026‑09‑08, from two variants. The file is recognised on the way in like every other kind of file, and gets the same box under the header that one of our own map exports gets — the same two buttons, the same focus and Esc behaviour. Two paragraphs are reworded, because an AtlaClient map names no faction, and there is a third paragraph that breaks the file's hexes down by the turn they were actually seen. An AtlaClient map is unlike one of ours in exactly this way: ours is normally one player's current knowledge of a rectangle, while an AtlaClient map is a lifetime's accumulation with turn stamps ranging over the whole game. The extra line is what makes Cancel a real choice rather than a formality.

What the file looks like

Real, from the navigator's own game. ;16 means “this hex as of turn 16”; ;16‑11 means “file written on turn 16, this hex last seen on turn 11”; ;16‑0 means AtlaClient itself does not know when — the hex came into it from some other tool's import.

The middle paragraph is dimmer than the other two: it is detail, not the decision. Each of its three clauses disappears when its count is zero, so a file whose hexes are all current says only “83 hexes are as new as turn 16.”

Refusals

could not read atlaclient‑map.16 — the AtlaClient map does not say which turn it was written on

could not read atlaclient‑map.16 — the AtlaClient map has no hexes in it

The faction refusal our own exports have cannot apply: an AtlaClient map never names a faction, and that is normal rather than a fault.

The other three decisions

Recognition is automatic, from the file's content, like every other kind of file this application takes. The tell is the 60‑character underline ending in ;16 or ;16‑11, which no real turn report has.

Hexes with no turn are added as turn 0 — the oldest a hex can be, so they can never displace anything the map already knows and the map's own shading calls them very old. Their terrain and province name is most of what a never‑seen hex is worth.

The Import button's file filter goes. AtlaClient names its exports after the turn number and nothing else, so atlaclient‑map.16 is greyed out by today's accept list and can only be dragged in. Everything is judged by content anyway, so the filter buys nothing and costs a file that cannot be picked.

An AtlaClient import leaves a row in Merged reports, reading AtlaClient · turn 16. One of our own map exports leaves none, because it is our own map; this one can drop turn‑5 hexes onto a turn‑71 map, and the panel exists to answer where they came from a week later.

The cost

One more sentence of counting in the core (three numbers out of the ages the reader already builds) and one more paragraph of prose with its own test. No new surface, no new control, no new state.
