ah-ehgy — MOVE, and the structure a walker leaves behind (round 1)

What is settled and not being re-opened. The Structure popup’s shape — two lines, was above structure, with the order that did it as a dim trailing clause — was chosen in ah-rgkk.5.3 and ships today. Nothing below changes it.

What is wrong today. The preview changes a unit’s structure from ENTER and LEAVE and nothing else. But rules/move gives “2) A structure number” and “3) OUT, which will leave the structure that the unit is in” as directions of their own. So a unit ordered MOVE OUT or MOVE 12 keeps drawing where the report found it, and its popup says Nothing this month changes this. about a unit that moved. Separately, a unit that walks to another hex cannot take its fort with it — and its arriving row says In no structure. Nothing this month changes this. about a unit the report had inside one.

One question, Q1, and it is only about the third case — the walker. Everything else follows from the shape already chosen.

Settled — MOVE OUT and MOVE 12 read exactly like LEAVE and ENTER

These are not questions. The pair already says everything; the only new thing is that the trailing clause names the order actually written, so a MOVE OUT is never reported as a LEAVE.

Q1 — the walker’s arriving row

The scene. Rangers (1533) was found by the report inside Odds and Ends [12] · Fort in plain (21,33) in Tanoshi. This month you write MOVE N. Two rows are drawn: a departing row in the origin hex, still inside the fort, and an arriving row in forest (21,31) in Tanoshi. A unit cannot take a building with it, so the arriving row is in no structure — and today it says so as if nothing had happened.

The Structure cell of that arriving row is empty either way: the unit is in no structure, and the cell has nothing to print. On the walker that enters something when it gets there. MOVE N 12 walks north and then enters structure 12 in the hex it arrives in — a different building from the one it left, which may happen to carry the same number and which the destination’s report often does not describe. That case is drawn above and needs no decision: it is an ordinary was/structure pair whose after-side falls back to the bare number, exactly as an undescribed structure does anywhere else.
