A passage, in words — round two

The shape is settled. What is left is every word a player will actually read, and one mark on the map.

Settled in round one. The brass route line stops at the structure's hex and that hex carries a ring; nothing is drawn beyond a passage whose far side is unknown. The trouble is a counted problem in a new Movement group, found the same way an overloaded fleet is. Where the far side is known, each level of the map draws its own half of the journey, joined by a pair of rings that name each other.

Question 4 — the problem line

Problem lines in Atlantis HUD sit in three places at once from one source, with the unit's number in a column of its own, so the sentence itself starts lower-case and talks about “this unit”. The house shape is the trouble, a colon, the facts, and what follows from it — as in “this unit is overloaded: it carries 512 and the most it can move with is 500, so it will not move”.

Which sentence ships? All three are shown in the region panel's Problems section, beneath a real one for company.

W1 The passage is the subject

Problems2
1841⚠the passage in Shaft [3] leads somewhere no report has shown: the 2 steps ordered after it cannot be placed on the map.
2207⚠this unit is overloaded: it carries 512 and the most it can move with is 500, so it will not move.
  • Names the thing that is unknown first, which is what the player must act on.
  • Breaks the “this unit…” opening its neighbours share.

W2 The unit is the subject

Problems2
1841⚠this unit goes through the passage in Shaft [3], and no report says where that passage comes out, so the 2 steps after it cannot be placed on the map.
2207⚠this unit is overloaded: it carries 512 and the most it can move with is 500, so it will not move.
  • Opens exactly like its neighbours and ends with the consequence, as they do.
  • States plainly that the gap is in the reports, not in the orders.
  • The longest of the three.

W3 Short

Problems2
1841⚠this unit goes through an unseen passage in Shaft [3]: the rest of its route cannot be drawn.
2207⚠this unit is overloaded: it carries 512 and the most it can move with is 500, so it will not move.
  • Reads fastest in a list of seven.
  • Drops the step count, so the player cannot tell how much is missing.
  • “Unseen passage” could be read as a passage nobody knows exists.

Question 5 — the words on the map

The ring on the structure's hex is hovered like anything else on the map. What it says there.

Which hover ships?

H1 Full, with the count

Through the passage in Shaft [3] Where this passage comes out is not in any report yet, so the rest of the journey — 2 more steps — cannot be drawn.
  • Says the same thing as the problem line, so the two never disagree.
  • Three lines in a narrow window.

H2 Brief

Through the passage in Shaft [3] Where it comes out has not been seen. 2 more steps, not drawn.
  • Two short lines; the numbers still there.
  • Clipped enough to read as a machine's note rather than the app's voice.

H3 The known passage, for comparison

Whatever is chosen above, the hover on a passage whose far side is known reads like this — and the matching ring on the other level says the mirror of it.

Through the passage in Shaft [3] Comes out in cavern (12,34), in the underworld. Costs 2 movement points, the cost of entering that cavern.
Out of the passage from Shaft [3] On the surface, in mountain (7,53). The journey carries on from here.

Question 6 — what the ring looks like

Three rings are needed: one for a passage going nowhere we know, and a matched pair for one whose ends are both known. The route line already stops dead at the hex; the ring says why.

Which set of marks ships?

M1 A question mark, and arrows for the two ends

  • The arrow says which way through the world the unit went, which is the one fact the player needs to know which level to look at.
  • Arrows mean down and up, and two passages on the same level mean neither.

M2 A question mark, and a pair of linked rings

  • One glyph whichever way the passage runs, and on any level.
  • Reads as “this joins to somewhere else” without claiming a direction.
  • A new glyph the player meets nowhere else.

M3 A question mark, and the level's own name

  • Says outright where the other end is, with no glyph to learn.
  • Long words on a hex at far zoom; nothing else on the map is lettered like this.

Question 7 — the switch

Every advisory in Atlantis HUD can be turned off from Settings → Warnings, in a group with a heading. This is the first entry in its group.

What is the group called, what is the entry called, and is it on to begin with?

T1 Movement / Passages with no known exit / on

Settings — Warnings
Movement
Passages with no known exit
A route goes through a passage inside a structure, and no report says where it leads.
  • Movement sits beside the existing Sailing, Orders and Studying/Teaching groups and has obvious room to grow.
  • On by default: a route drawn short with no explanation is worse than a line in a list.

T2 Movement / Routes through unseen passages / on

Settings — Warnings
Movement
Routes through unseen passages
A route goes through a passage inside a structure that nobody has been through yet.
  • Named for the route rather than the passage, which is what the player was doing.
  • “Unseen” again risks reading as a passage nobody knows about.
