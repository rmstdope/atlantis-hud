The one row that no clause covers well

“Both at once” is men joining and men leaving. Every option above describes it with a clause about one side or with a clause about nothing in particular. Writing a sentence that names both movements means a fifth shape, and the numbers it would carry (two joined, one left, 2 → 0) do not add up in any obvious way for a reader — the level fell because of dilution, not because of the man who left.

My recommendation is to accept that and let whichever clause you choose cover it, rather than build a shape for the rarest case. Say so if you would rather it were spelled out.
