A mage sheet in a pile of files — what a batch import does with it

Dropping twenty files at once already takes a road of its own: the batch decides for itself and never asks, and tells you what it did in one summary dialog at the end. A mage sheet dropped in that pile has to be recognised whatever we do — left alone it parses as a report and gets merged into your map as an ally's turn, which would put phantom hexes on it. The question is only what the batch does once it has recognised one.

Variant 1 — taken in, and named in the summary

VARIANT 1The batch handles it

A sheet in the pile is taken in like any other step, and the summary says so. The refusals still apply — an own-faction or older sheet becomes a skipped line instead.
4 files: 1 turn imported, 1 report merged, 1 mage sheet taken in, 1 skipped
  • knights-t23.rep — imported as turn 23
  • borg-t23.rep — merged into your map, turn 23
  • mages-Borg-turn-23.txt — mage sheet from Borg (21), turn 23: 6 mages taken in
  • mages-Borg-turn-21.txt — skipped: you already hold Borg (21)'s mages from turn 23, which is newer
Close
  • Dragging the whole email folder onto the window does the obvious thing to everything in it.
  • A sheet is never mistaken for a report, and the summary is the one place that already accounts for every file.
  • Sheets are taken in with no chance to refuse, even if you chose Option A for a single file — a batch never asks, by design.

Variant 2 — recognised, and skipped

VARIANT 2One at a time, please

The batch recognises a mage sheet, does nothing with it, and says why. You open it on its own.
4 files: 1 turn imported, 1 report merged, 2 skipped
  • knights-t23.rep — imported as turn 23
  • borg-t23.rep — merged into your map, turn 23
  • mages-Borg-turn-23.txt — skipped: a mage sheet is taken in on its own, not with a batch of reports
  • mages-Borg-turn-21.txt — skipped: a mage sheet is taken in on its own, not with a batch of reports
Close
  • Whatever you chose for a single sheet, that is the only road a sheet ever takes — one behaviour, not two.
  • Less to build and less to get wrong.
  • A player who drops the folder has to find the sheets again and open each one.
  • Reads as a failure in the summary when nothing was actually wrong with the file.
