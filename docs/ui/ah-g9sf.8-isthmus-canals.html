ah-g9sf.8 — sailing through a neck of land, and the two grades of canal

A fleet cannot barge straight across a strip of land. It must leave by the side it entered or a side beside that one (newage/trident rules/movement_sailing). A canal in that region lifts the restriction, and the two grades are priced apart: a stone Canal's through-pass costs 2 movement points, a Mystic Canal's costs 1, where an ordinary sailing step costs 1 (newage/trident rules/economy_canals, newage/trident data/Canal, newage/trident data/Mystic Canal).

Today Atlantis HUD knows none of it. It plans the shortcut with confidence, prices every sailing step at 1, marks nothing in Problems, and the turn comes back with the fleet exactly where it started — or a month later than promised.

The shape agreed: the app refuses in the player's own words when the crossing is illegal, and says nothing extra when it is legal. The building's name carries its price. No new colour on the map, no new panel, no advice about what to build.

The situation three hexes, used in every card below

A one-hex-wide plain between two stretches of ocean. The fleet is in the ocean top left and wants the ocean bottom right. Straight through is SE SE — in one side of the plain, out the opposite side. That is the illegal move. The legal way is E SE S, three steps by open water.

1 · The planner, when there is no way through at all

Suppose the far ocean is a landlocked pocket: through the plain is the only route, and it is illegal. The planner names the restriction and the hex it stopped at, in the voice of the fleet refusal already beside it. It says nothing about canals — the app states what will happen, it does not recommend construction projects.

3 · A step through a canal

Put a canal in the plain and the shortcut becomes legal, at the grade's own price. The suffix names the building, so the cost follows from it — the shape · road already has on a land step. A canal seen once counts for ever: canals do not fall down, and a remembered one is drawn exactly like one seen this month.

4 · The player typed the illegal order themselves

A typed SAIL gets no planner and no list of steps anywhere: it is drawn on the map, and the words live in Problems. The line is solid as far as the month reaches and as far as the game will allow, then dotted. A step the game refuses is therefore already dotted, and stays so — no new colour.
