Proposal 2 — an Army is a shape on the map

You are grouping units in order to fight with them, and a fight happens somewhere. The map is already the whole background of this application — use it.

Each Army takes a colour and an initial. Every hex holding one of its units carries that mark, so a force's footprint is visible without asking: concentrated, strung out over five hexes, or split either side of a mountain range. You said an Army's units are usually adjacent; this is the surface that shows you when they are not.

Assembly is spatial. The map already takes a shift-drag marquee — it is how a region is chosen for map export. Drag one over your force and every own unit inside it can be added in a single action. One unit at a time is still a click in the units table.

Drawn to the real chrome. Note how little room there is to spend: MapViewControls already occupies the top right, the panels are 80% opaque over the map, and the terrain colours are fixed by theme.css.

  • “Frame this Army” reuses the map framing the app already has, so an Army doubles as a bookmark for a place.
  • Adds nothing to the units table and nothing to the Unit panel beyond a read-only line.
  • Colour is the scarcest thing here. Terrain already spends fourteen colours, and badges and movement are drawn over it.
  • A hex mark says “some unit of this Army is here” — never which, never how many.

Own units only. A box over your own territory would otherwise sweep in every foreign scout standing in it.

  • A twelve-unit force is one drag and one click.
  • It matches how the force exists in the game — a body of men standing in a place.
  • Shift-drag is already taken by the map-export region marquee. Two meanings for one gesture; one of them has to move.
  • Zoomed out the box is imprecise; zoomed in it will not reach across a scattered force.
  • It cannot express “these three units but not that one” without a second gesture anyway.
