R3 — the words, exactly as they will ship

The shape is settled: B1 the material leaves and the hover explains; M1 spend the material it holds, mark it when it holds both; H1 count a BUILD HELP, mark an unfinished ship, leave a skill-less unit upright; P1 count each unit alone. What is left is every sentence.

The ITEMS hover is a stack of lines. Line one is always was: <the report’s list>, and it already exists. A PRODUCE already adds “Includes 10 WOOD this unit will produce. Production resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month.” and, when a cap bit, “This unit has materials for 5 swords, not the 8 its men could make.” A BUILD’s lines have to sit alongside those.

Four questions. They are independent; N is asked first because W and K both use whatever it decides.

N — how a structure is named in the hover

Two of the four are unnamed in the report and print a placeholder with a number; one has a real name. Whatever is chosen here is substituted into every sentence below.

N1 — the same label the warnings already use (structure_label: the report’s name, or the placeholder with its id appended). Today already-built says “Building 4 is already finished”.recommended

unit 5541… on Building 4 …
unit 5543… on Guild Hall …
  • One name for a structure across the whole application. A player who has read the already-built warning meets the identical phrase here.
  • Unambiguous when a hex holds three Stockades.
  • “Building 4” tells a player nothing about what is being built.

N2 — the structure’s kind

unit 5541… on the Stockade …
unit 5543… on the Stockade …
  • Says the thing the player actually cares about, and needs no cross-reference.
  • Two Stockades in one hex read identically, and a hex under construction usually has several structures.
  • Disagrees with the wording of the warning on the same order.

N3 — both

unit 5541… on Building 4, a Stockade, …
unit 5543… on Guild Hall, a Stockade, …
  • Unambiguous and meaningful at once.
  • Long, in a line that already has a figure and a unit in it, inside a hover that may carry four other lines.

W — the sentence saying what the material went on

Shown with N1 substituted. This line appears on every counted BUILD, capped or not.

W1recommended

hover on unit 5541's ITEMS cellwas: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Spends 30 WOOD on Building 4 this month.
  • Says the figure, the item and the destination in seven words.
  • “Spends” is the right verb: unlike a PRODUCE, the material is gone.

W2

hover on unit 5541's ITEMS cellwas: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD 30 WOOD goes into Building 4 this month.
  • Puts the figure first, where the eye lands.
  • “Goes into” reads as though the wood is stored there and could come back out.

W3

hover on unit 5541's ITEMS cellwas: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Building Building 4: 30 WOOD spent.
  • Shortest, and groups by subject if a unit ever had two builds.
  • “Building Building 4”. The placeholder name makes this form unusable.

K — the sentence naming what cut the work short

Only on a capped row. Two different caps: unit 5542 has too little wood, unit 5543’s structure wants less than its men could do. The PRODUCE cap sentence next door reads “This unit has materials for 5 swords, not the 8 its men could make.”

K1 — the same shape as the production cap sentence, one form per caprecommended

unit 5542 — short of woodwas: 10 HUMN, 15 WOOD Spends 15 WOOD on Building 4 this month. This unit has wood for 15 units of work, not the 30 its men could do.
unit 5543 — structure nearly finishedwas: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Spends 6 WOOD on Guild Hall this month. Guild Hall needs 6 more units of work, not the 30 its men could do.
  • A player who has learned to read the PRODUCE sentence reads this one without stopping.
  • The two caps are genuinely different facts and are said differently.
  • Two sentences to keep in step rather than one.

K2 — one form covering both

unit 5542was: 10 HUMN, 15 WOOD Spends 15 WOOD on Building 4 this month. Its men could have done 30.
unit 5543was: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Spends 6 WOOD on Guild Hall this month. Its men could have done 30.
  • One string, and it fits on the end of the line that is already there.
  • Never says why, which is the only reason a player hovers a capped row. Unit 5543’s player is left wondering whether they are short of wood.

K3 — fold the cap into the first sentence

unit 5542was: 10 HUMN, 15 WOOD Spends 15 of its 30 units of work on Building 4 this month, for want of wood.
unit 5543was: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Spends 6 of its 30 units of work on Guild Hall this month, which is all it needs.
  • One line rather than two, and the whole story is in it.
  • Stops naming the item. The column is a list of items, and the reader wants to know it is wood that moved.
  • Diverges from the PRODUCE sentence one row away, so the two columns word the same idea two ways.

D — does a BUILD HELP say something different?

Unit 5553 spends its own wood on a structure it is not responsible for. Its cell and its figure are the same either way; this is only about the sentence.

D1 — name the unit being helpedrecommended

hover on unit 5553's ITEMS cellwas: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Spends 30 WOOD helping unit 5541 build Building 4 this month.
  • A player scanning for why their wood is disappearing sees at once that it went to somebody else’s build.
  • Matches build-without-skill, which already says “cannot help build” rather than “cannot build”.
  • A second string, differing from W1 by four words.

D2 — say exactly what an ordinary build says

hover on unit 5553's ITEMS cellwas: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Spends 30 WOOD on Building 4 this month.
  • One string for every counted build; nothing extra to word or test.
  • Hides the one fact that makes this row unusual. The structure named is not one this unit is in, which is confusing rather than merely terse.
