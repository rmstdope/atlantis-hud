Question 1 — the unit the goods arrive at

TRANSPORT (and its synonym DISTRIBUTE) moves goods to a unit in another hex, up to two or three hexes away, through a quartermaster. Both ends of the order change: the sender ends the month holding less, the receiver holding more.

The sending end is not in question — goods leave, the list falls, exactly as a gift does today. This page is about the receiving unit's row, which sits in a different hex entirely and whose figure is decided by an order written in somebody else's block.

The timing fact that makes this a question. rules/sequenceofevents puts TRANSPORT dead last — after give, after the market, after movement, after production and building. So the goods really do arrive this month, but they arrive too late to be spent, sold, given or carried. The column already has a wording for exactly that shape, on production: Production resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month.

unit 5530  ; in mountain (34,2)
  transport 6857 30 STON

unit 6857  ; the quartermaster, in mountain (36,4) — two hexes away
  ; no orders of its own

  • The column keeps its one promise: what this unit will have. A quartermaster's whole job is receiving, so a quartermaster whose row never moves is the column at its least useful.
  • Italic already means this figure is a projection, and nothing new has to be learned.
  • The caveat sentence is the shape the column already uses for production and casting, so a player who has met one has met this.
  • A row can now go italic because of an order written in a different hex's block — the first time that is true of anything but a GIVE between neighbours.

  • Nothing can be wrong about a figure that never moved: whether the goods actually arrive depends on the target really being a quartermaster with a Caravanserai in range, and the app checks none of that today.
  • This is the half of the bug the bead calls the worse half. A receiving unit can be told it cannot afford or cannot carry out orders that the incoming goods would cover.
  • Goods vanish from the map: they leave one row and appear on none. A player reading both hexes sees stone destroyed.
