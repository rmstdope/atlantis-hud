Battles · a view for reading the turn's fighting

Bead ah-1is.2. The parser (ah-1is.1) now puts the turn's battles on ParsedReport.battles — headline, both rosters, the rounds with their spells and loss lines, total casualties, damaged units and spoils. Nothing shows them yet.

What the real data looks like, from neworigins-3.0.0-g7-f95-t71.rep: two battles, 158 units on one attacker roster against a single defender, one round each, and 75 KB of the 199 KB report — most of it the round and battle statistics blocks, which the bead already settles as folded away by default. So the question is not what to show. It is where a thing this large lives, given every faction-scoped surface today is a header chip with a popover about 28rem wide.

Chosen: B, one dialog with the list beside the battle. A 158-unit roster has nowhere to go in a popover, and a two-step popover→dialog (A) makes the player pay a click to find out which battle they wanted. The outcome on a list line is stated neutrally — attacker −0 · defender −15 — rather than as “we” and “they”: in the first battle below the player's units fight inside a 158-unit allied army whose attacker belongs to another faction, so a “we” would be a guess, and a roster line that prints no faction cannot be attributed at all. Whose units were there is answered where it can be answered — in the rosters, which mark the player's own units and count them.

AChip → popover list → full-window dialog

The chip behaves exactly like Turn messages and Problems: it opens a narrow popover anchored under the header. The popover holds only the one-line summaries. Clicking one closes it and opens the battle in a large centred dialog over the workspace. Two surfaces, each doing one job.

A2 · the battle in full, over a darkened workspace
  • The chip and its popover are exactly the idiom the header already has, three times over.
  • The dialog gets the whole window, which is what a 158-unit roster needs.
  • Two clicks and two surfaces to read one battle, and the popover is thrown away the moment you use it.
  • Moving between battles means reopening the popover, or duplicating the list as ← → in the dialog.

Build: a chip, a popover (a near-copy of TurnMessagesPanel), and a dialog. Two dismissal behaviours to get right instead of one.

BChip → one dialog, list beside the battlechosen

The chip opens one surface: a dialog with the turn's battles down a narrow left rail and the selected battle filling the rest. The list is never thrown away, so moving from one battle to the next is a single click, and the summaries stay on screen as context for what you are reading. Opening the chip selects the first battle, so the dialog is never empty.

B · one surface — the list stays put, the battle fills the room it needs
  • One click from the chip to a battle, and one click between battles.
  • The list is context, not a menu: what happened this turn stays readable beside the detail.
  • Scales to a turn with a dozen battles without changing shape.
  • One surface, one dismissal, one place for keyboard handling (↑↓ the list, Esc closes).
  • The only modal in the workspace that is not a question — the map is covered while it is open.
  • Below about 900px the two columns have to stack, so it needs a narrow-window rule.

Build: one dialog component holding a list and a detail view, the two testable apart. No new popover.

CA sixth collapsible panel, in the inspector

Battles join the five collapsible panels in the inspector column rather than the header. Unlike the other five it is turn-scoped, not hex-scoped, so it shows every battle of the turn — unless a hex is selected, in which case it narrows to that hex's battles. Nothing is ever covered; the map and the panels stay side by side.

C · and the empty state, which is most hexes
  • Nothing is ever covered, and the map stays beside what you are reading.
  • Reuses CollapsiblePanel outright — the cheapest of the three to build.
  • About 22rem wide: the rosters cannot sit side by side, and a 158-unit roster reads as an endless column.
  • Turn-scoped in a column where every other panel is hex-scoped — the odd one out, and the empty state is what most hexes show.
  • Competes for vertical space with the five panels a player actually works in.

Build: one panel, no new surface at all — but the bead's "shows it in full" is the part that suffers.
