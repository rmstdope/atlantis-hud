ah-1j5 — finding trade routes

A hex sells a good; another hex will pay more for it. The report carries both halves with prices, so the arithmetic is free — what has to be decided is how much of it is worth doing, and where the answer appears.

Chosen, across three rounds. The answer is a hex pair carrying every good tradeable between those two hexes, ranked by what the trade is worth (quantity × margin), computed over the known map with each half dated, with distances as three numbers — 14/7/4 turns on foot/riding/flying, an em dash where a mode cannot make it, no known way where none can. Both directions fold into one circuit where each way pays. It lives behind a counted Trade chip in the header opening a list, as the Problems chip already does; a row click flies the map to the buying hex and selects it. Carrying capacity is not modelled — the quantity is shown and the player judges. Rejected: margin-only ranking, this-turn-only data, a staleness cutoff, a rail panel, a map layer (a later bead), one row per direction, and capacity in the first version. The full interview is in bead ah-1j5's children.
First, a fact that changes the shape of this bead. I ran the join over every committed fixture report. In a single turn's report there is almost never a route at all — the turn 71 report the smoke tests use has seven goods for sale and thirteen wanted, and no overlap between them. Across the whole fixture corpus, one report yields four routes at best and most yield none. A faction only sees the markets of hexes it has units in, and that is a handful per turn.

Across a game's remembered hexes it is a different picture: game 3, faction 42, over its five stored turns, yields seven routes — and the two best pair a price seen in turn 42 with one seen in turn 82.

Round two — settled, and what is left

Settled: margin, quantity and distance; the answer is a hex pair carrying every good tradeable between those two hexes, not one row per good; and the known map counts, with each half dated.

Q4 — "9 turns away" — for whom?

The route planner costs a journey for a specific unit: its movement mode, its load, what it may cross. A trade route has no unit yet, so the distance column has to say what it is assuming.

A — hexes apart, no journey at all

49,3 → 0,48$10,209
chocolate · 41 × +$249 · 63 hexes apart

Straight-line distance across the lattice. Free, needs no ruleset, no unit and no map knowledge — and says nothing about the ocean in between.
