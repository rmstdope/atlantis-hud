ah-sup — Emphasising the selected hex

Every figure is the same busy neighbourhood: mixed terrain, a brass route line, a red high-risk tint, own (green) and foreign (blue) unit shields, a settlement — and the dashed keyboard-focus ring on the hex next to the selection, because that is the pairing the treatment must stay distinct from. The selected hex is always the one in the middle. The small figure beside each variant is the selection on unexplored ground, which the current code deliberately supports. The strip at the bottom is the light theme.

Baseline — what ships today

Brass 2.5px ring. Brass is also the route line, the marquee and the battle chip — the ring drowns in its own colour family.

Option A — white-hot double ring (CHOSEN)

On unexplored ground.

Near-white 3px ring over a 7px dark casing, screen-constant. The map's only achromatic double outline, so it collides with no faction, risk or route colour, and the light-on-dark pairing survives a colour-blind reading. Cost: quieter than red; it says "you are here" rather than shouting it.

Option B — red ring (your suggestion)

On unexplored ground.

Crimson 3.5px ring over a dark casing. Unmissable — but red already means monster units, high route risk and battle in every theme (#f07070 family), so a selected empty hex reads as flagged dangerous, and next to a real risk tint (top row) the two red meanings sit side by side. The mock uses a deeper crimson to soften that; the collision remains.

Option C — selection wash + bright ring

On unexplored ground.

The existing --color-select blue as a 20% fill wash, plus a brass-bright 3px ring. The whole hex pops, not just its rim. Cost: the wash tints the terrain colour underneath, stacks confusingly with the blue low-risk route tint, and blue is already foreign units and ships.

Option D — corner reticle

On unexplored ground.

Thick brass-bright ticks at all six corners, screen-constant — a targeting reticle. Distinct by shape rather than colour, so nothing collides. Cost: a new visual language on a map that has none, and less total ink than a full ring, so at far zoom on busy terrain it can still hide.

Follow-up: keyboard focus landing on the selected hex (Option A)

Arrow the keyboard cursor onto the already-selected hex and both marks want to draw. Left: both drawn, dashed focus ring on top of the white ring (today's layering). Right: the focus ring suppressed while it coincides with the selection.

Both drawn. (CHOSEN)

Focus ring hidden while on the selection.

Follow-up: the pulse (Option A, animated — click a figure to replay)

Both fire once when the selection changes, then rest as the static double ring. Left: lock-on — the ring starts ~1.6× the hex and contracts to fit, ~300ms. Right: blink — the ring flashes twice in place, ~360ms. With reduced motion set, neither plays.

Lock-on: contracts onto the hex. (CHOSEN — fires on click and keyboard selection, not on the restore at app load; suppressed under prefers-reduced-motion.)

Blink: two quick flashes in place.

Light theme

Same treatments over the light palette. A wants its ring dark and casing pale in light mode (shown — CHOSEN over keeping the white ring in both themes); B–D keep their colour and rely on the casing.
