ah-rgkk.4.2 — round 1: the Men popup

Resting on the Men cell today opens one line — men  14 — paired against the report’s figure, plus the sentence explaining a tilde. It says the total moved; it never says who or why. This bead fills it, on the shell ah-rgkk.1 built and in the pair notation ah-rgkk.6 settled.

Two questions. Q1 — which figures the popup draws: the total alone, or a line per race as well. Q2 — how the prose underneath reads. Everything else in the family is settled and is not re-opened: the heading, the twelve-line cap, the pair notation with green up and red down on the after only, and the amber warning line.

The scene, used in every panel below. Braves (1487) is a mixed unit. Its report showed 10 humans and 4 orcs — the Men cell read 14. This month it recruits 6 humans in the market, gives 3 humans to Watch (1604), and Scouts (1502) takes 2 orcs from it. It ends the month with 13 humans and 2 orcs, so the cell reads 15.

Note what the total hides. Eleven people moved in and out of this unit and the headline figure changed by one. That is the case Q1 is really about, and it is not rare: a unit that recruits while giving its veterans away is an ordinary month.

Q2 — how does the prose read?

Both panels use M2’s figures, so again only one thing differs. The family has settled this question twice already, and the two answers disagree: the Items popup joins every movement of one item into one sentence led by the item’s name; the Skills popup writes one sentence per arrival of men.

S — one sentence per race, led by its name  —  the Items popup’s rule

Braves (1487) — men

  • The prose block can never be longer than the list above it — one sentence per line, at most.
  • The leading name ties each sentence to the figure directly above it.
  • Identical to the Items popup, so the two columns read as one design.
  • A unit whose humans arrived four different ways gets one long sentence that wraps.
  • The month’s order is lost across races: a gift that happened before a recruit reads after it if the races differ.

  • The month is told in the order it happens — gifts and takes first, then the market’s recruits, which is the order the rules resolve them in.
  • Each sentence is short, and no sentence ever wraps.
  • Reads like the Skills popup on the same row, which is telling the same story from the other side.
  • The prose grows without limit. A unit that gave men to four units and recruited twice gets six sentences under three figures.
  • Nothing ties a sentence to the line it explains, so a two-race unit’s reader has to match names by eye.
