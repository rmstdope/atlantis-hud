ah-dna4 — a unit's skills after this month's arrivals

Decisions recorded from the planning discussion (navigator, 2026-08-29/30). A unit that is given men, or that recruits them, has its skills diluted — rules/buy says so in as many words: "Recruit 5 barbarians into the current unit. (This will dilute the skills that the unit has.)" The merge averages skill points across the new headcount and recomputes the level, so a level can fall to nothing. Five surfaces read a unit's skills and only one of them knew.

The fault, reproduced

A smith with weaponsmith 1 and 8 orcs, given one unskilled orc by its neighbour, ordering PRODUCE sword. One orc is enough: 8×30 points shared over 9 men is 26, and level 1 needs 30.

today produced = 9 swords "cannot produce sword: needs weaponsmith 1, has no weaponsmith" "giving 1 man to this unit lowers its weaponsmith from 1 to 0"

The row credits nine swords the game will not make, while the panel beside it says the unit cannot make any.

Where each phase sits (rules/sequenceofevents)

PILLAGE and CAST settle before the market, so a same-month recruit must not touch them. And the application's own pipeline settles the hex's product pool on both sides of the recruit merge, so every figure must read one phase-stable list. That list is the gift-merged one, and it is what every surface below now reads.

Rejected: moving the two production surfaces only, which would fix the fault above and knowingly leave three surfaces reading a pre-gift list — each one a row where the app tells the player something the game will not do.

Decision 2 — the warning stays stricter than the columns

The recruit merge is computed from the ledger that builds the ITEMS cell, so that cell can never see recruits — the dependency is circular. For a unit that recruits itself below the recipe's level, the two columns therefore show a figure while the warning fires.

Problems panel cannot produce sword: needs weaponsmith 1, has no weaponsmith buying 10 men lowers its weaponsmith from 1 to 0

Chosen deliberately, and pinned by a test so it is not later read as a regression: a warning that catches a real fault beats three surfaces agreeing by making the check blinder. Rejected: moving the warning onto the gift list too. Gifts — the case in the title — are fixed on all three surfaces either way.
