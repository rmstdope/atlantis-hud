Region panel · hiding the Problems section

The Problems section sits at the top of the region panel body, above the region facts (RegionPanel.tsx:71). On a hex carrying several order diagnostics the list grows and pushes population, tax base, wages, products and the rest down out of view, so the numbers you came for are behind a scroll past problems you have already read.

The behaviour is settled by the bead: the control stays visible while the section is hidden and says how many problems are hidden, it does not appear at all on a hex with no problems, and the choice is persisted beside the panel-collapse state. What is open is what the control looks like and what it says — three candidates below, drawn in the workspace palette and in the vocabularies this application already owns.

Chosen: 1, a chip in the panel header. The application already has a word for “this is on, and turning it off hides something” — the map’s layer strip — and reusing it costs the player nothing to learn. It keeps the count on the control rather than in the body, and leaves the section header reading as it does today. 2 is the clearest and the widest, in a header that already carries terrain and coordinates; 3 keeps the panel header clean but spends a line of the body it exists to give back, and puts the control somewhere the bead did not ask for.

1A chip in the panel header chosen

The map’s layer strip already says “this is on, this is off” with a lit chip and a tick. The same chip, in the panel header, for the same kind of statement.

hidden — the facts reach the top of the body

  • The application’s existing word for an on/off that hides something: lit means showing.
  • The count is on the control itself, so it is never lost with the section.
  • Chips have so far meant map layers; this is the first one on a panel.
  • A tick in a 26-pixel header is a small target.

  • Same shape as Plan move / Clear — nothing new to learn.
  • The largest target of the three.

  • The label changes under the pointer, which a chip’s does not.
