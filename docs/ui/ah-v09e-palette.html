Reading for an hour — dark palette proposals (bead ah-v09e)

A player reported that after about fifteen minutes their eyes hurt, and suggested the font colour is “not contrast enough”. I measured every colour in the palette before proposing anything, and the measurement disagrees with the diagnosis — which changes what this bead should do.

Correction, found after the first three questions were asked. Everything in §1–§4 measures text against an opaque panel. The floating panes are not opaque. --pane-transparency defaults to 90 (theme.css:304), and .bg-pane (theme.css:344-350) paints the panel colour at 10% opacity over the live map. So for every floating pane, the real background of the text is the map. §6 measures that, and it is a much bigger effect than anything in §3.

So the bead's acceptance criteria aim at a problem that does not exist. They ask for body, header and dimmed text to each meet a stated ratio, with AA (4.5:1) as “the floor to argue from”. Every one of them already clears it, and body text clears AAA twice over. An implementer following those criteria would find nothing to fix — or, worse, would raise contrast further.
And the reporter's own reference argues the other way. Discord's dark theme, which they named as “very good”, runs body text at 9.36:1 on a much lighter background (#313338 against our #10151c). It is not more contrast than us. It is less — about two-thirds of ours — on a ground that is nowhere near black.

§2  What I think is actually happening three causes, none of which is “too little contrast”

CauseEvidenceWhat would fix it
Halation
near-white on near-black		 Body text runs at 13–14:1 on a ground of #10151c, which is very close to black. At that separation light text bleeds visibly into the dark around it, and the eye keeps hunting for a focus that is not there. This is the best-known cause of dark-theme eye strain, and it gets worse the more contrast you add. Discord avoids it by lifting the ground. Lift the background, soften the text, or both — §3.
Small type The panes' whole type scale is --text-pane-sm: 11px, --text-pane: 12px, --text-pane-lg: 13px (theme.css:119-121). Twelve-point-something pixels is fine for a glance and punishing for an hour. There is an Interface size setting that scales all of it — so the reporter may simply never have found it. Raise the default, make the setting findable, or both — §4.
Invisible structure Borders sit at 1.27:1 and inner dividers at 1.12:1. They are very nearly not there. When the boxes do not separate themselves, the reader's eye does the grouping instead — on every glance, all session. This is the one genuine contrast failure in the palette, and it is not a text one. Strengthen the two edge tokens — included in both §3 options.

§5  What comes with any of it

  • The light theme moves in step. theme.css:149+ defines a full light palette, and theme.test.ts fails if any --color-* token lacks a light counterpart. Every token this bead touches needs its light partner considered, not merely present.
  • The map is not in scope. Terrain, fog, unit and settlement colours are their own tokens with their own rules, and the map themes have their own stylesheets. Nothing here touches them — but option A changes what the map is seen next to, which is a thing to look at rather than a thing to change.
  • The reporter should be answered either way. Whatever is chosen, what they described is real; the diagnosis was simply not the one they offered.

§6  The real cause: pane text sits on the map same tokens, measured against what the reader actually sees

Every panel in the workspace uses .bg-pane. At the default setting it is 10% panel over whatever the map is drawing underneath. Contrast therefore depends on the terrain the pane happens to be floating over — and it changes as you pan, which is the single worst thing you can ask a reader's eye to cope with for an hour.

Nothing in this application is “large text”. The biggest size is 13px and the heaviest weight is 500, so every pair above is held to the 4.5:1 small-text bar. Body text fails it over three common terrains; secondary text fails everywhere but forest; dimmed text is at 1.7:1, which is very close to not being there at all. This is the eye strain.
Why no test caught it. theme.test.ts:100-117 does do real WCAG maths, and asserts all nine ink×surface pairs clear 4.5:1 — against the opaque panel. It models a background the reader never sees for a floating pane.
