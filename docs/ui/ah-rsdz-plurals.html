ah-rsdz — “buys 200 horse”

The ruleset stores item names in the singular, and several messages put a count immediately in front of one:

your units here buy 120 horse between them and this market has 100
this unit buys 200 horse and this market has 100
short 5 horse: this unit can have 0 and its orders spend 5

These are the sentences a player reads when the app is telling them something is wrong, so reading like a bad translation costs more here than elsewhere.

Measured what a fix has to cope with

  • A naive “add an s” is definitively wrong. Of 114 items seen in the corpus with a count above one, 84 pluralise irregularly — amulet of protection → amulets of protection, book of exorcism → books of exorcism. “+s” gives amulet of protections.
  • 30 are invariant. grain, iron, fish, livestock, chain armor — “10 grain” is already correct and must not become “10 grains”.
  • No source is complete. The report's own item lines carry the engine's plurals and cover 114 of 171 items here; the data page carries them only incidentally, in prose, for about 91. Neither covers everything.
  • There is already a precedent. not-traded-here reads its names from the report's market lines and so already prints plurals: “its market has perfume, gems and hill dwarves”.

Question 1 where does the plural come from?

Option
A — the report Read the plural from the report's own item lines, which is where not-traded-here already gets its names. item_name already falls back to them; today the ruleset's singular shadows it. The engine's own words, so never wrong. No scraping. Covers only items somebody in the report holds — and a message about goods a unit is buying may name something nobody holds yet.
B — scrape plurals Add plural to the ruleset from the data page, as ah-19l2.1 and ah-1ad6.1 did for recipes and weapons. One catalogue, available whether or not anyone holds the item. The page states plurals only incidentally, inside prose — noisy to extract and about 91 of 171.
C — both Report first, scraped plural second, singular last. Best coverage. Two sources to keep in step, and a bead that is really two.
D — never abut them Reword every message so a count never sits against a name: “this unit buys 200, and this market has 100 horse” — or the count and the goods separated some other way. Needs no data at all and is right for all 171 items. Changes sentences that are otherwise fine, and the awkwardness moves rather than leaves.

Question 2 when no plural is known

Whatever the source, some item will have none — a new item, or one nobody holds.

OptionWhat prints
Fall back to the singular buys 200 horse — today's behaviour, for the residue only. Wrong, but wrong in one place instead of everywhere, and never invented.
Fall back to “+s” buys 200 horses — right far more often than not, and confidently wrong on amulet of protections.
Name it once, without a count buys more horse than this market has — drops the numbers for the residue, which are the useful part of the sentence.

Question 3 how wide

Option
Every message that pairs a count with a name One helper, and every call site moved to it — today region-pool-oversubscribed and not-enough-items, and whatever else the grep finds. The acceptance criteria ask for exactly this: the rule in one place, so the next message inherits it.
Only the two named Smaller diff. Leaves the next message to make the same mistake, which is the thing the bead says is cheap to get wrong again.
