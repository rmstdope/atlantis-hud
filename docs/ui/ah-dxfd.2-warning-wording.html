ah-dxfd.2 — the warning now counts men that have not moved yet

The pillage warning's sentence does not change shape in this bead — but the meaning of its number does. Today "this region has 19" counts the men and weapons the report printed. After this bead it counts what the region will hold once this month's gifts and takes have run — a forecast, on a line that reads today like a statement of fact.

Everywhere else in the app a forecast is marked: the units table prints a predicted figure in italic brass with a was: hover. A warning message has no such affordance, so if it should say it, it has to say it in words.

The sentence today, verbatim

Two shapes, depending on whether the app can say why a particular unit's men do not count.

cannot pillage here: needs 24 combat ready men, this region has 19 — its 35 swords need weaponsmith 1, and it has no weaponsmith
cannot pillage here: needs 24 combat ready men, this region has 19

The case that makes it a forecast

unit 1234 ; Home guard — 19 HUMN, COMB 1 (30), 35 SWOR FORM 1 PILLAGE END GIVE NEW 1 10 HUMN GIVE NEW 1 10 SWOR

The region holds 19 combat ready men, both before and after those gifts — but only because the gifts move men and weapons together. Change GIVE NEW 1 10 SWOR to GIVE NEW 1 10 XBOW, which the Raiders cannot wield, and the figure the warning prints drops to 9 on the strength of orders in the document, not of anything the report says.

W1 — leave the sentence exactly as it is
cannot pillage here: needs 24 combat ready men, this region has 9 — its 10 crossbows need crossbow 1, and it has no crossbow
  • No new string in this bead at all, which is what the parent bead settled for every other surface it touches: the numbers change, the words do not.
  • Every warning in this app already reads the orders as well as the report — pillage-without-men only fires because a PILLAGE was ordered. The number was always about the month you are writing.
  • "this region has 9" reads as a fact about the region, and a player who has not yet noticed that their own GIVE caused it may go looking for the missing men on the report.
W2 — say that the count is after this month's transfers
cannot pillage here: needs 24 combat ready men, this region will have 9 once this month's gifts have run — its 10 crossbows need crossbow 1, and it has no crossbow
  • Names the cause on the line where the player is standing. The one thing they can act on is the GIVE, and this points at it.
  • A long sentence, and it grows the common case for the sake of the uncommon one: most pillage warnings involve no gift at all, and would now carry a clause about gifts that did not happen.
  • A new string to agree, translate and keep in step with the tooltip vocabulary.
W3 — say it only when this month's transfers actually changed the figure
cannot pillage here: needs 24 combat ready men, this region has 9 after this month's gifts (19 before) — its 10 crossbows need crossbow 1, and it has no crossbow

A hex where nothing moved keeps the sentence exactly as it reads today:

cannot pillage here: needs 24 combat ready men, this region has 19
  • The common case is untouched, and the uncommon one carries both numbers, so the player can see the size of what their own orders did.
  • Two shapes of one sentence, chosen by a condition — the kind of thing that is cheap to build and expensive to keep true, since the "before" figure has to be computed and carried purely to be printed.
  • The parenthesis competes with the — clause after it; on a narrow window the line wraps twice.
