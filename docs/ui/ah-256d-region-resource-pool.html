ah-256d — A hex's yield, shared between the units producing there

A region's Products line states how much of each resource the hex yields this month, and every unit producing there shares it. rules/tableiteminfo: an item that lists no raw material "may be produced directly from the land … Shown on the description of a region is a list of the items that can be produced, and the amount of each that can be produced per month. … If the units in a region attempt to produce more of a commodity than can be produced that month, then the amount available is distributed among the producers". The app modelled no pool at all, so after ah-vtwn a lone quarrier was shown making 20 stone in a hex holding 16, and two miners were shown making 72 iron between them in a hex holding 36.

Decisions (recorded from the planning discussion, navigator, 2026-08-29, two rounds): the cap is quiet — no Problems entry, no new warning, no Region-panel change; the hover sentence reads "This region has iron for 20, not the 40 its skill and tools could make."; a hex that yields none of the item makes 0 and says nothing, exactly as a unit below the recipe's minimum skill already does; nothing is said about other factions. Rejected, and why, below each choice.

Every row is reproduced exactly by split_pool — the same proportional division the app already applies to a contended market line. Iron: 32 and 40 against 36 gives 16 and 20, which is what the report says. A lone producer is capped too (stone, mithril, admantium), and a pool that covers everybody divides nothing (livestock). The pick bonus is inside the asks and is load-bearing: without it Miners would ask 24, and 24 against 40 gives 13, not the 16 the report states.

Decision 1 — the cap is quiet (chosen)

Chosen — A Problems panel, mountain (36,4): (nothing about production)
Rejected — B * Miners (1795) — your units here produce 72 iron between them and this region has 36 * MinersA (5105) — (the same sentence) * Stoneworkers (2693) — this unit produces 20 stone and this region has 16 * MinersM (3826) — …10 mithril… has 9 * MinersA (7671) — …10 admantium… has 6
Rejected — C The same entries as B, under a new toggle of their own, so they could be switched off without also losing the tax, wage and market warnings.

Why A. A busy mining hex is oversubscribed on purpose: you post two miners because you want both of them mining, and the game splits the seam without refusing anything. Five entries from one perfectly ordinary hex, every turn, is a line the player learns to ignore — and the Problems panel's value is that it is short. B was rejected for that, and because its toggle is all-or-nothing with the tax, wage and market warnings, which are mistakes. C was rejected as a new warning code, a new Settings row and a new piece of vocabulary bought for a fact the ITEMS figure and its hover already carry. What A costs: a unit whose man-months are being wasted is discoverable only by hovering that one unit; nothing says "this hex is over-mined by 36 man-months".

Decision 2 — the cap sentence (chosen: W1)

W1 — chosen

This unit will produce 20 iron. This region has iron for 20, not the 40 its skill and tools could make.
This unit will produce 16 stone. This region has stone for 16, not the 20 its skill and tools could make.

W2 — rejected

This unit will produce 20 iron. This unit takes 20 of the region's 36 iron, not the 40 its skill and tools could make.

W3 — rejected

This unit will produce 20 iron. Your units here produce 72 iron between them and this region has 36, so this unit takes 20 of the 40 its skill and tools could make.

Why W1. One sentence for both the crowded hex and the small one, and it is the shape the three existing reasons already use — "This unit has materials for 5 swords, not the 46 its skill and tools could make." W2 was rejected because "16 of the region's 16 stone" is what a lone producer exhausting a hex reads as, and it is odd; W3 because its contended form runs to about 130 characters where the other three reasons run to about 70. What W1 costs: it never says why 20, so a small hex and a crowded one read identically.

Where the noun comes from

The sentence needs a bare noun. The region's own Products line already carries the right word for every one of the nineteen things a hex can yield, and it is not the catalogue's singular — which is what the rejected alternative would have used:

Decision 3 — the hex yields none of it (chosen: silent)

A unit ordered PRODUCE floater hide in a mountain. The Products line does not mention it, so nothing can be produced there and the figure goes to 0.

Z3 — chosen

This unit will produce no floater hides. (no cap sentence) Problems panel: * cannot produce floater hide here: this region produces livestock, iron, stone, mithril and admantium

Z1 — rejected

This unit will produce no floater hides. This region has no floater hide, not the 10 its skill and tools could make.

Z2 — rejected

This unit will produce no floater hides. This region produces none of it, not the 10 its skill and tools could make.

Why Z3. produce-not-here already says it, immediately below, and two copies of one sentence are two things to keep in step — the same reasoning that made ah-vtwn silent about a unit below the recipe's minimum skill, and the same shape in the code: an empty plan, no cap, no sentence. Z1 was rejected as a second source of nouns inside one sentence family; Z2 because "none of it" leans on the line above to say what "it" is.

Decisions 4 to 6 — three things that do not change

The ITEMS cell at zero — unchanged

ITEMS 8 orcs · 20 iron

A run that makes none pushes nothing into the cell today, and still does. The rejected alternative added a dimmed +0 floater hides row to a cell that is already crowded in a big unit.

The Region panel — unchanged

Products 34 livestock 36 iron 16 stone 9 mithril 6 admantium

Rejected: appending "· 72 claimed" per line, or "· oversubscribed". Both were real answers to what decision 1 gave up, and both were declined — on an established hex the answer is nearly always "oversubscribed", so the column would say the same thing every turn.

Other factions — not mentioned

This unit will produce 20 iron. This region has iron for 20, not the 40 its skill and tools could make.

Only our own units are visible, so every figure here is an upper bound. Nothing says so: the whole preview is a forecast and no other figure in it carries a caveat. Rejected: a second hover line, always or only when a foreign unit stands in the hex.
