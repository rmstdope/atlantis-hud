A finding sighted three times — what the sweep should print

Every retrospective carries a **Seen before.** line naming earlier beads with the same finding, and nothing reads them. The apt/Playwright stall reached nine sightings before the fix landed; sightings three and four were two days before the day it cost about eight hours.

The citations form a graph — ah-csni cites ah-3c80, which cites four others — so one root cause is one connected component, however many beads are in the chain. That is what stops a chain of five reading as five separate findings.

The numbers below are real, counted from the 114 committed retrospectives.

1 · What the sweep prints

AOne line each, newest sighting first

A compact list. Every finding is one line: how many sightings, and the bead that saw it last.
Repeated findings (3+ sightings)

  4  smoke fixtures break on a new check last: ah-csni
  3  cerebro worktree trap              last: ah-4ao

  bd recall retro-watermark  ·  3 new retrospectives since
  • Fits in a greeting without pushing everything else off screen
  • The count is the first thing on the line, which is the thing that matters
  • Does not say which beads sighted it, so acting means going and looking
  • "apt stalls installing Playwright" has to come from somewhere — see section 2

BThe beads, named

Each finding lists the beads in its chain, so the evidence is in front of you.

2 · Where the finding's name comes from

The retrospectives cite bead ids, not causes. Nothing in the data says "apt stalls installing Playwright" — that phrase would have to come from somewhere.

iThe oldest bead's own headline

Use the ## heading of the earliest retrospective in the chain — it is already written as "the symptom, not the cause".
  9  ah-k6i.5: the apt step hung for twenty minutes
  10 ah-9r0: the build refused to start on a full disk
  • Free, and written by someone who saw the problem
  • Traceable — the name is a quotation, with its source
  • The earliest sighting is often the least well understood one

iiNo name at all — just the ids

Print the chain and let the reader open one. This is option B above.
  • Cannot be wrong, because it claims nothing
  • No parsing beyond the citation itself
  • Every report costs a file-open before it means anything

3 · Silencing one that has been dealt with

The container fix landed; the apt chain should stop being reported. A cause is a set of bead ids, and the set grows, so what exactly gets remembered?

iName any bead in the chain

pnpm sightings --dismiss ah-k6i.5 silences the whole chain that bead belongs to, now and as it grows.
$ pnpm sightings --dismiss ah-k6i.5
dismissed the finding containing ah-k6i.5 (9 sightings)
it will stay hidden even as more beads join it
  • Survives the chain growing — a tenth sighting stays silent
  • Forgiving: any member works, so you need not know the "right" one
  • A genuinely new cause that happens to cite a dismissed bead goes quiet too

iiDismiss, but speak up if it keeps happening

Same, except a dismissed finding returns if it gains, say, three further sightings — the fix did not hold.
$ pnpm sightings
Repeated findings (3+ sightings)
  12 ah-k6i.5: the apt step hung
      dismissed at 9 — three more since
  • A fix that did not work cannot hide behind the dismissal
  • More state to keep: the count at dismissal, not just the fact of it
  • Re-nags about something you decided to tolerate
