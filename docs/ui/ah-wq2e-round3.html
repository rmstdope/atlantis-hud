Walls between hexes, round 3

Settled so far: the rampart look, in each map style's own strongest ink. Walls are always shown, with no switch. A move into a wall is drawn up to the wall and ends in a red bar. Its warning reads "There is no exit North from…". Three things are still open.

1 · The rampart when zoomed out

With no switch, a busy underground level cannot be calmed down. Up close the cross-ticks are what make it a rampart. Zoomed right out they become a fringe of short marks on every closed side.

Z1: cross-ticks at every zoom

Z2: ticks drop away when zoomed out, leaving a plain bar in the same ink

Z2 changes the look at the zoom level where province names already drop away, so walls and names simplify at the same point.

2 · The warning when the hex has no name, or the wall is old

Your chosen wording names the settlement and says "the report". Most caverns have no settlement, and a wall can come from a sighting many turns old. Two ways to handle both:

N1: name if it has one, otherwise the terrain; always "a report"

There is no exit North from Ciestucshire (9,3,2): a report shows a wall on that side.
Ciestucshire Chieftain (3521) · MOVE N NW
There is no exit Southwest from cavern (7,3,2): a report shows a wall on that side.
Stonebeard (812) · MOVE SW

N2: name if it has one, otherwise the terrain; say which turn

There is no exit North from Ciestucshire (9,3,2): the report shows a wall on that side.
Ciestucshire Chieftain (3521) · MOVE N NW
There is no exit Southwest from cavern (7,3,2): the turn 79 report shows a wall on that side.
Stonebeard (812) · MOVE SW

N1 is the same sentence every time. N2 says "the report" for this turn's report and names the turn for anything older, which is longer but tells a player how old the wall is.
