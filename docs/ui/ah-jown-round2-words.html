Round 2 — the words, men, and whether SILVER says it too

Round 1 settled the shape: the cell shows the settled figure plainly (A), the hover names the cap that bit (H2), a unit that can afford none says so (Z2), and a line split between your own units gets its own sentence (M2).

What I got wrong in round 1. I invented a sentence shape. This application already has one: capSentence produces “This unit has materials for 5 swords, not the 8 its men could make.” for PRODUCE and “This unit has silver for 2 amulets of protection, not the 3 its level could make.” for CAST — and the ITEMS hover already prints those. So a BUY ALL sentence in my round-1 wording would sit directly underneath one of these, in a different voice.

R1 — which voice the three sentences speak in

The same unit throughout: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR ordering buy all GRAI. Each card shows all three cases stacked, which is how you should judge them — and the last block in each is a unit that also has a PRODUCE, so you can see the new sentence next to the shipped one.

  • Two voices in one hover. The last block reads as two different people wrote it.
  • Never says what it would have bought, so the player cannot see how far short they fell.

W2The house voice — capSentence’s shape

“This unit has X for N, not the M …”, the shape already on screen for PRODUCE and CAST.

  • One voice. The last block reads as one hover rather than two.
  • Always names both numbers, so the shortfall is visible without arithmetic.
  • The first two are literally capSentence with a different tail, so the existing function extends rather than a second one being written.
  • Longer, and the number the player came for is no longer first.

R2 — BUY ALL of men

rules/buy: “or to buy new people in any region where people are available for recruiting … specify the race of the people as the [item]”. Recruits appear on the For Sale line like anything else, so buy all NOMA is a BUY ALL that changes the MEN column as well as ITEMS. ah-dxfd.1 shipped the rule that a headcount follows men bought — but it cannot follow an order nothing counts, so today this is silent too, and it stops being silent for free when this bead lands.

R3 — does the SILVER hover say it too?

The SILVER cell already shows 98 and explains nothing about where 344 silver went. productionCapSentence is exported “because the ITEMS hover says it too” — one sentence, two hovers. The question is whether the BUY ALL sentence joins that arrangement or stays in ITEMS alone.

S1Both hovers say it

ITEMS hoverwas: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR
This unit has silver for 19 grain, not the 30 this market offers.
SILVER hoverholds 356 · spends 342 · at month end 14
This unit has silver for 19 grain, not the 30 this market offers.
  • The player gets the answer wherever they hovered, which is the arrangement PRODUCE already has.
  • One exported function, so the two cannot drift apart.
  • The sentence is about goods, and the SILVER hover is about money — it is a longer hover for a cell whose number was never in doubt.

S2ITEMS only

ITEMS hoverwas: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR
This unit has silver for 19 grain, not the 30 this market offers.
SILVER hoverholds 356 · spends 342 · at month end 14
(unchanged)
  • Smaller change, and the SILVER hover stays about money.
  • Breaks the pattern PRODUCE set, so a reader wonders why one cap sentence travels and the other does not.
  • A player who hovers SILVER asking “where did 342 go?” is told nothing.
Recommended: W2, and S1. W2 because the ITEMS hover already prints capSentence’s wording and two voices in one tooltip is worse than a longer sentence; S1 because the sentence exists to explain a number, and the number it explains is in both cells. R2 needs no decision unless the wording there looks wrong to you.
