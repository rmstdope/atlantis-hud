Round 2 — the consequences of exporting what we last knew

You chose P3, and you chose to keep and export a unit that is not in this turn's report. The second choice is the interesting one, and it reaches further than it looks.

To export a unit the current report does not mention, the Army must remember that unit — its men, items, skills, flags and faction — as of the last turn it was seen, refreshed whenever it turns up again. An Army stops being a list of unit numbers and becomes a small dossier of its own.

That is the right call for the reason you gave: a foreign unit is out of sight far more often than it is destroyed, and the whole point is not to reassemble a force by hand every month. But it means a row in this table can now be showing you data from three turns ago, and the interface has to be honest about which rows those are.

My earlier drawing showed a stale member with — for Men and Hex. That is now wrong — if we export the numbers we must show the numbers. Section R1 is three ways of doing it.

R5 — whose Armies are they?

A game can hold several factions, and you switch between them. Hex notes — the closest existing thing — are scoped to the game.

Scoped to the game

Every Army is visible whichever faction's report is loaded. Matches hex notes. An Army may hold units of any faction — which is already true — so nothing about it is really “yours”. Switching faction leaves the rail unchanged.

Cost: playing two factions in one game, you see both sets of Armies mixed together with nothing separating them.

Scoped to the faction

Each faction has its own Armies; switching faction changes the rail. Tidier when you play two factions in one game.

Cost: an Army made of another faction's units belongs to the viewer who made it, which is a slightly odd thing to explain — and a force you scouted as one faction is invisible to the other.

Everything else — the exact wording of every label, button and warning, what Escape does, where focus lands, and the narrow-window fallback — is round three, once these are settled.
