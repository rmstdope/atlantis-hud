1 · What the folded chip is called

It now stands for three things that used to have a chip each: what the engine reported went wrong, what order validation found across the map, and what the parser could not read. Turn events — 333 of them in this fixture — are not warnings and are not counted; they keep a section in the panel. All four read at 13 px, in place:

W1 Says what to do with it. Does not claim they are all your fault — an unreadable line is ours.
W2 Keeps today's word — but today it means order problems only, and this chip is wider than that.
W3 Accurate and neutral. Says nothing about whether you can act on them.
W4 Widest margin (67 px saved), but a bare number beside a triangle names nothing.

And when nothing is wrong

The chip does not vanish — it drops its amber and says what the turn contained, so it is not a control that appears and disappears as counts move.

N1 — the events count takes the chip over N2 — a fixed name, so the chip never changes width

A turn with no events and nothing wrong: N1 would have to hide the chip entirely (nothing to count), N2 would still be there and open a panel saying the turn was quiet.
