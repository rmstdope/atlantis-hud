Map levels · the nexus on a level of its own

Bead ah-4b4. A region header’s third coordinate is a name, not a number: the engine writes nexus (0,0,nexus) in The Void for the nexus and cavern (7,53,underworld) in Deeps underground — and only those two names ever appear in a NewOrigins report (the engine also knows underdeep and abyss, which no shipped ruleset turns on). The parser read that field as a number, fell back to the surface when it was not one, and so filed both the nexus and every underworld hex on the surface, sharing an identity with whatever is genuinely there.

Settled by the bead: the nexus (level 0), the surface (1) and the underworld (2) become distinct levels with distinct region ids, and units, orders and the turn comparison follow. What is open is what the player sees of that — four questions below, drawn from the strip above the map (LayerChips.tsx) and the region panel as they are today. Everything else in the strip is unchanged; only the level control and its words are in question.

Chosen (2026-08-16): Q1 A — the report’s own words, lowercase, shallowest first, supplied by the core. Q2 B — the nexus is remembered like any other hex and the control offers it for the life of the game; a game imported before this bead has its mis-filed nexus row repaired when the known map is resolved, not by a store migration. Q3 A — “…and no neighbour has named it, in the underworld.” / “…, in the nexus.” Q4 A — hex labels stay terrain (x,y) in Province; the report’s three-part form is a bead of its own if ever wanted. And on loading turn 1 the map stays on the nexus level until the player picks the surface — a new turn never moves the player, and this is not made an exception.

Q1What the level control calls a level

Today the control reads surface for level 1 and level 2 for anything else, and it is a plain <select> shown only when the map has more than one level; with one level it is a static word. That rule stays. The question is the words, now that every level has a name of its own. Three states are drawn for each variant: a game with the nexus and the surface both known, one with the surface and the underworld, and turn 0 — where the whole report is one hex, the nexus, and the control is the static word.

Q2The nexus after the faction has left it

The map remembers every hex a report has ever described, so once the nexus is on its own level it would stay on the map for the life of the game — a level with one stale hex, which the rules say a unit can never return to and which has no exits. Two ways to treat it.

AShown only while the report describes it

Turn 0 opens on the nexus, the one hex the report has, with the starting unit in it. From turn 1 the nexus is not remembered: the level control has no nexus entry, and a game saved on the nexus level opens on the surface — the same rule that already moves a saved level the map no longer draws.

StalenessMovementBadges ▾ nexus
turn 0 — the nexus, one hex, your unit in it
StalenessMovementBadges ▾ surface
turn 1 onward — the nexus is gone; one level, no control
  • No permanent entry in the control for a level nothing can ever be on again.
  • Loading turn 1 lands the player on the surface without a click.
  • Fixes a game imported before this bead by itself: the mis-filed nexus rows in its memory are the same rows this rule drops, so nothing has to be re-imported or migrated.
  • The nexus is not on the map as history — the turn comparison still shows the unit having left it, but you cannot go and look at it.

BRemembered like any other hex chosen

The nexus stays as a stale hex on level 0 for the whole game, and the level control offers it alongside the surface.

A hex is named terrain (x,y) in Province everywhere — the region panel title, the units dock, the turn comparison — and today that omits the level even underground. Two choices; the second is a wider change than this bug.
