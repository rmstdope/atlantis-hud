Last turn's battles on the map — four decisions (bead ah-sdw5)

Your words: “last turns battles should be displayed as a badge on the map. It shall be possible to turn this on and off as other badges. Each theme should have the appropriate icon for this.”

Please open this page before answering — every mark below is drawn from the real code, at real proportions, and none of it survives being described.

§0  The good news: the five icons already exist nothing to design

The map view model has carried a battle: boolean field since the themes were written, marked reserved — and all five themes already draw it, each in its own idiom, each with a station and a colour already chosen. Nothing here is drawn by me; these are the real paths out of the theme files.

So this bead does not ask you to choose icons. The work is: read the battles the report already parses, light the reserved field, and add one entry to the Badges popover. The four questions below are all about meaning, not looks.
One thing I have decided rather than asked, say if it is wrong: the badge shows the battles of the turn you are looking at — the report currently on screen. Scroll back to an older turn and you see that turn's battles. It never mixes turns.

§1  A battle in a hex you have never surveyed the question triage sent to you

A battle headline names its own hex: Einar (2713) attacks City Guard (6742) in desert (0,48) in Dernvin! — terrain, coordinates and province, all three. But the map is built from region sections, and a hex with no region section is not in the map at all, so there is nothing to hang a badge on.

Measured, not guessed. Every battle in every committed fixture report — 20 battle hexes across 11 reports:

The one is (18,6) in neworigins-3.0.0-g3-f42-t82.rep: mentioned exactly once in every report that faction ever received, in the headline of the battle. So this is roughly one battle in twenty — real, but rare.

A Suppress it

  • Nothing to build. The map only ever shows ground you hold data for, which is what it promises today.
  • The battle is still in the Open battles list, with its hex named — it is not lost, just not on the map.
  • One battle in twenty is on the map for everyone else and silently absent for you.

B Draw the hex, from the headline

  • The headline carries terrain, coordinate and province, so the hex can be drawn honestly as unsurveyed ground — the dashed rim every theme already uses for a hex named by a neighbour.
  • Nothing is invented: it says exactly what the report said.
  • A new way for a hex to enter the map, which no other feature has. Touches the map model, not just the badge.
  • The hex stays on the map after you switch the badge off — or it flickers in and out with the toggle, which is worse.

C Suppress, but say so

  • Same as A, plus a small count somewhere on the map chrome that opens the battles list.
  • A new piece of map furniture for a one-in-twenty case, and a second thing to design and place.
  • Widens the bead well past “a badge”.

§2  One mark, or does it say whether you were in it?

The report tells you who fought. The application already computes this: allegianceOf() in workspace/battles.ts answers own / other / unknown for every combatant. What it does not know is who won — the parsed battle has casualties but no outcome, so a won/lost distinction would be a guess, and I am not offering it.

P One mark: “a battle happened here”

  • Exactly what you asked for, and exactly what all five themes already draw.
  • Nothing new to design, nothing new to explain.
  • Scanning the map, you cannot tell the hex where your army died from one where two strangers fought.

Q Two tones: yours, and everyone else's

  • The one distinction that changes what you do this turn: your own losses are the urgent thing on the map.
  • A second tone in every one of the five themes, each needing a light and a dark value — five stylesheets, not one.
  • Emblem & Dots states one fact per hex on its medallion; two battle tones is a second decision inside its priority rule.

§3  Two battles in one hex

M A mark, not a number

  • The field is already a boolean and all five themes already draw it that way. Zero extra work.
  • The hex is a place to look, not a tally — the count is in the battles list.
  • A hex where three fights happened reads the same as one where a scout was ambushed.

N A tally, like ships and buildings

  • Consistent with ships, buildings, shafts and lairs, which all carry counts.
  • battle stops being a boolean, so all five themes change — and each has its own way of printing a number, or none.
  • Emblem & Dots and Miniature World do not print numbers on their marks at all.

The costs here are lopsided, and I got this backwards at first. The store already reconciles a returning player's saved badges against allBadges(true), so a key that did not exist when they last saved defaults to on, by itself. Option 1 is therefore free; option 2 needs an explicit exception written into the defaults in two places, and a returning player still gets it on unless that exception is written carefully. That does not decide it — “nobody's map changes without asking” may still be worth the exception — but you should know which way the grain runs.

§5  The quiet states, for completeness

— A turn with no battles

Battles
  • The popover entry is always there, ticked or not. No hex carries a mark. Nothing says “no battles” — the map does not announce absences today and would not start with this one.

— Badge switched off

  • Every battle mark disappears from every theme at once, like every other badge — and reappears with All. The battles list is untouched: it is a different surface with its own way in.

— A battle hex on another level

  • The map draws one level at a time. A battle in the underworld marks the underworld hex, and is invisible while you are looking at the surface — exactly like every other mark.
