A unit alone in its hex: what its warning says

We agreed that the warning shared by every unit in a hex counts food in: it names the whole fee, and what the units can have includes the food that pays part of it.

That shared warning only appears when another of your units in the hex had silver to lend and the hex still came up short. A unit alone in its hex, or next to units with nothing to lend, gets its own warning instead. Its wording is slightly different: it talks about orders and upkeep together, because the same sentence also covers silver that orders spend, such as buying.

Agreed: Option A — food counted in. The unit's own warning counts food in exactly as the shared warning does, in every world, accepting that when the unit also buys, "can have" includes food that cannot pay for the purchase (section Three). Option B is kept beside it as the rejected alternative.

Option A counts food in the same way the shared warning now does. Option B leaves the unit's own warning as it reads today, with food already taken off, so the two warnings add up differently. Everything on the unit itself (the upkeep line, the month-end figure and the food sentence) is the same in both.

ThreeA leader alone that also buys: two grain, 50 silver, buying for 80 (Trident)

The purchase costs 80 and the upkeep left after food is 30, so it needs 110 in silver. It has 50, so it is 60 short. This is where "orders and upkeep" together matters.

The $110 is 50 silver and 60 of grain, and the grain can't pay for the purchase.
