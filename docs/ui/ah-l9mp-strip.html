Giving the strip back to the map — proposals (bead ah-l9mp)

The staleness, movement and badge controls sit in a strip over the map and take room the map could use. The bead asks for them to move into settings, and for the selected level to move up into the top bar instead — because it is changed often and wants to be seen at a glance.

That empties LayerChips completely, so the strip goes rather than shrinks. Which is the appeal — and it is also why one of the four controls deserves a second look.

§2  The one that may not belong in settings badges is ten toggles you flick while reading the map

Staleness and movement are two switches you set once. Badges is not. It is a menu of about ten marks — ships, shafts, lairs, roads and the rest — and its own chip already lights up when any of them is off, “so a hex missing a mark is never a mystery” (LayerChips.tsx). Hiding a badge to read a crowded hex is a thing done while looking at the map; behind a settings dialog it becomes open-dialog, find-tab, toggle, close, look.

A All three into settings

The bead as written. The strip goes entirely.
  • Simplest rule, and the map gets the whole band.
  • One place for every display preference.
  • Hiding one badge to read a crowded hex becomes four gestures and covers the map while you do it.
  • Loses the chip's “something is hidden” light, unless settings grows one.

B Staleness and movement move; badges stays

The two set-once toggles go; the map-reading menu keeps its place.

  • Keeps the “something is hidden” light on the map.
  • A strip survives, so the map gains a chip's width rather than the whole band.
