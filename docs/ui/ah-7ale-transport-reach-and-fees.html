Shipping goods to a quartermaster — reach, price, and what cannot be known

Today this application forecasts every TRANSPORT order as succeeding and as costing nothing. It never looks at how far the goods must travel, so it takes them off the sender, puts them on a quartermaster that will never receive them, re-works what each of them can carry on the strength of that, and leaves the sender's month-end silver too high by the whole shipping bill. Everything below is the agreed behaviour instead. Every sentence is quoted as it will ship.

unit 900  ; owns a Caravanserai, Quartermaster 5
  TRANSPORT 901 9 FUR

A shipment the game will refuse is an ordinary problem in every sense: counted in the warning strip and in the hex's problem count, marked by the editor's amber bar beside the shipping line, a stop on the F8 walk that selects the order, and worded identically in the editor's list and the hex's. A shipment the application cannot measure is not a problem at all — it may well succeed — so it is never reported as one; it is marked on the figures instead.

The other reach: quartermaster to quartermaster, which grows with skill
unit 900 ; owns a Caravanserai, Quartermaster 1
  TRANSPORT 901 1 IRON
Unit 901 is 4 hexes away and this unit can ship 3 hexes, so 1 IRON stays with this unit.

The limit rises with the sender's own skill, so the same order becomes possible later without the sentence ever naming a skill. Two shipments refused by one unit get a line each, in the order they were written. On a narrow window each line wraps inside its own list.

The forecast keeps the goods. The sender keeps the stone and its weight, so what it can carry this month is right again too; the quartermaster's forecast gains nothing. A shipment that is in reach says nothing at all — no warning, no extra line.

2 · A long shipment costs silver

The shipment's own line names the price
Sends 9 FUR to unit 901 for 45 silver.

The price sits beside the order that caused it, so it is read while reading the shipment. A shipment that costs nothing says nothing — never “for 0 silver”.

One word, at home among taxed, bought and studied, with the aside that explains the number — the same kind of clause a purchase already carries, and the only place a player can see that a better quartermaster ships more cheaply.

Several shipments
Sends 9 FUR to unit 901 for 45 silver.
Sends 20 GRAI to unit 902 for 500 silver.
Sends 4 HERB to unit 903.

The paid ones add into one shipped −545 line, the way two purchases already add into one bought. The free one is silent. Grain is heavy: twenty of it weighs a hundred.

A beginner's bill
shipped−1359 weight at 15 silver

Three times the price for the same nine furs at Quartermaster 1, and the aside is what says why.

Free, and the other game
Sends 9 FUR to unit 901.
; two hexes, Trident: free

Sends 9 FUR to unit 901 for 45 silver.
; two hexes, New Origins: charged

Trident carries a short shipment free; New Origins has no free short range. Each game is priced by its own rules.

No new sentence is invented: this is the shortfall wording already in use, with the tail clause a purchase already uses — …so it buys 2 of the 5 horses ordered. The arithmetic is the whole month's, so a unit short because of a study as well reads one figure for both.

A shipment is all or nothing, so the tail always says none of the 9 furs. Silver already pools across your units in a hex, and shipping draws on it like any other cost: a faction-mate here with money enough means no shortfall and nothing said.

4 · The distance cannot be worked out

The target's position was never reported
Could not count 9 FUR for unit 901 because your report does not show where it is.

Actionable: scout it, or read a turn it appears in.

The world's width was never reported
Could not count 9 FUR for unit 902 because your report does not show how far the world reaches around, so the distance cannot be worked out.

Nothing the player did wrong and nothing scouting fixes — worth its own sentence for exactly that reason.

The goods are neither delivered nor kept
unit 900— items
Items  9 FUR + ?
⚠ “+ ?” in the cell: this month is only partly counted, so this list may be short.

The furs are still listed, because for all the application knows they are still there, and the + ? says the list is not the whole story. Both the mark and that sentence already exist for every month that cannot be fully added up.

This month cannot be added up, so what moved this unit's silver is not listed.
The distance a shipment must travel is not known, so what it costs cannot be said.

An unknown distance is an unknown price, so the month has no total — the same ? and the same pair of notes already shown when a cost cannot be priced.

Nothing certain is ever reported as uncertain. A target you can see, at a distance that can be measured, behaves exactly as it does today: the goods go, the price is named, and no mark appears anywhere.

The reaches and the price are from Trident's rules/economy_transport — any unit may send to a quartermaster within 2 hexes, a quartermaster may ship to another quartermaster further, free within 2 hexes and otherwise 5 silver a weight unit at the top skill and more below it — and data/quartermaster, which gives the reach as 3 plus (level+1)/3 hexes and the rate as 4−((level+1)/2) × 5 silver per weight unit. New Origins' rules/economy_transport carries the same reaches without the free short range. Weights are from data/items: a fur weighs 1, grain 5, stone 50. Sentences are quoted exactly as they will ship.
