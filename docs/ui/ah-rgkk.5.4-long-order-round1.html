ah-rgkk.5.4 — the Long order popup, round 1

What is settled and not being re-opened. The popup itself, its heading and its cap (ah-rgkk.1); the family’s before → after pair (ah-rgkk.6, R1); and ah-rgkk.5.3’s L2 — where two sides are too long for one line, the Structure popup draws them as two lines, was above the column’s own label.

What this popup does today. One line — long order valued whatever your orders document currently holds — or one of two sentences (No long order this month., Another faction’s orders are not in your report.). It repeats the cell and says nothing more. Unlike every other column it has no Nothing this month changes this. sentence, because there is no recorded change behind it to hang one on.

What this bead adds, and where the baseline comes from. Your report carries an orders template — the #atlantis … #end block, one unit NNNN section each, holding the orders the turn arrived with (in practice your repeating @ orders). That is what seeds your orders document when a report is loaded, so on a freshly loaded turn every unit reads “unchanged”, and every difference you then see is one you made.

The eleven. rules/playing_turns: “Each unit can also do exactly one action that takes up the entire month… The orders which take an entire month are ADVANCE, BUILD, ENTERTAIN, MOVE, PILLAGE, PRODUCE, SAIL, STUDY, TAX, TEACH and WORK.” One of those eleven, at the top level of the unit’s block, is the long order — on both sides of the comparison.

Three questions: Q1, Q2, Q3. Q1 is the one that matters.

The scene. Braves (1487). Your report’s template holds @study obse for it. This month you decide it should tax instead, and the block now reads TAX. The Long order cell reads TAX.

Q1 — how the difference is drawn

The values here are longer and more varied than any other column’s: TAX is three characters and MOVE N NE NE NW is fifteen, while a PRODUCE … or a long SAIL route can run past thirty. The popup is 384px at its widest; the frames below are drawn at that width, so what you see is what wraps.

  • Identical to the Structure popup, which is the only other column with two long sides.
  • Never wraps, whatever the route length, and both sides read straight.
  • Two lines where one figure changed — more vertical space than the pair.

D2 — one line, the family pair

  • One rule for the whole table, and the shortest of the three.
  • Most long orders are short enough that it fits on one line.
  • A long SAIL or MOVE wraps, and the arrow can land at a line end where it reads as a hyphen — the exact reason the Structure popup rejected this.

  • The cell’s own value stays on the one line it has always been on.
  • Says in words where the other side came from, which neither D1 nor D2 does.
  • A fourth way of writing a before/after in one table, and the report’s side is demoted to dim italic where every other column gives it equal weight.

Q2 — does the popup say what the order will do?

A TAX earns silver; a STUDY costs it and moves a skill. The application already forecasts both — in the Silver popup and the Skills popup, on the same row.

E1 — no; the difference only

Braves (1487) — long order
was@study obse
long orderTAX
  • One column, one subject: what the unit will spend the month on and whether you changed it.
  • Nothing is said twice. The silver a TAX earns is the Silver popup’s working, and it is already there, one cell along.
  • You have to move the pointer to another cell to see the consequence.

E2 — yes; one dim clause on the line

Braves (1487) — long order
was@study obsewould have cost 200 silver
long orderTAXearns 145 silver
  • Answers “so what?” without moving the pointer.
  • Duplicates the Silver and Skills popups, which can disagree with it the moment either changes.
  • Only some of the eleven have a figure at all — MOVE, ADVANCE, SAIL, BUILD and TEACH have no silver figure at all — so the clause is absent more often than present.
  • The was side needs a counterfactual forecast that does not exist anywhere and would have to be built: substantially more work than the rest of this bead.

Q3 — what an unchanged long order says

Every other column ends its popup with Nothing this month changes this. when nothing moved. This column has never had that sentence, because until now it had nothing to compare against.

S1 — the same sentence as every other column

Braves (1487) — long order
long order@study obse
Nothing this month changes this.
  • Eight columns already end this way; the ninth joining them needs no explaining.
  • Tells you the popup did look, rather than leaving you unsure whether it can.
  • One more line on the commonest state there is.

S2 — nothing; the one line, as today

Braves (1487) — long order
long order@study obse
  • Quietest, and the state you will see most often by far.
  • Indistinguishable from a popup that never compared anything — and two of the states below are exactly that.
  • Out of step with the other eight columns.
