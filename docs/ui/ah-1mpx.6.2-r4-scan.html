Round 4 — the scan, and the export while it runs

This is the child that pays the family off: ah-1mpx.6.2. A background scan reads every stored turn's battle rosters when a game opens, and the export sends what it recovered.

Already settled, rounds 1–3, and nothing here reopens any of it: the scan reads every stored turn of the game, whoever's report it is; the newest observation wins; a derived skill never displaces a real one; and the export notice for the mixed case reads “6 units belong to another faction. 4 of them go out with combat skills read from battle reports; the other 2 go out with none.”

Three things that sentence and those rules do not yet answer. Each is user-visible and each is yours.

S Exporting while the scan is still running

The scan starts when the game opens and takes a moment — it loads and re-parses every stored turn. A player can open this dialog and press Export… before it finishes. The file is written once and never corrected, so whatever is true at that instant is what the simulator gets. Example throughout: 14 units exported, 6 foreign, 4 of them recoverable — but the scan has not landed yet, so right now the app knows about none of them.

S1 — say nothing, write what we have
AttackersNorthern Host — 9 units
DefendersCoastal Watch — 5 units
14 units will be exported.
6 units belong to another faction. They go out with no skills — no battle we have seen involved any of them.
CancelExport…
  • Nothing new on screen, and the dialog never makes the player wait.
  • The sentence is false, and confidently so — four of those six were in battles we have read. Two seconds later it would have said the opposite.
  • The file goes out missing four units' skills, and nothing ever tells the player.
S2 — a notice line, and Export waits   recommended
AttackersNorthern Host — 9 units
DefendersCoastal Watch — 5 units
14 units will be exported.
Still reading this game's battle reports. The foreign units' skills are not counted yet.
CancelExport…
  • Never writes a file that is missing something the app was about to know.
  • Says plainly why the button is off, so a disabled button is never a mystery.
  • The line and the disabling both disappear on their own the moment the scan lands, and the real notice replaces it.
  • A player who wants the file now is made to wait — a second or two on a long game.
S3 — no line; the button says it instead
AttackersNorthern Host — 9 units
DefendersCoastal Watch — 5 units
14 units will be exported.
CancelReading battle reports…
  • Quietest of the three — the dialog does not grow a line it will throw away a second later.
  • Also never writes an incomplete file.
  • The foreign-units notice is absent until the scan lands, so the caveat list visibly jumps when it arrives.
  • Overloads the button, which already says Exporting… while a file is being written — two different waits, one word.

W4 The same notice when it is not the mixed case

Round 3 chose the sentence for 4 of 6 recovered. Three other cases exist, and one of them — none recovered — is the commonest of all: 14 of the 26 committed reports contain no battle at all. Pick a shape and every case follows from it.

W4-A — one sentence shape, always
6 foreign, 4 recovered
6 units belong to another faction. 4 of them go out with combat skills read from battle reports; the other 2 go out with none.
4 foreign, all 4
4 units belong to another faction. 4 of them go out with combat skills read from battle reports; the other 0 go out with none.
6 foreign, none
6 units belong to another faction. 0 of them go out with combat skills read from battle reports; the other 6 go out with none.
1 foreign, recovered
1 unit belongs to another faction. 1 of them goes out with combat skills read from battle reports; the other 0 go out with none.
  • One sentence in the code, one to keep true.
  • “the other 0 go out with none” is not English, and it is on screen in two of the four cases.
  • Spends the reader's attention on arithmetic that resolves to “all” or “none”.

  • The none case borrows the wording already agreed for the Unit panel’s empty state — “No battle we have seen involved this unit.” — so the two surfaces say the same thing.
  • Five strings rather than two, and five to keep in step.

  • The commonest case is exactly today's text, so most players see no change at all.
  • Two fewer strings to write than B: the none branch is the code that is already there.
  • “a report never shows you those” sits one dialog away from a units table that is now showing exactly those — the sentence the whole family exists to stop asserting.

N A turn imported while the game is already open

The scan runs when a game opens. A player then imports this month's report, which contains a battle. Does that battle’s roster count straight away?

N1 — yes: the new turn folds in as it is imported   recommended
Import turn 72
Its rosters are read as part of the import and merged in. Turn 72 is newer than everything scanned, so where it disagrees it wins.
Units table, immediately
A unit that fought in turn 72 shows COMB 5 (turn 72).
Export, immediately
Counts it.
  • The turn you just imported is the one you are about to plan against; it is the worst one to be missing.
  • Costs nothing extra — the report is already parsed and in hand at that moment, so no turn is re-read from storage.
  • One more place the store is written, so two paths have to agree on the merge rule.

After closing and reopening the game

  • One write path, one rule, nothing to keep in step.
  • Stale in exactly the moment the player cares most, and with no sign that it is: the cell shows a turn number, confidently, and it is the wrong one.
  • “Close the game and open it again” is not something the application ever asks of the player anywhere else.
