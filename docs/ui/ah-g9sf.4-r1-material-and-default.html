A Farm of wood, a Farm of stone — round 1

Ten structures in the game can be built out of either wood or stone — Farm, Mine, Quarry, Ranch, Timber Yard, Inn, Trapping Hut, Stables, Oasis, Caravanserai. In the newer game world a player can say which they want (BUILD Farm WOOD), and if they don't say, the game spends stone before wood. That is written down in the rules, so the application can work it out exactly.

Today it does neither. The material word is thrown away, and a unit holding both is shown with a + ? mark — the application's way of saying something here could not be worked out.

The same unit, four ways, all of it run rather than remembered. One builder — 10 humans, farming 3, so its men could do 30 units of work in the month. A new Farm wants 10 units of work and one stick or block per unit. Whatever it is carrying, the answer is arithmetic: 10 of something, and then it stops, because the Farm is finished.

Q1 The player asks for wood and has none of it

This is the case that actively misleads today: the application shows a Farm going up and the stone leaving, and the game will simply refuse the line. Under all three options the stone stops moving and nothing is built — the question is where the player is told, and in whose words.

There is already a home for this. When a player tries to build something that cannot be built where they are standing, a line appears in the order editor directly under the offending order: “Cannot start a Tower here: this region already has a Tower. No stone will be used.” A build that cannot happen is exactly what that line is for. Separately, the ITEMS column's hover states flat refusals in its own voice — “Unit 901 is not a quartermaster, so this TRANSPORT moves nothing.” Both surfaces could carry this; neither has to.

Q2 The unit holds both, and the rule picks stone

The + ? mark goes in all three of these — the answer is no longer unknown. What is left is whether the player is told why their stone went and their wood stayed. The sentence naming the spend already ships; the amber line is the only thing in question.

Q3 The stone runs out halfway and wood finishes the job

Four stone and plenty of wood: the rule spends the stone, then carries on in wood — 4 stone and 6 wood on one Farm. Both piles drop in the cell either way; the question is how the hover says it. The application's existing sentence is written per material, so A is what falls out of the machinery and B is a deliberate choice.

Unchanged either way. Clicking the cell opens the fuller list, which already speaks one line per item — “stone: spent 4 on a build.” and “wood: spent 6 on a build.” Those stay per material under both options; this question is only about the one-glance hover.

  • Stone has vanished from the cell with no hint that running out of it is what pulled the wood in.

Q4 The suggestions while the line is being typed

The editor suggests words as you type. After a building name it offers only COMPLETE today, and after WOOD it offers nothing at all — so a player has no way to discover that the material words exist. Both options below fix that. The list wakes only once a letter is typed, as it does everywhere.
