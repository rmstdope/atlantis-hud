Descriptions for skills and items — proposals (bead ah-3cj4.2)

The ruleset carries 171 items and 96 skills and not one line of prose about any of them, so a reference entry can only ever show numbers. The prose is right there in the page — the parser reads the numbers out of it — but it is thrown away.

Two things about its shape decide this bead, and both are measured from the committed fixture.

§1  A skill is written five times, once per level 480 paragraphs, 96 skills, exactly 5 each

mining [MINI] 1: This skill deals with all aspects of extracting raw metals and gems from the earth. They tend to be found more often in mountainous regions, but may be found elsewhere as well. A unit with this skill may PRODUCE iron [IRON] at a rate of 1 per man-month. This skill costs 10 silver per month of study. mining [MINI] 2: No skill report. mining [MINI] 3: A unit with this skill may PRODUCE mithril [MITH] at a rate of 1 per man-month. A unit with this skill is able to determine if a region contains mithril. A unit with this skill may BUILD a Mine… mining [MINI] 4: No skill report. mining [MINI] 5: A unit with this skill may PRODUCE admantium [ADMT] at a rate of 1 per man-month…

Level 1 carries the general description; the odd levels say what the skill unlocks; levels 2 and 4 usually say “No skill report.” — a placeholder, not a description. The ruleset holds one entry per skill today, so five paragraphs have to become one field, or a field that holds five.

A Level 1 only

The general description, and nothing else.

Skill
mining
Cost
10 silver a month
Max level
5
This skill deals with all aspects of extracting raw metals and gems from the earth. They tend to be found more often in mountainous regions, but may be found elsewhere as well. A unit with this skill may PRODUCE iron [IRON] at a rate of 1 per man-month.
  • One field, one string — the smallest change.
  • Reads as a description, because that is what level 1 is.
  • Loses what the skill unlocks — that a miner reaches mithril at 3 and admantium at 5 is exactly what a player opens the entry to find out.

B Every level that says something

All five, with the “No skill report.” placeholders dropped.

This skill deals with all aspects of extracting raw metals and gems from the earth… may PRODUCE iron [IRON] at a rate of 1 per man-month.
Level 3
May PRODUCE mithril [MITH]… may BUILD a Mine.

  • Answers the question a player actually has: what do I get for studying this further?
  • Dropping the placeholders is what makes it readable rather than a wall.
  • The field is a list, not a string — more shape for ah-5jkt to render.

Including the placeholders.

§2  An item's entry begins with the numbers we already have and the prose follows it

chain armor [CARM], weight 1, costs 150 silver to withdraw. This is a type of armor. This armor will protect its wearer 33% of the time versus slashing attacks, 33% of the time versus piercing attacks… leader [LEAD], weight 10, walking capacity 5, moves 2 hexes per month. This race may study all skills to level 5. Longship [LONG]. This is a ship with a capacity of 150 and a speed of 4 hexes per month. This ship requires a total of 4 levels of sailing skill to sail.

The bold part is already in the ruleset as name, tag, weight and capacity. Keeping the whole paragraph as the description prints it all a second time.

D The prose after the preamble

Item · armor
chain armor
Weight
1
This is a type of armor. This armor will protect its wearer 33% of the time versus slashing attacks, 33% versus piercing, 33% versus crushing…
  • Each fact appears once, where it belongs.
  • Needs a rule for where the preamble ends — the first full stop after the [TAG], which holds for all three shapes above but is a rule that can be wrong.

E The whole paragraph

Item · armor
chain armor
Weight
1
chain armor [CARM], weight 1, costs 150 silver to withdraw. This is a type of armor. This armor will protect its wearer 33% of the time versus slashing attacks…
  • No rule to get wrong — it is the page, verbatim.
  • Carries facts the ruleset does not hold, such as costs 150 silver to withdraw.
  • The name and weight are printed twice in one small dialog.
Note what E would rescue: costs 150 silver to withdraw is in the page and in no field of the ruleset. Whichever is chosen, that fact is either kept as prose or lost — capturing it as a field is a different bead and is not proposed here.
