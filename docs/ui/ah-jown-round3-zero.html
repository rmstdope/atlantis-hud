Round 3 — the two ways of buying nothing

You chose Z2 (a unit that buys none says so in the hover) while the sentences were still in the W1 voice, and then chose W2. So the “buys none” sentence has not actually been settled in the voice that won. This page is only that.

And there are two of them, not one. A unit buys nothing either because it cannot afford one, or because the market has none left for it — and under M2 the second splits again when your own units took the line. The general sentences degrade badly at zero: “This unit has silver for 0 grain, not the 30 this market offers” is technically true and reads like a bug.

The situation on this page

Case 1 — cannot afford one. Unit 5518 Scouts holds 10 SILV, orders buy all GRAI, grain is $18.
Case 2 — your own units took the line. Unit 5518 orders buy all GRAI, but 5512 Hunters ordered buy 10 GRAI first and the market has exactly 10. 5518’s share is nothing.

For Sale: 10 grain [GRAI] at $18

Option Z-A — the general sentence, with zero written as a word

One template for every quantity. counted_item is asked for “no grain” rather than “0 grain”.

case 1 — cannot afford onewas: 10 SILV, 2 LEAT
This unit has silver for no grain, not the 10 this market offers.
case 2 — own units took itwas: 10 SILV, 2 LEAT
This unit gets none of the 10 grain this market has, because your units in this region are competing for it.
  • No special case in the code at all — one template, and zero is just a quantity.
  • Case 2 reads perfectly: “gets none of the 10” is exactly what happened.
  • Case 1 reads oddly. “has silver for no grain, not the 10 this market offers” contrasts a nothing with a something and makes the reader work.

Option Z-B — a dedicated sentence for each zero

Zero is its own case and says the thing the player needs: this order will do nothing, and why.

case 1 — cannot afford onewas: 10 SILV, 2 LEAT
This unit buys no grain: it holds 10 silver and one costs 18.
case 2 — own units took itwas: 10 SILV, 2 LEAT
This unit buys no grain: your other units in this region have claimed all 10 this market has.
  • Leads with the consequence — the order is dead — which is the one thing the player must not miss.
  • Case 1 gives both numbers in the form that explains it: what it holds, what one costs.
  • Still recognisably the house voice: “This unit …”, a colon, then the reason.
  • Two more strings, and two more branches, on top of the three from round 2.

Option Z-C — one zero sentence, cause not named

Zero collapses to a single line whichever cap produced it.

either casewas: 10 SILV, 2 LEAT
This unit buys no grain, so this order does nothing.
  • One string, and the warning is unmissable.
  • Does not say why, so the player cannot tell whether to give the unit money or to move it — opposite remedies.
Recommended: Z-B. The two zeroes have opposite remedies — send the unit silver, or stop its faction-mates competing — and Z-B is the only option that tells them apart while leading with the fact that the order is dead. Z-A is cheaper and its case 2 is already right; it is case 1 that reads like a bug.
