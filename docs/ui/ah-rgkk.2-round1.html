ah-rgkk.2 — the Skills popup

Resting on a unit’s Skills cell. The frame is settled by ah-rgkk.1: the head is “unit — column”, a change is an arrow and a signed amount, and dim italic sentences sit under the lines. This page is only what goes inside it for Skills, and there are three questions on it: A, B and D.

One rule settles section A before we start. rules/skills_studying: a STUDY order buys “one months worth of training”, and rules/sequenceofevents processes STUDY in the month-long phase — so a study never moves this month’s figures. It arrives in next turn’s report. The only thing that changes a unit’s skills within the month is men joining it: recruits, who bring nothing (rules/economy_recruiting: “New recruits will not have any skills or items”; rules/buy calls the effect dilute), and men arriving by GIVE or TAKE, who bring their own and can push a figure either way. Points are per man, so every arrival re-averages every skill at once.

B — What the arrow measures

A skill is a level and a count of study points (rules/skills_studying; the thresholds are 30, 90, 180, 300, 450). Dilution divides the points among more men, so the points always move and the level only sometimes does. Both variants show one unit: 12 men at COMB 2 (90) take 8 recruits, leaving (12×90)/20 = 54 — still level 1.

P1 — the arrow counts study points
Braves (1487) — skills
combat COMB1 (54) ▼ −36
observation OBSE1 (30) ▼ −20
8 barbarians recruited, and recruits bring no skills.
Combat falls from level 2 to level 1.
  • The arrow is on the thing that always moves, so it is never absent when something happened.
  • The points sit in brackets immediately to its left, so −36 reads against (54).
  • A level crossing gets its own sentence, where it can be said in words.
  • The number the player cares about most — the level — carries no mark of its own.
P2 — the arrow counts levels
Braves (1487) — skills
combat COMB1 ▼ −1
90 points down to 54
observation OBSE1
50 points down to 30
8 barbarians recruited, and recruits bring no skills.
  • The arrow is on the number that decides what the unit can actually do.
  • Observation has no arrow at all — it lost 20 points and one more recruit would drop it a level, but the line reads as if nothing happened.
  • The value drops the points, which the cell beneath the pointer and the whole-unit summary both show.

This unit’s headcount is a guess, so what recruiting does to these cannot be worked out.
  • C-c is real: the merge is integer arithmetic, and a skill whose share rounds to zero is dropped from the unit’s list outright. It has to be shown, or a skill vanishes from the cell with nothing said.
  • C-d is real: the core refuses to re-derive an estimated headcount rather than guess, so no dilution is computed for such a unit — and a popup showing unchanged figures without saying so would be lying.

D — A foreign unit whose skills were read out of a battle

Since ah-1mpx.6.3 the Skills cell of a foreign unit can show skills recovered from a battle roster: the cell prints COMB 3, RIDI 2 (turn 71). ah-rgkk.1 settled only the case where nothing was recovered, which is where its sentence “A report never shows another faction’s skills.” comes from. This is the case where something was.

B1 — list them, and say which battle they came from
Watazka (4839) — skills
combat COMB3

Read from the battle in plain (12,8) on turn 71.
A report never shows another faction’s skills; these are what a battle roster gave away.
  • Says where the knowledge came from and how old it is — turn 71 may be many months ago.
  • That sentence already exists and already ships, in the whole-unit summary (battleSkillSource).
  • No points to show — a battle roster gives a level and nothing else — so the lines are shorter than an own unit’s and the two popups do not look alike.
