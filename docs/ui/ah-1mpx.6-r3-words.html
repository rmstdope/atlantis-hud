Round 3 — the words, exactly as they ship

Settled: derived skills in the export, units table and Unit panel; the turn in the cell, collapsed when shared; newest observation wins; not disclosed holds until the scan lands; the panel heading is Skills from battle reports; the scan reads every stored turn of the game.

What is left is the sentences. Everything quoted on this page is quoted, not paraphrased — whatever is chosen here is what the implementer writes into the code, verbatim.

W The export dialog's foreign-units notice

It reads today: “6 units belong to another faction. They go out with their men and equipment but no skills — a report never shows you those.” That sentence is about to become false for some of them. The example throughout: 14 units exported, 6 of them foreign, 4 of those 6 carrying derived skills.

W1 — one sentence, split by the count   recommended
14 units will be exported.
2 units were not in this turn's report. They go out as they were when last seen.
6 units belong to another faction. 4 of them go out with combat skills read from battle reports; the other 2 go out with none.
  • One notice, as today — the dialog does not grow a line.
  • Gives the number that actually matters: how many of the six you know something about.
  • Has to name three numbers in one sentence.
W2 — the old sentence, corrected, plus a second notice
14 units will be exported.
2 units were not in this turn's report. They go out as they were when last seen.
6 units belong to another faction. A report never shows you their skills.
4 of them go out with combat skills read from battle reports instead.
  • Each line says one thing, which is how the other notices in this dialog read.
  • A fourth line in a dialog that already stacks three, and the second line contradicts the third until you have read both.
W3 — the rule rather than the count
14 units will be exported.
2 units were not in this turn's report. They go out as they were when last seen.
6 units belong to another faction. They go out with their men and equipment, and with any combat skills a battle report has shown.
  • Shortest, and always true whatever the counts are.
  • Never tells you whether that is four units or none — and none is the common case.

R A remembered Army member: two turns in one row

The strongest case for this whole bead, and the one that reads oddly. A foreign unit joined an Army at turn 71 and has not been seen since; the report on screen is turn 74. Its Seen column says turn 71 because that is when we last saw the unit. Its Skills say turn 71 too — but they need not: the skill could come from a battle at turn 72 in which the unit fought somewhere we could not see it standing.

X Three rules the implementer would otherwise have to guess. Say if any is wrong.

  • A derived skill never displaces a real one. Derived skills are used only where the unit's own skill list is empty — so one of your own units, and any unit whose report showed its skills, is untouched. In practice that means foreign units and nothing else.
  • Two battles in the same turn that disagree: the later one in the report wins, by the same “newest observation” rule, since report order is the only ordering a single turn has.
  • Sorting and filtering the Skills column read the text as drawn — so filtering for COMB finds derived rows, and sorting puts COMB 5 (turn 71) where its text falls. Nothing special-cases derived rows.

The JSON above is the shape packages/shared/src/armyExport.ts already emits; only the skills array changes from empty. RIDI, COMB, LBOW, TACT and XBOW are the ruleset's own tags for the five skills a roster prints, read from config/public/ruleset.json.
