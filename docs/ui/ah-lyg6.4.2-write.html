ah-lyg6.4.2 — Put into my orders

Your own faction's section on the study planner's Orders tab gains a third button, chosen as O2 in ah-lyg6.4's round-two interview. It merges next turn's STUDY and TEACH lines into the orders document you have been editing by hand — the first thing in this application that writes into that document from somewhere other than the editor. This page is the four things that decides.

Where the button is

Unchanged from the tab that ah-lyg6.4.1 builds; only the own faction's section gets the button, and only when there is at least one order to write.

What "merge" means

The same shape as the route planner's applyRoute: the unit's month-long order is replaced and everything else the player wrote is left exactly where it is. Ereb's block below is hand-written; only the marked line changes.

Before
;*** plain (7,53) in Inhead ***
unit 1234
  claim 200
  build Tower           ; nearly finished
  give 1250 20 silv
After
;*** plain (7,53) in Inhead ***
unit 1234
  claim 200
    build Tower           ; nearly finished
  give 1250 20 silv
  STUDY FORC 4
  • claim and give are not month-long orders, so they survive.
  • The comment annotations from the tab are not written into your own document — they are for a stranger reading a mailed file, and your own editor already names the mage. (Mine to decide unless you say otherwise.)
  • The build line is the question in section X below.

W — how safe the write is made

The one place in this application where a button rewrites text you typed — and Ctrl-Z will not save you. The orders editor deliberately keeps a write that came from outside it out of its undo history ("kept out of the undo history because the player did not do it", OrdersEditor.tsx:421), and its history is per unit anyway. There is no other undo in this application. So whatever is drawn below is the whole of the safety.

The line that reports the result is drawn inside the Orders tab, under the section heading, not in the header status line: this dialog covers the header, which is why it already reports its own save errors inline.

W1 Write at once, offer Undo

No dialog. The document changes, and the status line says what happened with an Undo that puts the whole document back byte for byte.
Study orders written for 3 mages. Undo
  • One press for the thing you came to the tab to do.
  • Undo covers every mage, not just the one on screen.
  • It happens to your hand-written text before you have seen what it will do.
  • The status line is easy to miss, and the Undo goes with it.

W2 Confirm first, no undo

A dialog listing every mage and the exact line about to be written, naming what it replaces. Write orders / Cancel.

  • You see exactly what will happen to text you wrote by hand, before it happens.

W3 Confirm first, and Undo afterwards

W2's dialog, and then W1's status line with its Undo.
Study orders written for 3 mages. Undo
  • The strongest, and what the round-one note asked for: "must be undoable".
  • The dialog explains; the Undo forgives a confirmation pressed too fast.
  • The most work of the three, and two safety nets on one action.

W4 Ask only when something would be lost

Silent, with W1's status line, whenever every mage's block is empty or already carries a STUDY/TEACH. W2's dialog only when a BUILD, PRODUCE, TAX or MOVE would go.
  • The dialog appears exactly on the turns where it earns its place.
  • The button behaves differently on different turns, which is the kind of thing a player learns wrongly and then trusts.

X — a mage already carrying another month-long order

Ereb's BUILD Tower. A unit gets one month-long order (rules/sequenceofevents), so the study and the build cannot both stand.

X1 Replaced, and said so

The study wins. The dialog row, or the status line, names what went.
Study orders written for 3 mages; 1 other order replaced. Undo
  • The planner is the thing you just told what each mage does; it finishes the job.
  • A build you were three months into is gone on one press.

X2 Kept, and the mage skipped

Ereb is left exactly as he was and named as skipped. You resolve it in the editor, or on the Schedule.
Ereb (1234)kept your BUILD Tower — no study written
  • Nothing you typed by hand can ever be destroyed by this button.
  • The plan and the document silently disagree, and the tab still shows the study as if it had been written.

X3 Yours to choose, per mage

W2's dialog, with a checkbox on every row. Rows that replace nothing are ticked; a row that would replace something else is unticked and has to be chosen.

  • Every case answered by the person who knows which they meant.
  • A real control with real state, and it forces W2 or W3 — X3 cannot exist without the dialog.

Y — a mage with no unit block in the document

What a report carrying OPTION TEMPLATE OFF produces: the document is #atlantis … #end and nothing else. writeUnitOrders leaves such a unit alone rather than inventing a block, so this has to be decided.

Y1 The block is made, under that mage's own region banner

Exactly as the editor's first keystroke makes one today, and as the report itself would have written it.
#atlantis 95
;*** plain (7,53) in Inhead ***
unit 1234
  STUDY FORC 4

#end
  • A player who turned the template off still gets a complete, valid orders file.
  • Each mage gets the banner of the hex he stands in, not the hex you have selected.
  • The button writes structure, not just a line — the largest thing it does.

Y2 Skipped, and named

Only mages the document already lists are written. The rest are reported: Thane (1301) — no block in your orders; select him and type a line first.
  • Nothing structural is invented.
  • With the template off, that is every mage, so the button does nothing at all for the players who most need it.
