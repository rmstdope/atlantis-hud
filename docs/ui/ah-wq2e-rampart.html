Walls between hexes: how a wall looks

This is a real piece of the underground from the Arcanum report (faction 3, turn 84), around the cavern Ciestucshire (9,3) in Sostrunland. The report gives exits for seven hexes. A wall is drawn wherever one of those lists leaves a neighbour out. Ciestucshire itself has walls on its North, Northeast and Southwest sides.

What to look for in every picture: the outlined hex is Ciestucshire. Hexes shown with only their terrain were named in somebody's exits but never visited, so their own sides get no wall. Grey hexes are unexplored. A wall facing an unexplored hex is still drawn when the visited side proves it (for example the Southwest side of (7,3)).

Option A: black wall

A thick, near-black bar on the shared edge, the same on every theme, with a thin pale halo so it still shows on the dark caverns. This is closest to "some kind of black wall". It is the most visible option, but on the dark themes it reads a little like a hole in the map.

Option B: rampart

A bar in the theme's own ink with short cross-ticks on both faces, like a wall drawn on an old map. It cannot be mistaken for a province border or a hex edge, but it is the busiest of the three where an underground area has many walls.

Option C: heavy edge in the theme's ink

The hex edge itself, drawn about four times heavier in each theme's own ink. It is the quietest option and fits each theme. It is the easiest to overlook, and on a theme whose ink is pale it reads more as "outlined" than "blocked".
