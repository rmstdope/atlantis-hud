A sharing unit's BUY, and the purse it is sized against

A unit that shares lets the other units of your faction standing in the same hex spend its silver. Atlantis HUD adds all of that silver into one purse and sizes a BUY against it — which is right: rules/buy and the engine both size each buy against the whole shared purse and cut nobody on account of another buyer (settled in ah-szye).

What was wrong is which money went into the purse. It counted money the other units merely hoped for — tax they will have to split with your own other units taxing the same region. In one real turn that inflated a hex's purse to $232,238 and told a unit holding nothing it would buy 389 gnolls for $25,674, then said in the same row that the unit finished the month about $21,700 short. Two numbers in one row that cannot both be true.

Chosen options are outlined in green. Everything else was drawn, offered and rejected, and the reasons are kept below so the same ground is not walked twice.

The scene every drawing uses. A hex with a market selling 100 horses at $50. Three of your units stand there and all three share. Two of them tax the same region, so neither collects what it could alone — the HUD already settles that split (ah-t2pn) and shows each of them the smaller figure everywhere else on screen.

Question 1 — which money goes into the purse

The number that changes is how many horses Herders is told it buys.

rejectedA Only silver already in hand

The purse holds what your units are sitting on right now and no income at all. Nothing yet to arrive can be spent at the market. Herders buys 12 of 100.
  • Never promises a horse the faction cannot pay for.
  • The game does not work this way. rules/sequenceofevents puts TAX before BUY, so taxed silver really is spendable — a player following the HUD would buy 12 and watch the game hand them 52.
  • Makes the HUD wrong in a new direction rather than right.

chosenB Silver in hand, plus the income we already believe

The purse holds cash plus each unit's settled income — the same figure the HUD already shows on that unit's own row. One number for one unit, everywhere on screen.

rejectedC Count the hopeful money, mark the count ?

The purse counts everything anybody might get, as today, but the quantity bought shows as ? whenever any of it is uncertain.

  • Answers nothing about the month-end figure. ? is not agreement, it is silence.

rejectedR Charge each neighbour its own share

Herders shows only its own $1,000 spent; the other $1,600 appears as a cost on Treasury's and Garrison's rows.
  • Every row true on its own, and the hex adds up.
  • Needs a rule for which neighbour pays and in what order. The game does not say, and ah-szye deliberately left it alone.
  • Silver appears to leave a unit that issued no order at all.

Question 3 — a warning about tax nobody has collected yet

The purse contains $2,000 of uncollected tax. If a taxer is beaten to it the player buys fewer horses than promised. Chosen: nothing is added. The count is a forecast like every other number in the HUD, and forecasts are not annotated one by one; a note on every BUY in a taxing, sharing hex would be there nearly always and stop being read. Rejected with it: the same note gated on the purse being mostly unearned, which adds a threshold a player cannot see.

Question 4 — when the purse cannot be worked out at all

If a unit taxing that region has an estimated headcount, nobody's settled income is a number, so neither is the purse. The hex is still holding $600 of actual silver.

The purse falls back to silver actually in hand — a number nobody disputes — so the count stays a number, and the sentence says it is a floor.

Question 5 — the sentence that already covers this

The HUD already shows a dim note whenever a neighbour's money pays for a unit's orders: “A faction-mate's silver in this hex pays for this unit's orders.” With a line now carrying the figure, that sentence repeats it a line lower and without the number.

What does not change

SituationWhat happens
Nobody in the hex shares No purse. The unit buys against its own money exactly as today, and no new line appears.
The unit shares but no neighbour has anything The purse is the unit's own money. No was lent line: it is drawn only when a neighbour's silver actually pays for something, and $0 is nothing to report.
Only one unit taxes the region Nothing is contended, so settled income equals hoped-for income and today's figures are already right. Nothing on screen changes.
The market runs out before the money does Unchanged. The shipped sentence still explains it — “This market has 52 horses, not the 100 this unit's silver would buy.”
Two of your units both BUY against the same purse Unchanged, deliberately: the game sizes each order against the whole purse and cuts neither (ah-szye). Both are shown buying what they ordered, and the hex-level shortfall is reported as it is today.
The buying unit's own row Still red, still marked ⚠ when a check names it, and the Problems panel still says “the units in this hex are short $N between them…” only when the hex really is short. With the purse corrected, that sentence stops firing on hexes that were never short.
