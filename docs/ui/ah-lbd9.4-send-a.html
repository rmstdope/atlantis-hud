ah-lbd9.4 — Option A: one Send dialog, which can also sign you in

Send is switched off for a New Age game today, because the ruleset declares no upload address. This bead turns it back on by a different road: the REST API, with the bearer token ah-lbd9.2 produces. This page is one of two shapes. In A, Send is always live — pressing it opens one dialog that asks for whatever it still needs. Signed in, that is the faction password alone; signed out, it is the faction number and the password, and signing in happens as part of sending. The verdict comes back as a sentence and a list. Compare with Option B, where Send is off until you have signed in from the header, and the verdict comes back in the same monospace block the New Origins send already uses.

Already decided and not being reopened: credentials are never stored; New Age is desktop only; one step, not two — Send posts the orders and then shows what came back, and the check-without-saving endpoint is deliberately not used; and when a session runs out, the surface you are already in asks for it (E1) rather than sending you back to the header.

Chosen (2026-09-04): A — Send is always live and the dialog signs you in as part of sending; errors and warnings as two lists; saved-with-errors reads amber; and the footer after any settled answer is Close only. Round 2 below (§6) is the two states that answer opened up.

The awkward fact this whole page is about: the world can save orders it also thinks are wrong. It keeps them as long as the #atlantis … #end envelope, the faction id and the password are right, and tells you separately how many syntax errors it found. So the answer is never a simple yes or no, and there are three outcomes to tell apart, not two.

6 · Round 2 — two states your answer opened up

Neither was drawn above, and both are visible. W is what the sentence says when the world found no errors but did raise warnings — §3’s clean sentence, “found nothing wrong with them”, sits badly over a list of warnings. M is whether the world’s own message is ever put in front of you: it is the only thing the API returns when a send is refused outright, and the API’s prose is partly in Russian, so it may or may not arrive in English.
