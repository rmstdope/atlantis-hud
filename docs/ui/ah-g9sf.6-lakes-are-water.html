Lakes are water — the agreed shape

In New Age: Trident a lake is water. A fleet on its shore may sail out onto it and back; a unit on foot cannot walk across it; a flier may cross it but drowns if the month runs out over it. Atlantis HUD treated a lake as dry land in all three cases — planning marches straight across one and refusing legal sailings — while the map painted it as water, so map and route contradicted each other in front of the player. Every panel below is what the player sees after the change. Nothing changes in New Origins, where a lake stays dry land.

Agreed, and this is the record. A refusal names the terrain the report named, and never explains that this game counts lakes as water. One sailing sentence serves both games, saying water. A clicked-on lake gets a sentence of its own. A flier's water hexes are marked on the step; a fleet's are not. The unexplored-hex warning says may be water.

What is left alone. A lake keeps the terrain name its report gave it — it is never redrawn or relabelled as ocean anywhere the player looks. The map already paints lakes as water and keeps doing so; after this the route agrees with it. Nothing new is shown on the map itself, no hex is greyed out or marked unreachable in advance, and every refusal reaches the player where refusals already reach them: the movement panel for a planned route, the region's problem list for an order typed by hand.
The agreed experience for one piece of work: lakes count as water in New Age: Trident — a lake stops a march, allows a sail, and can drown a flier. Agreed over two rounds; this drawing is the chosen shape.
