Walls — where two neighbouring hexes have no way between them

Underground, a cave can sit right next to another one and still have no way through. The cavern at (9,3) in Sostrunland lists only three ways out: south-east, south and north-west. Its north, north-east and south-west sides are closed. The map draws all six sides the same, so it looks as though you could walk through any of them.

Every drawing below is that same patch of the underworld, taken from the turn 84 report. Two caverns were seen in full: (9,3) and (9,1) just north of it. Their neighbours are only named, and the grey hexes have never been seen at all. A wall is drawn only where a hex we have seen in full leaves out the side facing its neighbour.

Question 1 — what a wall looks like

The same patch in each of three looks: close up, then zoomed right out, where a dense underground level shows many walls at once.

ABlack wall along the whole side

A heavy black line along the entire closed side, with a thin pale edge so it stands out against dark ground. Like a wall on a floor plan.

Zoomed out
  • Unmistakable. Reads as a wall with no explanation.
  • Closed-off caves are outlined from end to end, so you can see the passages between them.
  • The heaviest of the three. A dense level gets very black when zoomed out.

BRock face

A jagged, zig-zag line along the side, like a cliff drawn on an old map. It suits the hand-drawn atlas look.

Zoomed out
  • Clearly different from a region border and from a hex outline.
  • Zoomed out, the zig-zag blurs into a thick grey smudge.
  • The other map styles need a look of their own rather than the same zig-zag.

CA bar across the middle of the side

A short, thick black bar on the middle of the closed side only. The corners stay clear.

Zoomed out
  • Lightest when zoomed out, and never covers the corners where other lines meet.
  • Reads more like a gate that is shut than a wall, and the outline of a closed-off cave is broken up.

Question 2 — an order that walks into a wall

A unit in (9,3) with the order MOVE N NE. The first step goes north, where there is no way through. Today the map draws that step as an ordinary solid line straight through. (The route planner already goes around closed sides, so nothing about it changes.) Both drawings use wall look A.

AThe line stops at the wall

The route runs up to the wall and ends in a red cross. Nothing after it is drawn.

  • Plain: the journey does not go past this point.
  • The rest of what you wrote (the NE step) is not shown anywhere on the map.

BThe whole order is drawn, the blocked step in red

The line reaches the wall and gets a red cross. The step through the wall is drawn dashed in red, and everything after it is dotted to show it is in doubt.

  • You can still see the whole of what you wrote, and where it goes wrong.
  • Draws a journey past a wall that the unit cannot actually make.
