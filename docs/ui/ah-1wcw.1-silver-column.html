The Silver column — as agreed in planning

Every decision on this page was taken by the navigator during the planning of ah-1wcw.1, several of them against measurements from the committed turn-71 template. Rows are the shapes the column has to survive, not a happy path.

The column

A tenth column, headed Silver, last in the shipped order after Long order. It shows the silver each of your own units is expected to have when the month ends: what it holds now, plus what its orders earn, less what they spend. Right-aligned, tabular figures, sortable like Men and Long order.

This bead models three sources — TAX, WORK and STUDY. Selling, buying, giving, receiving, entertainment, magic and upkeep follow in ah-1wcw.2, .3 and .4, each as another term in the same arithmetic.

Red and ⚠ mean different things

This is the decision that shapes the column, and it was taken against measurements of the committed turn-71 template.

  • Red — this unit, counted on its own, ends the month below zero. True of 17 of the 27 units in that turn, because most of that faction's mages hold no silver at all: they are flagged sharing and funded from a purse unit standing beside them.
  • ⚠ — this unit carries the existing not-enough-silver finding. That check already counts what a unit holds, what its orders earn and spend, and pools it across every same-faction unit in the hex flagged sharing, exactly as the engine's own GetSharedNum rule does. It fires zero times on turn 71: that faction is solvent.
  • No new advisory code is added by this bead. The warning already existed; what was missing was the number. The cell and the Problems panel therefore agree by construction, and the existing Not enough silver switch under Settings → Warnings turns both off together.
  • Had the warning fired on any negative net change instead, it would have spoken about 22 of 27 units, against the ten findings every other check produces between them.
  • In a hex where units share, the existing finding is anchored to the hex rather than to one unit — deliberately, because blaming one of several would be as wrong as blaming the treasurer for holding the purse. No row carries a ⚠ in that case; the region panel carries the finding instead.

The hover

The table already opens a panel when the pointer rests on a row, showing that unit's skills and items. The silver working joins it as a third section rather than opening a second panel on the cell — one hover surface per row, as today. A term that could not be priced shows ?, and the section gains one dim line wherever the number needs explaining.

A warning on a positive number

The column counts what WORK earns, because wages really are paid and the unit really does end the month holding them. not-enough-silver deliberately does not, because wages arrive in the turn's last phase and cannot pay for anything ordered this month. So a unit that works and buys can read positive and still carry the ⚠. Both are true, about different moments in the turn, and the hover says so rather than either one being hidden.
