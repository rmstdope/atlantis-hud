The Silver column — as agreed in planning

A tenth column on the units-in-hex table, headed Silver: what this month's orders are expected to leave each of your own units up or down. This bead builds the seam and proves it with three sources — TAX, WORK and STUDY. Selling, buying, giving, receiving, entertainment, magic and upkeep follow in ah-1wcw.2, .3 and .4, each as another term in the same arithmetic.

Every decision on this page was taken by the navigator during planning. Rows are the shapes the column has to survive, not a happy path.

The warning

A negative net is a real advisory finding, code spends-more-than-it-earns, on by default. It carries the same ⚠ the rest of the application uses, counts in the header chip, is walkable with F8, and the cell is clickable: it selects the unit and opens its orders, exactly as the Problems panel's own unit button does.

PROBLEMS PANEL

MOUNTAIN (7,53) IN INHEAD

Units that spend more than they earn
A unit whose month's orders are expected to leave it out of pocket.

MEASURED What this fires on today

The committed turn-71 template holds 27 unit blocks: 22 studying, 2 with no orders, one moving, one sailing, one working, none taxing. So this check produces 22 findings on that turn, against the ten every other check produces between them.

  • The planner recommended shipping it off by default, on the precedent of hex-unguarded, and the navigator chose on: the request says a negative balance should warn, and a player who studies at a loss on purpose can switch it off.
  • It is not not-enough-silver, which asks whether a hex's units can pay for their orders from what they hold and share. A mage holding 300 silver and studying at 300 passes that one and is negative here.
