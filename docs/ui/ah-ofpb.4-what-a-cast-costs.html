ah-ofpb.4 — what a cast costs, when the spell makes more than one

Today cast() charges a spell's stated cost exactly once, whatever the mage's level. The rules state that cost per item created, and every creating spell makes more than one at higher levels — so a level 3 amulet maker makes three amulets and the app charges for one. This bead fixes the arithmetic. It moves the Silver column, and it changes which units the shipped not-enough-silver and not-enough-items warnings fire on, which is why all three questions below are yours and not mine.

Already settled, and not being re-asked: that a percentage total past 100 makes whole items and carries the remainder as a chance (so 90 × 3 = 270% is two runeswords certain and a 70% chance of a third), and that the ruleset records what every creating spell makes. Both were decided on 26 Aug while planning ah-ofpb.3. What is not settled is which single number a column can show for “between $1,200 and $1,800”, and that is Q2.

Q1 — the mage that cannot afford the whole run

The Amuletsmith's level says three amulets, $600, and it holds $400. The Swordwright's level says fifteen mithril swords, and it holds four swords. This is the same shape as a PRODUCE whose unit is short of silver or materials — and that case is already answered in this app: ah-ofpb.1 shows the capped figure and explains it in the hover, and no warning fires. The question is whether a cast follows PRODUCE, or goes on warning. It decides whether a shipped warning — the one that today tells an enchanter with no swords that its cast will do nothing — survives this bead.

Only the two certain spells appear here, so Q2 does not muddy it. Silver shown is the month-end figure the column actually prints.

Q2 — the chance spells: which number does a column show?

The Ringmaker's cast is 60% — no ring for certain, a 60% chance of one, at $600. The Runesmith's is 270% — two runeswords certain, a 70% chance of a third, at $600 each, so somewhere between $1,200 and $1,800. The Silver column has one number to print. Which end? Both mages here can afford either answer, so Q1 does not muddy it.

The page's own wording for the one spell that only ever attempts: “The mage has a 20 percent times his skill level chance of success, and the attempt costs 1000 silver.” Construct Gate is charged whether or not it succeeds — which is a reason to think a failed roll may cost the mage too, and an argument for B. The seventeen artifact spells say “at a cost of” and never say “the attempt”.data/CGAT, committed data page
