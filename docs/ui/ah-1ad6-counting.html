ah-1ad6 — counting the combat ready, round 2

Settled: weapons get scraped into the ruleset first, so the count can be done properly rather than guessed; a faction that cannot pillage sees income 0 and a mark on the order line; and the men counted are every own unit's in the hex, as the rule says.

That makes this two beads: a ruleset bead for the weapon data, and this one for the count and the mark.

The data is smaller and cleaner than feared. Sixteen weapons, every one kind: equipment, and exactly three sentences between them:

No skill is needed to wield this weapon.  — 12 of them
Knowledge of crossbow [XBOW] is needed to wield this weapon.  — XBOW, MXBO
Knowledge of longbow [LBOW] is needed to wield this weapon.  — LBOW, DBOW

What the rules never say is how those weapons turn into a number of combat ready men.

Question 4 how many of a unit's men are combat ready

A unit of 50 orcs holding 10 swords, in a hex needing 90.

OptionCounts
A — one weapon per man 10 — a man needs a weapon he can wield, so the count is capped by the weapons held. How the engine reads a taxing unit, and the strictest reading of “combat ready”. Understates nothing; may overstate the problem if the phrase means less than that.
B — armed unit, all its men 50 — the unit holds a usable weapon, so the unit is combat ready and all its men count. Reads “combat ready” as a property of the unit, which is how the rules use it elsewhere: “only combat-ready units may issue this order”.
C — every man, armed or not 50, and 50 again with no swords at all. The one-sided headcount test, keeping weapons out of it entirely. Never warns wrongly, and misses the faction of 200 unarmed peasants.

A weapon needing crossbow or longbow counts only for a unit that has that skill, under A and B alike.

Question 5 does an avoiding unit count?

The observed unit — The Lost One (683) — is flagged avoiding, behind. AVOID 1 means the unit runs rather than fights.

Option
No — avoiding men are not combat ready A unit set to run from every fight is not ready for combat in any ordinary sense. The rules do not say so, and this is the reading that most matches the words.
Yes — the flag is about combat, not readiness avoiding decides whether the unit is drawn into a battle, not whether it could fight. Fewer assumptions; counts men the game might not.

behind is not in question either way — a behind unit fights, from the back rank.
