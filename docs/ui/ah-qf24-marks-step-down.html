Option A — the coordinates keep the top edge; whatever was there steps down beneath them

Every hex, in every map look, keeps its coordinates in the same place: just inside the top edge. Where a look draws something there (a battle mark, Blueprint's revision note), that one thing moves down just far enough to sit under the coordinates. With coordinates switched off, nothing moves and the map looks exactly as it does today.

One rule for every look: the number is always at the top, everything else sits below it — the way printed hex maps are numbered. Cost: the battle mark and the revision note sit a little lower than their looks first drew them, and they move when the switch is flipped.
