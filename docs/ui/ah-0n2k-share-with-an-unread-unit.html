ah-0n2k — a unit's share of a hex it shares with an unread unit

Chosen 2026-09-10, over two rounds. Atlantis HUD already knows what to do with a unit whose line the turn report cut short: it keeps the unit on the map, refuses to show figures it does not have, and stops advising about it (ah‑l09a). What it does not do is notice that such a unit is still standing in a hex, still able to compete for that hex's wages, its entertainment demand, its tax base and its market. So every other unit of yours in the same hex is quietly told it will earn more than it will — and those units were read perfectly, so nothing on their rows suggests anything is uncertain. That is the same false confidence ah‑l09a exists to remove, moved one hex outward onto units that look fine.

The decisions: a neighbour's earnings become a ceiling rather than going silent, because an unread unit can only ever take share away; the phrase is “60 at most”, mirroring the application's existing “5 or more”; it covers all four of a hex's shared things; nothing louder than the unit itself is said; where a neighbour's headcount is also only an estimate the existing “not known” still wins; and the compact unit list marks nothing extra.

The states what it looks like when it is not one tidy hex

WhenWhat happens
The hex is fineNothing changes at all — no ceiling, no sentence, no mark. This is the common case and must cost nothing.
The unread unit itselfUnchanged. It still shows “not known” for everything and still gives no advice.
The unread unit could not possibly want the poolThere is no such case. A cut‑short line loses the very flags and skills that would say whether the unit works, taxes, entertains or trades, so it has to be treated as a possible claimant on every one of the hex's shared things.
A foreign unit could not be readNothing happens. Foreign units were never part of the sharing‑out — Atlantis HUD only ever divides a hex between your own units.
Nothing in the hex looked contestedThe ceiling appears anyway. This is the change: a hex that looked uncontested may not be, and the unread unit is why.
Your unit is not earning hereNothing. A unit that neither works, entertains, taxes nor trades has no share to bound.
The hex's totalsFollow the units. A total containing a bounded figure is itself bounded, and reads “N at most”.
Both kinds of trouble at once“Not known” wins, with the existing estimated‑headcount sentence. See above.
The overrun warningAtlantis HUD warns when your units ask a hex for more than it has. It cannot know that now, so it stays quiet — the ceiling is what says something is uncertain.
Several unread units in one hexOne ceiling and one sentence, exactly as for one — the sentence stays singular in shape rather than counting them.

Considered and rejected

InsteadWhy not
The figure goes away — “not known”Consistent with the estimated‑headcount case and never shows a number that could be wrong. Rejected: a player who was going to earn between $45 and $60 would be told nothing at all, which is less than they knew before, and one badly wrapped line would silence the month for every other unit in the hex.
up to 60The plainest English, but it puts the qualifier first so a column of figures no longer lines up on the digits, and “up to” reads as advertising — a promise rather than a limit.
60 or lessThe closest mirror of “or more”, one word swapped. Rejected: it sounds like a shrug rather than a bound.
Wages and entertainment onlyNarrower blast radius, and the two the trouble was first noticed in. Rejected: the same false confidence would survive untouched on a unit taxing or trading beside an unread one.
A ceiling for an estimated headcount tooOne rule, honestly applied — any neighbour that cannot be counted only ever takes away, so a ceiling is true in that case as well, and more useful. Rejected as wider than this piece of work: it would silently change what the application already shows, making a figure appear where none did before.
A line on the region panelWould explain it once for the whole hex rather than once per unit. Rejected: the unread line is already counted in the ⚠ N to check chip and listed on the panel's Not read tab with its own sentence, and a third telling is noise.
An entry in ProblemsVisible without hovering. Rejected for the same reason, and because Problems is for things the player can act on — a badly wrapped report is not one of them.
Marking the neighbour in the compact unit listVisible at a glance. Rejected: in a hex of eight units one wrapped line marks all eight, and it duplicates what the unit's own figure already says.
A shorter sentence dropping “from the turn report”Rejected: every other unread sentence in the application names the turn report as where the trouble was, and dropping it here would leave the player with nowhere to look.
