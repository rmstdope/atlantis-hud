Going through a passage — what the map shows

Some structures hold a passage that leads out somewhere else entirely — a shaft down into the underworld, a gate that opens on the far side of the world. A player's orders can send a unit through one, and then keep walking on the other side.

Today Atlantis HUD acts as though the passage were not there. The unit is left standing where it was, and every step written after the passage is drawn from the old hex — a firm, ordinary brass line through country the unit will never see. When the report does not say where the passage comes out, the map still draws that line with total confidence.

The rules are plain about what the order means — rules/move: “IN, which will move through an inner passage in the structure that the unit is currently in”; and rules/tableitemweights: “the movement point cost is equal to the normal cost to enter the destination region”. So it is travel, and it is not free.

Three questions. Each one is drawn below: the same unit, the same orders, three ways of showing them. Only hand-written orders are affected — the route planner never proposes a passage, because it only ever offers journeys it can stand behind.

What the map does today, and why it is wrong

One unit standing in a shaft at the top-left, ordered through the passage and then two steps south-east. The line you see is what ships today.

Today — the passage is ignored
  • The two steps after the passage are drawn from the shaft's own hex.
  • Solid brass, the same line the map uses for a journey it is sure of.
  • The passage itself costs nothing and moves nothing.

Question 1 — the passage leads somewhere we do not know

The commonest case by far: the report never says where the shaft comes out, because the player has not been down it. We know the unit leaves; we do not know where it arrives. Three ways to draw that.

Which of these should the map do when the far side of a passage is unknown?

A The line stops, and the hex is marked

The route ends at the structure's hex. That hex carries a small brass ring with a question mark: the journey goes on, somewhere we cannot draw. Hovering it says so.

Through the passage in Shaft [3]
Where this passage comes out is not in any report yet, so the rest of the journey — 2 more steps — cannot be drawn.
  • Nothing false is drawn anywhere on the map.
  • The mark says the route is unfinished, not that it failed.
  • The remaining steps are still counted, so the player knows how much is missing.
  • A new mark to learn.

B The line simply stops

The route ends at the structure's hex and nothing else is drawn. The map says only what it knows. Everything about the passage is told in words, elsewhere.

  • Quietest possible map; no new vocabulary at all.
  • Looks exactly like a unit that was ordered to stop there.
  • A player reading the map alone learns nothing about the missing half.

C The remaining steps trail off the hex

The route ends at the structure, and the steps we cannot place are drawn as a short faded stub leaving that hex in no particular direction — a tail that fades out.

  • The unfinished journey is visible without reading anything.
  • The stub points somewhere, and every direction it could point is a lie.
  • On a crowded map it reads as a real route into the neighbouring hex.

Question 2 — where the player is told, and in what words

Whatever the map does, something has to say why the route is short. Atlantis HUD already keeps a counted list of Problems and a Warnings tab of things a player can switch on and off.

Does an unknown passage belong in the Problems list, and what should it say?

1 A problem, in a new Movement group

It joins the counted Problems list like an overloaded fleet or a gift to a unit that is not here, under a heading of its own, switchable like the rest.

Question 3 — the passage leads somewhere we do know

Once the far side has been seen, the HUD knows both ends. The two ends are usually on different levels of the map — a shaft joins the surface to the underworld — and the map draws one level at a time, so the route arrives on a map the player is not looking at.

How should the map show a journey that comes out somewhere else?

K1 Each level draws its own half, with a matched pair of marks

The surface shows the walk up to the shaft, ending in a brass ring. Switch to the underworld and the journey picks up at a matching ring and carries on. The rings name each other.

nexussurfaceunderworld
Through the passage in Shaft [3]
Comes out in cavern (12,34) in the underworld. Costs 2 movement points, the cost of entering that cavern.
  • Truthful about distance: nothing is drawn crossing country it does not cross.
  • Works whether the two ends are on the same level or not.
  • The two halves are never on screen together.

K2 A drawn link between the two hexes

On the same level, a long faded arc joins entry to exit, so the whole journey is one line. On different levels it falls back to the pair of marks, because there is nowhere to draw the arc.

  • The whole journey reads as one thing when both ends are visible.
  • The arc crosses hexes the unit never enters — the exact fault being fixed.
  • Behaves differently on one level than on two, which has to be explained.

K3 Follow the unit — the map changes level with the route

Selecting the unit shows the level the route ends on, with the earlier half left behind on the level above.

  • Puts the interesting end in front of the player without a click.
  • The map moves under the player when they select a unit, which nothing else does.
  • The half they were looking at vanishes.

The cases the three drawings above hide

These are not variants to choose between — they are the corners, drawn so the answers can be checked against them.

CaseWhat happensWhy
Entering a structure by its number, and leaving it again Unchanged in every way. No mark, no notice, no cost, no step. Walking into a tower is not travel; it has always been free and invisible and stays so.
Through the passage and nothing after it The route ends at the structure's hex and the mark is still drawn; the words say the journey ends somewhere unknown. The unit really does end the month somewhere the player cannot point at.
Through a passage whose far side is known, and on for three more steps Drawn in full on the far level, priced at the cost of entering the destination. The rules price a passage as the cost of entering the region it leads to.
A nexus gate, which lands somewhere chosen at random Treated as unknown, always — even in worlds where a preference exists. A guess drawn confidently is the fault being fixed; no exception for a clever guess.
Through a passage in a structure the unit is not in The existing refusal, untouched. Already handled; the passage work adds nothing to it.
The month runs out before the passage The line goes solid then dotted as it always has; the mark sits on the structure's hex whichever month reaches it. The mark is about knowledge, the dotting is about time; they stack.
A narrow window The problem line wraps to as many lines as it needs; nothing is shortened. Every other problem line already behaves this way.

What is not being changed

  • The brass route line itself — solid for the month ahead, dotted beyond it — keeps every rule it has. A passage mark sits on top of that language rather than replacing it.
  • The route planner is untouched. It plans over country the faction has seen and will not offer a passage at all.
  • Nothing guesses where a random gate lands, in any world.
