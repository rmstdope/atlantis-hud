Q2–Q4 — the hover, and the states the happy path hides

These pages assume Q1 landed on a settled figure in the cell. What is left is what the hover says, and what the three awkward states look like: a unit that can afford none, a market that runs out before the silver does, and two BUY ALL lines on one unit.

Every sentence below is written as it would ship. The ITEMS hover is a stack of lines beginning was: … — that shape is already in place and does not change.

Q2 — what the hover says about a BUY ALL

Same unit as Q1: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR, buy all GRAI, market 30 grain at $18, buys 19 for 342. The silver ran out first.

H1Says nothing extra

The cell already shows 19 GRAI. The hover keeps only the line it always had.

hoverwas: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR
  • Nothing new to write, nothing new to translate, nothing that can go stale.
  • The player has to do 356 ÷ 18 themselves to see why it is 19 and not 30.
  • Gives no warning at all in the two states below, where the answer is none.

H2Names the cap that bit

One line, and it says which limit produced the number — the unit’s silver, or the market’s stock.

hoverwas: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR
Buys 19 grain — as many as its silver will pay for.
when the market runs out firstBuys 5 grain — all the market will sell it.
  • Answers the question the player actually has, which is “why that number?”.
  • The same shape BUILD uses (ah-ofpb.2): say the figure, then say what stopped it.
  • Two sentences to keep right instead of none, and a third for the tie.

H3One sentence, always the same

States the figure without naming the limit.

hoverwas: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR
Buys 19 grain, the most this order can get.
  • One string, whatever the situation.
  • Repeats the cell and adds nothing — the number is already visible two centimetres away.

Q3 — the unit that can afford none

Unit 5518 Scouts holds 10 SILV, 2 LEAT and orders buy all GRAI at $18. It buys nothing and spends nothing. Its items are unchanged, so nothing is in italic and there is nothing to project. The player has written an order that will do literally nothing.

  • The wasted order is recoverable, and only costs a hover.
  • Follows H2: the hover is where a BUY ALL explains itself.
  • Only reachable on hover, so a player scanning the table still sees nothing.
  • Depends on Q2 landing on H2 or H3; under H1 there is no hover line to put it in.

Q4 — two BUY ALL lines on one unit

Hunters again, 356 SILV, ordering buy all GRAI then buy all HORS. The first takes 342 of the 356; 14 is left, and a horse costs $60. The second order buys nothing — and that is the game’s answer, not a quirk: rules/buy gives no way to divide silver between two unbounded orders, so the earlier line simply wins.

For Sale: 30 grain [GRAI] at $18  ·  4 horses [HORS] at $60
IDNAMEORDERITEMSSILVER
*5512Huntersbuy all GRAI
buy all HORS		14 SILV, 3 SWOR, 19 GRAI14
hover, if Q2 lands on H2was: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR
Buys 19 grain — as many as its silver will pay for.
Buys no horses — 14 silver will not pay for one at $60.
  • Nothing to choose here if Q2 lands on H2 — one line per BUY ALL falls out of it, and the second line is what tells the player their second order is dead.
  • Under H1 this state is completely silent, which is the strongest argument against H1.
  • The SILVER column already resolves both lines this way today, so no arithmetic changes; only what is said about it.

Q5 — when a faction-mate took the rest of the line

Both our units order buy all GRAI and the market carries only 10 grain at $18. Both could afford more. rules/buy splits the line “in proportion to the amount each buyer attempted to buy”, and an unbounded order attempts everything there is — so the two attempts are equal and each gets 5. Neither ran out of silver, and neither is short because the market was small: they are short because of each other.

For Sale: 10 grain [GRAI] at $18
IDNAMEORDERITEMSSILVER
*5512Huntersbuy all GRAI266 SILV, 3 SWOR, 5 GRAI266
*5518Farmhandsbuy all GRAI110 SILV, 2 LEAT, 5 GRAI110

M1Two causes, one sentence

The market cap and the faction-mate cap read the same. Only two sentences ever exist.

hoverwas: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR
Buys 5 grain — all the market will sell it.
  • Two strings in total, both already needed for Q2.
  • Not quite true: the market would sell it 10. The reason it gets 5 is the unit two rows down, and the sentence hides that.

M2A third sentence for the split

When another of your own units contended for the same line, the sentence says so.

hoverwas: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR
Buys 5 grain — the market has 10 and your units in this region are competing for it.
  • Names the thing the player can actually do something about: they wrote both orders.
  • Matches what the region already warns about — an oversubscribed market line has its own shipped finding (ah-t2pn.4).
  • A third string, and a third branch to decide between.

M3Say the share, not the cause

One sentence covering both caps, stating the arithmetic instead of naming a culprit.

hoverwas: 356 SILV, 3 SWOR
Buys 5 grain — its share of the 10 this market has.
  • One string for both market cases, and true in each.
  • “Its share” needs the player to already know the line is split, which is the very thing they may have missed.
Recommended: H2, then Z2, then M2. H2 is the only option that has anything to say in Q3 and Q4, which are the states where a player has written an order that will not do what they think. Z2 then follows from it at no extra cost. If H1 is chosen, Q3, Q4 and Q5 have no answer at all and a wasted BUY ALL stays invisible. M2 is recommended because the one cap the player can act on is the one they caused themselves.
