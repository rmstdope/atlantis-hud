Option R — silver that comes in counts, silver that goes out is spending

Recommended. What a unit or a hex can have is the silver it holds in the report, plus every CLAIM, GIVE or TAKE coming in from outside. Silver handed out is part of what its orders spend. One rule for both sentences.

Every case keeps the same shortfall. Only the two figures beside it move. The wording does not change.

CaseA unit that takes silver

Traders (120) holds $40, writes TAKE FROM 88 30 SILV, then buys goods costing $100. It ends up $30 short.

Today
short $30: this unit can have $40 and its orders spend $70
Option R changes
short $30: this unit can have $70 and its orders spend $100
The $30 taken now counts, the same way a gift received already does. TAKE works just like GIVE (rules/take).

CaseA unit that gives silver away

Traders (120) holds $100, writes GIVE 89 60 SILV, then buys goods costing $100. It ends up $60 short.

Today
short $60: this unit can have $100 and its orders spend $160
Option R same
short $60: this unit can have $100 and its orders spend $160
No change: a gift handed out is still part of what its orders spend, as already agreed.

CaseA sharing hex where one unit gives silver to a unit outside it

Sharing units Smith (201) holds $100 and Miller (202) holds $50. Miller writes GIVE 300 60 SILV to a unit that is not counted with them. Their orders cost $250. They end up $160 short.

Today
the units in this hex are short $160 between them: they can have $90 and their orders spend $250
Option R changes
the units in this hex are short $160 between them: they can have $150 and their orders spend $310
Miller's $60 gift now joins the spending instead of being taken off what they can have, matching the single-unit sentence.

Not affectedEverything else

Units that take or give nothing, the upkeep-only hex warning, and every warning about items other than silver read exactly as today.
