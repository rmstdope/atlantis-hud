Peeking at a dossier row's hex — move the map, then move it back

The faction dossier rings the hex under the hovered row, but nothing moves the map. So the ring can be drawn off screen, or — the case you hit — underneath the dossier itself, which opens beside the row and therefore over the map.

Worth knowing: the "do not pan" behaviour was chosen, not overlooked. MapCanvas's own comment reads "Deliberately does NOT frame or pan the map, unlike arrow: running down a list while the map jumps under you is worse than no highlight at all." You have now met the other side of that trade, so this bead reverses it — but the worry behind it is real, and it is what sections 1 and 2 are asking about.

The machinery already exists: centreOn, isOffScreen, and a travel-and-restore effect used by the trade arrow whose own comment says "written once per gesture, not once per row — sweeping down the list must return to where the reader was before the first hover."

2 · How quickly, and how smoothly?

There is no easing anywhere in the map today — every move is instant. The only animation is the selection pulse, which honours prefers-reduced-motion.

iInstant, after a short pause

The map jumps, but only once the pointer has rested on a row for a moment (HOVER_DELAY_MS is already 300ms for tooltips).
  • Matches every other map move in the application
  • Sweeping down ten rows moves nothing — only a row you stop on
  • A jump is still a jump; you have to re-find your bearings

iiEased glide, after a short pause

The map slides over ~200ms so the eye can follow where it went.
  • Much easier to keep your bearings — you see the journey
  • The first animated map movement in the app; needs a reduced-motion opt-out
  • A quick scan can outrun the animation

iiiInstant, no pause

Moves the moment a row is hovered.
  • Most responsive
  • Crossing rows on the way to the one you want drags the map through every hex in between

3 · What happens on the keyboard?

Every row is a button and the dossier is tabbed through; the ring already follows focus as well as the pointer. But focus is not a peek — it is how a keyboard reader navigates.

iFocus peeks and returns, like hover

Tabbing to a row moves the map; tabbing away puts it back.
  • One rule for both paths — nothing extra to explain or test
  • A keyboard reader tabbing down the list gets the map yo-yoing back after every row

iiFocus moves and stays

Tabbing to a row moves the map and leaves it there; only pointer hover restores.
  • Treats keyboard as navigation, which is what it is — the bead itself suggests this
  • Two rules, and a reader who both tabs and hovers meets both
