A study that teaches nothing — costs nothing

Unit 8573 “Drones”: 60 gnolls at combat 5 (450), holding 900 silver, ordered STUDY COMB. No gnoll may take combat past level 5 (data/GNOL, rules/skills_limitations), so the month teaches this unit nothing. We already say that on the Skills hover and on the order line. Until now the Silver forecast still took $10 a head off all sixty of them — $600 — and said nothing about it.

Chosen. The fee comes off: a study that cannot raise the level is forecast as costing nothing, so the studied line simply is not there. A dim note under the silver list says why, leading with the money: “No study fee: no gnoll may take combat past level 5, so this month costs nothing.” The money is mentioned only there — the Skills sentence and the order-line warning are left exactly as they are. Rejected: keeping the charge and naming the waste; showing a range because the game's own behaviour is unestablished; a kept studied 0 row; an amber line; and two other wordings.

The bet being taken, stated plainly. The rules say what a study costs and where a race stops; they never say what the server does when you order both at once. This forecast assumes the study is not performed and not billed. If that turns out to be wrong, one unit's month-end figure is high by its fee, and the sentence above is where the next reader will find the assumption.

The two forms of the sentence

A RACE STOPS IT

No study fee: no gnoll may take combat past level 5, so this month costs nothing.
  • Several races in one unit are named as the shipped ceiling sentence names them, the least generous stopping the whole unit.

THE SKILL’S OWN CEILING

No study fee: combat stops at level 5 for this unit, so this month costs nothing.
  • Used where no race is to blame — naming one would blame it for a limit it did not impose.
