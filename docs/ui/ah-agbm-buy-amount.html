Q1 — how much a BUY puts in the ITEMS column

The ITEMS column already shows a pending GIVE in italic brass: the holding the unit will have once the order runs. This bead extends that to BUY, SELL, WITHDRAW and TAKE. The first question is what number a BUY contributes when the market cannot fill the order.

The situation on this page. The region's market states For Sale: 12 grain [GRAI] at $18. Two of our units in that hex both order grain — 20 and 10 — so 30 is asked of a line that carries 12. The rules split an oversubscribed line in proportion to what each unit tried to buy, so Hunters get 8 and Farmhands 4. The Silver column already settles it exactly this way and charges each unit for what it actually gets.

Option A — the settled figure: what the unit will actually hold

The column shows the outcome the market will produce. It is the same settlement the Silver column already charges against, so the two columns tell one story.

IDNAMEORDERITEMSSILVER
*5512Huntersbuy 20 GRAI356 SILV, 3 SWOR, 8 GRAI356
*5518Farmhandsbuy 10 GRAI78 SILV, 2 LEAT, 4 GRAI78
  • The ITEMS column and the Silver column cannot disagree: both read the one settlement.
  • The player sees the real consequence of two of their own units competing for one line.
  • The column shows 8 where the order says 20, and nothing on the row says why. The hover (Q5, next round) is where that would be said.

Option B — the asked figure: what the order says

The column takes the order at its word and shows the full quantity.

IDNAMEORDERITEMSSILVER
*5512Huntersbuy 20 GRAI140 SILV, 3 SWOR, 20 GRAI356
*5518Farmhandsbuy 10 GRAI30 SILV, 2 LEAT, 10 GRAI78
  • The column reads exactly like the order the player typed.
  • The two columns now contradict each other on the same row — ITEMS says 140 silver left, SILVER says 356. Both are on screen at once.
  • It shows grain the unit will not have, in the one place the player looks to check their orders did what they meant.

Option C — refuse: a BUY the market cannot fill projects nothing

The cell falls back to what the report says, upright, exactly as an unreadable order does today.

IDNAMEORDERITEMSSILVER
*5512Huntersbuy 20 GRAI500 SILV, 3 SWOR356
*5518Farmhandsbuy 10 GRAI150 SILV, 2 LEAT78
  • Never shows a figure that is not certain.
  • An oversubscribed line is the ordinary case whenever two of your units buy the same goods, so this would leave the column blind most of the time.
  • The Silver column moves and the ITEMS column does not, which reads as a defect.
