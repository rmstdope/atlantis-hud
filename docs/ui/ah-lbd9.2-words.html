ah-lbd9.2 — round 2: what the header control actually says

Settled in round 1: a header control (A), re-prompt in place when a session runs out (E1), one session per game (S2), faction number prefilled, our own sentence on a refusal. What is left is the text on the control itself. It sits in the right-hand group beside Send, and the header is already crowded, so its width is not free.

Chosen (2026-09-04): W2 signed out — Sign in to Arcanum; N1 signed in — the faction’s name, Merchant Guild ▾; and every string in the last table as written, including no new keyboard shortcut.

The control, signed out

“Sign in” alone is two words and no subject — in a header that also has Import, Export and Send, it may read as signing in to Atlantis HUD rather than to a game world.

W1Sign in

ImportExport ▾ Sign inSend⚙
  • Shortest, and the header has no room to spare.
  • Says nothing about what you sign in to.

W2Sign in to Arcanum

ImportExport ▾ Sign in to ArcanumSend⚙
  • Unambiguous, and names the world you are actually playing.
  • The widest of the three, and it grows with the world’s name.

W3Arcanum ▾ (always a chip)

ImportExport ▾ Arcanum ▾Send⚙
Not signed in to New Age: Arcanum.
Signing in lets Atlantis HUD fetch this turn and send your orders.
Sign in…
  • One control that never changes width or meaning; signed in or out is the popover’s job.
  • Two clicks to reach the dialog, every time.

The control, signed in

The same control, once there is a faction behind it. In all three the popover is where signing out happens.

N1The faction’s name

ImportExport ▾ Merchant Guild ▾Send⚙

Reads as an answer to “who am I signed in as?”. But the header already has a faction chip on the left showing the report’s faction, and the two are usually the same name twice.

N2The world’s name

ImportExport ▾ Arcanum ▾Send⚙

Brass means signed in, plain edge means not. No duplication with the faction chip, and the width never moves. The faction’s name is one click away in the popover.

N3Signed in

ImportExport ▾ Signed in ▾Send⚙

Says the state and nothing else. Fixed width, but it is the least informative of the three and reads oddly next to Import and Export, which are verbs.
