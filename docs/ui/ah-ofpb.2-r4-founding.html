R4 — founding a structure, and a kind nothing can price

Everything so far has been a unit working on a structure that already exists, which the report gives a label and a remaining need. BUILD [name] is the other half of the order and it has neither: the structure does not exist yet, so there is nothing for N1 to name and nothing printing how much is left to do.

The catalogue does have the two numbers that matter — a Tower costs 10 and is built from stone; a Stockade costs 60 and is built from wood — so the arithmetic is the same as everywhere else, with the full cost standing in for the remaining need. What is missing is only the words.

And 22 of the data page’s 58 structures state no build requirement at all — a Shaft, a Lair, a Ruin, a Gateway. Nor does the page know a structure the player has misspelt. Q G is what those rows do.

unit 5561 ; Masons, 10 humans, 120 stone, building 3 build Tower ; a Tower is 10 stone; its men could do 30 unit 5562 ; Carpenters, 10 humans, 120 wood, building 3 build Stockade ; a Stockade is 60 wood; its men could do 30, and it has the wood unit 5563 ; Prospectors, 10 humans, 120 stone, building 3 build Shaft ; the data page names no cost, no material and no skill for a Shaft unit 5564 ; Dreamers, 10 humans, 120 wood, building 3 build Barn ; there is no such structure in the game

F — the words for a build that founds something new

Unit 5562 is the plain case: 30 units of work, 30 wood, nothing capped. Unit 5561 is capped by the structure’s own cost — a Tower is finished in ten.

F1 — “a new <kind>” wherever a label would have gonerecommended

unit 5562 — nothing cappedwas: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Spends 30 WOOD on a new Stockade this month.
unit 5561 — the Tower is finished in tenwas: 10 HUMN, 120 STON Spends 10 STON on a new Tower this month. A new Tower needs 10 units of work, not the 30 its men could do.
  • One substitution and both agreed sentences keep working unchanged, cap sentence included.
  • “A new Tower” is exactly what the player wrote and exactly what will appear next month.
  • The cap sentence reads a little oddly for a structure that has had no work done on it — “needs 10” is its whole cost, not a remainder.

F2 — a sentence of its own for founding

unit 5562was: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Spends 30 WOOD starting a Stockade this month.
unit 5561was: 10 HUMN, 120 STON Spends 10 STON starting a Tower this month, which is all a Tower costs.
  • “Starting” says the thing that is different about this order.
  • The capped form reads naturally, because it never pretends to be a remainder.
  • Two more strings, and a second cap form, for a case that is arithmetically identical to the others.
  • A BUILD Tower written next to an unfinished Tower of the same type continues that one rather than starting a second (rules/build, the COMPLETE forms) — so “starting” can be wrong where “a new Tower” is merely imprecise.

F3 — name only the kind, with no article

unit 5562was: 10 HUMN, 120 WOOD Spends 30 WOOD on Stockade this month.
unit 5561was: 10 HUMN, 120 STON Spends 10 STON on Tower this month. Tower needs 10 units of work, not the 30 its men could do.
  • Identical in form to the existing-structure sentence, with nothing added at all.
  • Reads as though a structure called “Tower” already exists, which is the one thing this case must not imply.

G — a structure the catalogue cannot price

Unit 5563 names a real structure the data page states no cost, material or skill for. Unit 5564 names one that does not exist. Neither can be given a figure.

  • One rule with no exceptions to remember: a build the column cannot put a figure to is marked, whatever the reason.
  • Identical to what a wood-or-stone fork and an unfinished ship already get.
  • A misspelt structure will very likely be an error the engine rejects outright, so the mark spends the player’s attention on a row that will do nothing at all.

G2 — leave both alone, upright and unmarked

  • Neither order will move anything the engine recognises, so an unchanged list is arguably the truth.
  • Indistinguishable from a unit given no orders, on a row whose orders are almost certainly a mistake.
  • A rule with an exception in it: every other unpriceable build is marked, these two are not.
