ah-lyg6.2.3 — choosing next turn's study: where the control lives

The pane is the one you chose in ah-lyg6.2.2 — the mage list beside a mage detail, grouped by faction, your own first. It is read-only today. This bead makes it a planner: each mage gains next turn's STUDY — a skill, and optionally the level it is being studied towards — and a free-text note saying where his studies are heading. Both are kept in the storage that merged as ah-lyg6.2.1, so they survive a reload and are there for ah-lyg6.4 to export grouped by faction.

The game is the same fiction the 2.2 mockups used: your faction Wardens of the North (12) on turn 23, an ally Borg (21) level with you, an ally Selado (34) whose newest sheet is turn 20. Skill names are drawn lower case, verbatim from the ruleset, as the magic tree draws them — that was settled in 2.2.

Question 1 of this round: which of A, B or C. The other three questions are in the second file, ah-lyg6.2.3-detail.html.

Variant A — the chips are the picker

Nothing new is added to the detail. The chips already on screen under Knows and Can study now become choosable: click one (or press Enter on it) and it is next turn's study. The chosen chip is ringed, and one line at the top of the detail says what the plan now is.

A Choose by ringing a chip

The pane gains one line and one note box, and no new control at all. What a mage may study is already drawn; A says the drawing is the menu.

Variant B — a Next turn block, and the chips stay facts

A block at the top of the detail owns the plan: a skill menu, a level, a Clear button and the note. The chips below it are untouched — they stay the read-only picture ah-lyg6.2.2 landed.

B One block that owns the plan

The menu lists what he may study, in two groups — the skills he already holds and could raise, then the ones he could begin. Everything about the plan is in one rectangle, and Tab walks it: skill, level, note.

Variant C — the block, and the chips are a shortcut into it

B's block, plus A's clickable chips: clicking a chip fills the menu rather than being the only way to choose. The menu is the truth; a chip is a fast route to it.

C Both, with the block as the truth

Nothing else about the pane changes

F4 still opens it and still closes it. Esc still closes it and returns focus. The arrow keys still walk the list — but only while the list itself has focus; inside the note box an arrow key moves the cursor, as it must.

The groups, the headings, the staleness tint, the Knows / Can study now / Held back sections and every string in them are exactly what ah-lyg6.2.2 lands. This bead adds the plan and nothing else.

Teaching, and the warnings about hexes and seats, are ah-lyg6.3. Turning these plans into order lines you can paste or save is ah-lyg6.4. The STUDY force 4 line drawn in each block above is a preview of what ah-lyg6.4 will export, not an export.
