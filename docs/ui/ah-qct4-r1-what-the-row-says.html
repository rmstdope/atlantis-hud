PRODUCE after a GIVE — what the row says when the men have gone

What the code actually does — read, not guessed. The ITEMS cell’s production comes from semantics::produce (semantics.rs:4060), which prices the order as price_production(recipe, actor.unit.men, &actor.unit.items) — the report’s headcount, before this month’s orders. The SILVER cell on the same row prices the same order from facts.late().men (silver.rs:1140), which does follow this month’s GIVE and BUY — that was ah-dxfd.2’s work. So the two cells of one row already disagree about one order, and the ITEMS one is the stale half.
The sequencing itself is not a design question. rules/sequenceofevents puts “Give orders. GIVE and TAKE orders are processed” nine phases before “Primary PRODUCE orders… are processed”, and rules/tableiteminfo pays production in man-months, so a unit with no men makes nothing. Your symptom 2 is simply a defect: MinersA gives away all eight orcs and is still credited +8 iron. The seam to read it correctly already exists — Ordered::early_men() (semantics.rs:2299), which TAX already uses for exactly this reason. What is yours to decide is what the interface says once the number becomes nothing.
Your symptom 3, and why it is not the same bug. rules/give defines the ITEMS class as “the combination of all of the previous categories” — and MAN/MEN is one of those categories. So GIVE 14723 ALL ITEMS hands over the orcs as well as the iron, and the unit that is left has nobody to mine with. Under the rules it produces nothing, and the month’s iron does not stay behind. The app already reads the class that way (class_tags, semantics.rs:3682; tags_moved, effects.rs:1263). If you meant “give the iron and keep the men”, that is GIVE 14723 ALL IRON — and Q3 below is about exactly that case, which is the one where the month’s production genuinely should stay.

Q1 The row when the men leave and production falls to nothing

MinersA (5105): 8 orcs, mining 5, 20 iron. Orders GIVE 14723 ALL MEN and PRODUCE IRON. After the fix it makes nothing. Today’s row would simply stop mentioning production — no sentence, no mark. Iron 20 is unchanged, MEN drops to 0, and nothing anywhere says why the iron did not grow.

  • A new finding code, a new entry in codes::ALL, a new warning toggle and its generated TS — roughly doubles the bead.

  • Belt and braces: seen while writing, and explained when hovered.
  • Two surfaces saying one thing, which this codebase has twice had to pull back apart.

Q2 The partial case — some of the men leave

MinersA gives 5 of its 8 orcs away and still orders PRODUCE IRON. It makes 3, not 8. The existing cap sentence “This unit has materials for 5 swords, not the 8 its men could make.” does not fire here: nothing capped the run — the men themselves are simply fewer.

Q2·A — say nothing beyond the number
Items hoverwas: 8 ORC, 4 ADMT, 20 IRON
Includes 3 IRON this unit will produce. Production resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month.
  • The MEN cell says 3 in italics with was: 8; the arithmetic is then obvious.
  • Silent about the one thing that surprised you: that a GIVE written above changed a PRODUCE below.
Q2·B — name the men as the limit   symmetrical with the cap sentence
Items hoverwas: 8 ORC, 4 ADMT, 20 IRON
Includes 3 IRON this unit will produce. Production resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month.
This unit has men for 3 iron, not the 8 the report’s men could make.
  • Exactly the shipped capSentence shape with one more ProductionCap variant — “This unit has {men} for {N}, not the {W} its men could make.”
  • “has men for … not the … its men could make” says men twice and reads oddly. Needs its own wording rather than the template’s.
Q2·C — one sentence, its own words
Items hoverwas: 8 ORC, 4 ADMT, 20 IRON
Includes 3 IRON this unit will produce. Production resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month.
Its men leave before production resolves: 3 iron rather than the 8 the report’s men would have made.
  • Says the cause and the sequencing in one line, and covers Q1 too when the number is nought.
  • A fourth sentence shape in a hover that already has eight.

Q3 The other direction — the goods go, the men stay

Your symptom 3, written the way that keeps the men: GIVE 14723 ALL IRON and PRODUCE IRON. The 20 iron leaves in the Give phase; the 8 the men mine arrive in the last phase and stay. This is the case you expected, and it should read as a gain from nothing.

  • Two sentences about one tag in one hover.

A gift of a class the catalogue cannot classify (ADVANCED, MAGIC, SPECIAL) leaves the app unable to say what the unit still holds — Ordered::holdings_unknown(). Its production has to read something.
