ah‑ty3s.3 — the row of a new 1 that gains nobody

You chose K2 on 5 Sep: a formed unit's row stays on the units table while its FORM block stands, so new 1 never disappears from under you while you are writing its orders. That decision is settled and I am not reopening it. What is open is what such a row says.

The awkwardness is real and it is the whole of this bead. rules/form: “If no recruits are gained at all, the empty unit will be dissolved, and the silver and any other items it was given will revert to the first unit you have in that region.” So the row you asked to keep is a row for a unit the game will not create. Every other row on that table is a prediction that comes true. This one is a placeholder for something you are still building.

The example throughout. Hex plain (7,53). Unit 1922 Tax Collector holds $540 and its orders read FORM 1 / GIVE NEW 1 200 SILV / END — so new 1 has 200 silver, no men, and no BUY of peasants. Next month it is dissolved and the 200 goes back to 1922.

My recommendation is R2. The whole reason you kept the row is that you are mid-edit on it — and mid-edit is exactly when the useful thing to say is what is missing, not that something is doomed. R2 is also the only one of the three that answers the objection I put against K2 in round 4: the table stops asserting a unit it does not believe in. R1 is the cheapest and I would not fight hard for R2 over it if you find the marker noisy. R3 I would not take: a new visual word for one row, saying less than R2 says.

Where the 200 went belongs in the row's hover, which I will write as a sentence — and if you take R2, the marker beside the name has already told you the row is not real. I would take S3 only if you want the reversion visible without hovering, and I think it costs the column too much.

Question 3 — and in All my units?

A departing row is dimmed in This hex and not in All my units — that was ah‑tguk, on the grounds that in a roster of everything you own nothing is leaving anything, so a dimmed row there reads as a unit being lost. A dissolving unit is genuinely being lost, in both lists. So the question is whether this new treatment follows the departing rule or overrides it.

A1 The same in both lists

Dimmed and marked wherever it appears. The claim: “this will not exist” is true of the unit itself, not of a hex you happen to be looking at.

My recommendation is A1. ah‑tguk's reason for holding the dimming back was that a departing unit is not lost — it is somewhere else next month, and the roster should say so. A dissolving unit is not somewhere else. The premise does not carry over, so I would rather dim it in both than keep a rule whose reason has gone.

What I am deciding myself either way

Say the word on any of these and I will change it; otherwise they go into the plan as mine.

  • The row's hover gains one sentence: “Gains no recruits, so the game dissolves it and its 200 silver reverts to Tax Collector (1922).” — and, with no own unit shown in the hex, “Gains no recruits, so the game dissolves it. No unit of yours is shown in this hex for its goods to revert to.”
  • The screen reader hears the marker as words, like every other marker on this table: “dissolves, no recruits”.
  • The hex header's unit count includes the row, because the row is on the table and a count that disagreed with what you can see would be worse than one that counts something doomed.
  • The row is selectable, sortable and filterable exactly like any other — nothing about it is special-cased outside the marker, the dim and the Silver cell.
  • Reversed after the mockup went out. I had written that the ⚠ silver warning would never appear on a dissolving row. It stays. Reading settle_headcounts showed that a formed unit’s men are derived from its item list after the market settles, so a BUY 2 PEAS the unit cannot pay for is one of the two ways a unit gains nobody — and there the ⚠ is the explanation, not noise. Suppressing it would hide the cause of the very dissolve the row is announcing. The warned row shows ⚠ with the dash in place of the figure.
