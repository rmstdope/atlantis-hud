ah-lyg6.2.3 — the schedule: mages down, turns across

A second view inside the study planner. The pane you chose in ah-lyg6.2.2 becomes All mages — one mage at a time, in depth — and this is Schedule: every mage as a row, every coming turn as a column, so you can see at a glance where the whole faction's magic is heading. The two are one F4 pane with a switch at the top; the mage list and its detail are not thrown away.

Same fiction as before: your faction Wardens of the North (12), turn 23 just read, so the first planned column is turn 24. An ally Borg (21) is level with you; an ally Selado (34) last sent a sheet on turn 20.

The arithmetic, so the numbers below can be checked. rules/skills_studying: level 1 costs one month, level 2 two more, level 3 three more, and so on — and study months need not be consecutive. rules/skills_teaching: "A unit with a teacher can learn up to twice as fast as normal", so a taught month is worth two. Reports count study in points, and every fixture we hold agrees on 30 points to a studied month, which puts the levels at 30 / 90 / 180 / 300 / 450. That inference is the one soft number here and it is the same one ah-lyg6.2.2 already leans on for its stale-mage estimate.

Three variants, and the real question is whether a mage's plan is one goal or a queue of them. G1 keeps the single plan that storage holds today and merely draws its consequences. G2 and G3 make a plan a sequence, which is more than this bead was filed for and would need the stored row to grow — worth knowing before you pick.

G1 — the schedule is a picture of one goal per mage

Each mage still has exactly one plan: a skill, and the level he is studying towards (STUDY force 5). The grid is what that means turn by turn. Once he arrives, the rest of his row is empty — which is the honest picture, and also the argument for G2 and G3.

G1 Read-only, derived from the plan you already made

Nothing is edited here. Clicking a cell takes you to All mages with that mage picked, which is where the plan is changed. Storage keeps the one row per mage it holds today.

  • Nothing new is stored. The plan is the one row per mage that already merged.
  • The whole faction's magic on one screen, and the turn each level lands on it.
  • A tinted cell is a level gained that turn; a plain one is a month of study that changes no level.
  • ×2 marks a turn a teacher doubles — ah-lyg6.3's work, drawn here so the column means the same thing later.
  • Every row runs out. Once Sable reaches force 3 on turn 28 there is nothing to draw, and the planner cannot say what he does next.
  • You cannot plan force, then pattern at all, which is the ordinary shape of a mage's career.

G2 — the grid is the planner: click a cell, and the plan flows from it

A mage's plan becomes an ordered queue of goals. Clicking any cell asks what he starts studying from that turn on; the cells after it fill themselves in until that goal is reached, and the next goal takes over from there. A cell you have not touched is a consequence; a cell you set is a decision, and is marked.

G2 Drawn mid-edit: turn 27 of Ereb's row has just been clicked

Ereb was heading for force 5. Dropping pattern → 3 on turn 27 truncates the force goal at force 4 and reflows everything to its right. The old tail is drawn ghosted underneath while the menu is open, so you can see what you are about to overwrite.

  • The plan you can think — force to 4, then pattern to 3 — is the plan you can write.
  • You edit where you are looking; no switching views to change one mage.
  • An empty cell is a +: an invitation rather than a dead end.

G3 — the goals live in a column, the grid is their consequence

The same queue as G2, but it is edited in one place: a Plan column at the left, holding each mage's goals in order as small chips. The grid to its right is read-only and simply shows what those goals mean turn by turn.

G3 One column you edit, one grid you read

A chip is skill → level. Click one to change it, its × to drop it, and the dashed chip to add another to the end. The grid never has a menu open over it.

Things I have assumed, and would rather you overruled now than later

  • Six turn columns, starting with the next turn. The grid scrolls sideways if you want more; how far ahead it should go is a number, and yours if you want it different.
  • Your own faction first, then allies, exactly the grouping and order the All mages list uses.
  • An ally's stale mage is projected from his sheet's turn, so his row is longer-guessed than yours. The row is tinted, as its list row is.
  • A cell says the level he ends the turn with, not the one he starts it with.
  • Money is not shown. Magic study costs $100 a month (rules/skills_studying), so six mages studying for six turns is $3,600 — a column or a footer could say so, but I have left it out.
