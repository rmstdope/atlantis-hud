ah-ofpb.4 — round 2: the words, and the edges

Round 1 is settled: Q1 B — a cast makes what the mage can pay for, and a cast that can make nothing keeps a warning. Q2 B — a remainder chance is charged as a whole item, so the level 3 runesmith's Out is $1,800. Q3 A — a mage that can create none of it is charged nothing. What is left is the wording, which ships exactly as written here, and three edges the first round's examples did not reach.

R1 — the sentence under the Silver hover, when a cast is capped

PRODUCE already has this sentence, and it ships today as “This unit has materials for 5 swords, not the 8 its men could make.” A cast is capped by the same two things, but by the mage's level rather than by its men. All three read the Amuletsmith ($400, 3 amulets at $200) and then the Swordwright (4 swords, 15 possible).

V1 — the PRODUCE sentence, with “its level” for “its men”recommended

8901 AmuletsmithThis unit has silver for 2 amulets of protection, not the 3 its level could make.
8902 SwordwrightThis unit has materials for 4 mithril swords, not the 15 its level could make.
  • One sentence for both orders: productionCapSentence is already exported and already read by two hovers, so nothing is copied.
  • A player who has met it on a PRODUCE recognises it instantly on a CAST.
  • “materials” for a single sword is a slightly grand word, and it is what PRODUCE says today.

V2 — name the cast

8901 AmuletsmithThis cast makes 2 amulets of protection, not the 3 its level could: it has silver for 2.
8902 SwordwrightThis cast makes 4 mithril swords, not the 15 its level could: it has 4 swords.
  • Says what the mage is short of, by name — “it has 4 swords” rather than “materials”.
  • A second sentence to keep in step with the PRODUCE one, and they will drift.
  • Longer, on a hover that already carries six rows and can carry several notes.

V3 — the short form

8901 AmuletsmithThis unit has silver for 2 of the 3 amulets of protection its level could make.
8902 SwordwrightThis unit has materials for 4 of the 15 mithril swords its level could make.
  • Shortest of the three, and both numbers sit beside each other where they are compared.
  • Changes the PRODUCE sentence too, or leaves two shapes in the app.

R2 — the cast that makes nothing at all

You chose to keep a warning here. There are two ways to say it, and they differ in more than words: one reuses the shipped codes and their messages, the other is a new warning with its own switch in Settings.

W1 — charge it for one, and let the shipped warnings firerecommended

The rule becomes “a cast is charged for what the mage can pay for, and never for fewer than one”. The mage is then short by exactly one item's cost, and the two warnings this app already ships say so in the words they already use.

A new code, cast-makes-nothing, with its own sentence and its own toggle in Settings beside the other warnings. The mage is charged nothing, so its Silver does not move.

R3 — the second number, when the spell is a chance

The Runesmith holds $1,000. Its level says two runeswords for certain and a 70% chance of a third; you chose to charge the pessimistic three, $1,800 — and it can pay for one. So the cap sentence has to name a number the mage “could make”, and there are three candidates. All three sit under the same figures: Out 600, At month end 400.

P1 — the number the charge usedThis unit has silver for 1 runesword, not the 3 its level could make.
P2 — the rangeThis unit has silver for 1 runesword, not the 2 to 3 its level could make.
P3 — what is certainThis unit has silver for 1 runesword, not the 2 its level makes for certain.
  • P1 agrees with the arithmetic above it: 3 is the number the Out row was computed from, so hover and figure cannot be read as disagreeing. It never mentions the chance.recommended
  • P2 is the only one that tells the truth about the spell, and it is the same range ah-ofpb.5 will print in Items (2-3 RUNE). It needs the sentence to hold two numbers where PRODUCE's holds one.
  • P3 reads best and quietly contradicts the figure: the reader is told 2 while $1,800 — three — is what the column charged.

R4 — silver that has not arrived yet

The Wanderer holds nothing and a neighbour is ordered to give it $600 this month, in time to be spent. Is the cap judged on what it holds now, or on what it will have? PRODUCE judges on what it holds now, and the committed turn has a unit in exactly this position — unit 12881 Carpenters, which shows zero catapults for want of silver it does not hold.

  • Y1 understates a mage that is about to be funded, and a player who set that gift up will think the app has not noticed it.
  • Y2 is truer for this unit and makes the two orders disagree — the same hex, the same gift, a PRODUCE capped and a CAST not.
  • Y2 also has an ordering problem the app has met before: what a unit can afford depends on everything else its month does, so a cast priced from money still on its way has to wait for the running total.
