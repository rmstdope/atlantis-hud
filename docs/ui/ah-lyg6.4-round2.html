T — which turn the tab covers

The Schedule plans six turns. The bead says "next turn's orders".

No control at all. The sub-line says which turn it is, and that is the only turn there is.

  • Nothing to get wrong: you cannot mail an ally turn 75's orders by accident.
  • No new control, and the tab is one press from the plan.

  • The whole plan is exportable, not only its first month.

O — your own faction's orders

The application already has an orders editor holding your real orders document.

O1 Clipboard and file only

Your own block behaves exactly like an ally's. You paste it into your orders yourself.
Borg TNG (95) — your faction, turn 72 CopySave…
  • One rule for every faction, and nothing writes into your orders behind your back.
  • The commonest case — your own mages — is the clumsiest.

O2 A third button on your own block

"Put into my orders" merges the lines into the orders document, under the right UNIT headings.
Borg TNG (95) — your faction, turn 72 Put into my orders CopySave…
  • The planner actually finishes the job for your own faction.
  • Much the biggest piece of work here: it must merge rather than append, replace a STUDY the unit already has, and be undoable.
  • It writes to a document you have been editing by hand — the one place in this application where that has never happened.

V — saving to a file

V1 Per faction only

Each section's own Save…, writing study-orders-Creeping-Death-turn-72.txt. No whole-document save.
  • A file you send is a file for one recipient — which is how it is actually used.
  • Four allies is four saves.

V2 Per faction, plus a Save all… in the tab header

The same, and one more button writing every section into study-orders-turn-72.txt as a single file.
  • One press for a record of the whole turn's magic plan.
  • A file holding every ally's orders is the one thing you must not send to any of them — and it is the easiest one to attach by mistake.
