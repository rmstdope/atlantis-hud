Question 4 the capped sentence, when materials are what ran out

The agreed note assumed silver was the binding constraint. Now it may be wood.

OptionThe sentences
A — name the constraint silver: “This unit has silver for 1 catapult, not the 2 its men could make.”
materials: “This unit has materials for 1 catapult, not the 2 its men could make.”
both: the silver one, since silver is what the column is about.
B — one sentence, name neither “This unit can make 1 catapult, not the 2 its men could.”  — one string, and the reader looks at their own holdings to see which ran out.
C — name the item that ran out “This unit is 250 wood short of the 2 catapults its men could make.”  — most actionable, and it duplicates the not-enough-items mark that now fires on the same unit.

Question 5 a unit that cannot produce what it ordered

produces already carries the level each recipe needs — carpenter 4 for a catapult. A unit ordered to produce one without the skill makes nothing, and today the client says nothing about that either.

OptionWhat shows
A — a new advisory “this unit has carpenter 2 and producing catapults needs carpenter 4”, as its own code beside study-at-maximum and the rest. Catches a wasted month, which is what the advisories are for. A new code is a new row in validate_real_orders's table and its own interview about wording.
B — expense 0, no mark The column shows the unit spending nothing, correctly, and nothing is said. Keeps this bead to costing. The wasted month is a separate bead, filed unranked.

Question 6 do the produced goods arrive?

Carpenters ends the month holding 2 catapults it did not have. If a later order gives them away, the ledger decides whether that is possible.

OptionWhat happens
A — credit the output GIVE 2 CATP after a PRODUCE is affordable, and no not-enough-items fires. Truthful about the month. But production resolves in the month's last phase, like wages — so goods made this month cannot be given away this month, and crediting them would silence a warning that should fire.
B — debit inputs, do not credit output The materials go; the catapults do not arrive in time to be spent. A GIVE of them is still short. Matches how the ledger already treats wages and takings from entertaining (ah-uwa3): earned this month, unusable this month.
