ah-ofra round 2 — the exact words

Settled in round 1: the map draws the owner's course and nothing else; when the owner gave no course the map is silent; when the app cannot tell who the owner is the map is silent too; and clicking a destination while a passenger is picked is declined rather than written to somebody else. Two amber lines and one refusal now need their wording.

The house pattern for these lines is subject, then evidence, then consequence — as in the one that already ships: Longship [329] is short of sailors: 4 sailing levels aboard, but 0 are helping with SAIL; it needs 4, so it will not sail.

The two situations, side by side same ship, same three units

Situation 1 — nobody who can steer said so

Sea Rovers (900) — no order the owner Deckhands (901) SAIL SE Marines (902) — no order

The ship does not move. Map silent.

Situation 2 — the owner was overruled by nobody

Sea Rovers (900) SAIL NE the owner Deckhands (901) SAIL SE Marines (902) — no order

The ship sails north-east. Map draws north-east.

Question 5 one message or two

Both situations have the same cause — somebody who does not own the ship wrote a course — and differ only in what happens next. They can be one sentence with a changing tail, or two sentences written for their own case.

A one line, two tails

Problems — situation 1
⚠Only Sea Rovers (900) can set the course of Longship [329]: Deckhands (901) ordered SAIL SE, and the owner ordered no course, so the ship will not sail.
Problems — situation 2
⚠Only Sea Rovers (900) can set the course of Longship [329]: Deckhands (901) ordered SAIL SE, and the owner ordered NE, so the ship sails NE.
  • One shape to learn: who can steer, who tried, what happens.
  • The two cases read as the same mistake, because they are.
  • Long. Both tails carry a clause the other case does not need.

B two lines, each written for its case

Problems — situation 1
⚠Longship [329] will not sail: only its owner, Sea Rovers (900), can set its course, and it ordered none.
Problems — situation 2
⚠Deckhands (901) cannot set the course of Longship [329] — only its owner, Sea Rovers (900), can. The ship sails NE.
  • Each line is shorter and leads with what matters in that case.
  • Two messages to keep true, and situation 1 never names the crewman who tried.
Q5. One sentence with a changing tail, or two sentences written for their own situations?

Question 6 how the owner is named

A player who has never met this rule will ask why is Sea Rovers the owner? The answer is that it is listed first under the ship. The line can say so, or leave it.

A just "its owner"

Problems
⚠…only its owner, Sea Rovers (900), can set its course…
  • Short, and "owner" is the game's own word for it.
  • Leaves a new player guessing how ownership was decided.

B "its owner — the first unit aboard"

Problems
⚠…only its owner, Sea Rovers (900), the first unit listed aboard, can set its course…
  • Teaches the rule at the exact moment it bites.
  • Six extra words on every occurrence, for ever.

C "the first unit aboard", no "owner" at all

Problems
⚠…only the first unit aboard, Sea Rovers (900), can set its course…
  • Says the testable thing and nothing else.
  • Drops the word the rules themselves use, so it reads as our invention.
Q6. "its owner", "its owner, the first unit listed aboard", or "the first unit aboard"?

Question 7 the owner cannot be identified

Round 1 settled that the map draws nothing here. The question left over is whether Problems says anything. Nobody has made a mistake — we simply cannot see who is aboard first — but a course was written and no line appears, which is exactly the silence a player would want explained.

A silence

Problems
nothing about this ship

Problems is for things the player got wrong. This is not one.

  • No warning fires on a perfectly correct set of orders.
  • A course written and no line drawn, with nothing to explain it.

B a line saying the app cannot tell

Problems
⚠Where Longship [329] sails cannot be worked out: the first unit aboard it is not one your report describes, so there is no telling whether SAIL SE will be obeyed.

Amber, like every other "the app cannot be sure" line.

  • The missing line on the map is accounted for.
  • Fires when nothing is wrong, on every turn the ship stays half-seen.
Q7. Owner unknown: silence, or an amber line saying the course cannot be worked out?

Question 8 the switch in Settings

Every order check in Atlantis HUD can be turned off, grouped under Order checks › Sailing. Two entries already live there. The new warnings need to join them — as one switch or two.

A one new switch

Order checks › Sailing
Overloaded fleets A fleet ordered to sail with more weight aboard than it can carry.
Undercrewed fleets A fleet ordered to sail without enough sailing skill aboard.
Courses set by the wrong unit A fleet given a course by a unit that does not own it.
  • One rule, one switch — both situations come from the same mistake.
  • Cannot keep the "will not sail" warning and drop the noisier one.

B two new switches

Order checks › Sailing
Overloaded fleets A fleet ordered to sail with more weight aboard than it can carry.
Undercrewed fleets A fleet ordered to sail without enough sailing skill aboard.
Fleets left with no course A fleet whose owner set no course, so it will not sail.
Courses that are overruled A unit sets a course for a fleet it does not own, and the owner's course wins.
  • The noisier one — a habit many players have — can be silenced on its own.
  • Four switches in one group, two of them near-identical to read.
Q8. One new switch for the whole rule, or two so the habitual case can be silenced separately?
