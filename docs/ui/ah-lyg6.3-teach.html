ah-lyg6.3 — teaching in the study planner

The F4 planner already plans STUDY: every mage a row, the next six turns the columns, and a queue of goals behind each row. This bead adds the other half of the month — TEACH — and the warnings that go with it. The question below is only how a teacher is written down; where the warnings appear is the second mockup.

Same fiction as ah-lyg6.2.3. You are Wardens of the North (12), turn 23 is read, so the first planned column is 24. Ereb (2431) and Sable (2517) stand together in a Fort in Ryehold; Kestrel (2688) is two hexes away in Dunmoor. Your ally Borg (21) has Uln (3012) and Vess (3104), both in Borg's own hex.

The rules this leans on, looked up rather than recalled. rules/skills_teaching: "A unit with a teacher can learn up to twice as fast as normal … The unit doing the teaching must have a skill level greater than the unit doing the studying … Each person can only teach up to 10 students in a month; additional students dilute the training … Only leaders may use the TEACH order." rules/teach: "TEACH [unit] … All units to be taught must have declared you Friendly." So a taught month is worth two, a teacher spends his whole month teaching and studies nothing, and both must stand in the same hex.

Three ways to say who teaches whom. They differ in what is stored, and therefore in what happens when a mage moves or you change a student's plan.

T1 — teach is a goal, and it names its students

Clicking a teacher's cell opens the same popover that sets a study, with a Teach group above the skills. You tick the mages he teaches. The choice is stored as a goal in his queue: { teach: ["2517"], turns: 2 }. Export writes TEACH 2517 verbatim.

T1 Turn 24 of Ereb's row, clicked

The list offers every mage the planner can see standing in Ereb's hex whose planned study Ereb outranks. A mage elsewhere, or one Ereb cannot outrank, is shown greyed with the reason — so you learn why he is not offered rather than wondering where he went.

  • The one place a teacher is written down, and the one place a month is spent.
  • Export is exact: TEACH 2517 for Ereb, STUDY force for Sable.
  • A student's ×2 is a consequence of the teacher's row, so the two can never disagree.
  • If Sable's plan changes to a skill Ereb cannot outrank, Ereb's stored goal is now wrong and must be warned about rather than silently dropped.
  • Two things to keep in step by hand: the teacher's list and the student's study.

T2 — teach is a goal, and it names a skill, not students

The goal is "teach force here": { teachSkill: "FORC", turns: 2 }. Who that turns out to be is worked out afresh every time the grid is drawn — every mage in his hex whose planned study that turn is force and whose level he beats. Export computes the unit list at export time.

T2 The same click, one field shorter

Nothing goes stale: change Sable's plan and Ereb simply teaches somebody else, or nobody. The cost is that you cannot say "teach Sable and not Kestrel" when both are here studying force.

T3 — the student names his teacher

Nothing changes on the teacher's row; you set it from the mage being taught. His goal grows a field: { skill: "FORC", targetLevel: 3, taughtBy: "2431" }. The teacher's TEACH cells are derived from every student who named him.

T3 Sable's turn-24 cell, clicked

Reads the way a player thinks about a student — "get Sable to force 3 fast" — and the teacher's month is filled in for him. It also means a teacher's row can be overwritten by somebody else's decision, which is the thing to weigh.
