Everything so far settles the line that buys nothing. There is one state left, and today's sentences are wrong in it too: a second line that buys less than it could afford because its own earlier line already took part of the stock. It is not dead, so no finding fires — but a sentence is shown, and as things stand it blames the market or a faction-mate for what the unit's own first line did.

Q1 · No competition — the whole line was the unit's, and its own first order took part of it

Line: 5 grain at $12, no faction-mate. Purse: 100 silver (enough for 8). Orders: BUY 3 grain then BUY ALL grain. The second line correctly buys the remaining 2. What does its sentence say?

as it would ship with no further change — today's "market" sentence

This market has 2 grain, not the 8 this unit's silver would buy.

False. This market has 5 grain. Two are left because the unit's own line above bought three, and the sentence hides that.

a — name the earlier order, keep the silver clause

This unit buys 2 grain: its earlier orders have taken the other 3 of the 5 this market has.

b — name what is left, in the shipped “market” cadence

This market has 2 grain left after this unit's earlier orders, not the 8 its silver would buy.

c — leave it alone

This market has 2 grain, not the 8 this unit's silver would buy.

Accept the inaccuracy. The numbers in the columns are right, and this is a sentence about a shape the player is about to delete anyway.

Q2 · With competition — a share of 4, and its own first order took 3 of it

Line: 5 grain. 2390's settled share is 4; a faction-mate holds 1. Purse: 100 silver. Orders: BUY 3 grain then BUY ALL grain, which correctly buys 1. Two facts are competing to be said: the faction-mate, and its own earlier line.

as it would ship with no further change — today's "shared" sentence

This unit gets 1 of the 5 grain this market has, because your units in this region are competing for it.

Half true. Competition cost it 1 of the 5; its own earlier line cost it the other 3, and only the competition is mentioned.

a — the earlier order wins, as in Q1a

This unit buys 1 grain: its earlier orders have taken the other 3 of the 4 it can have here.

b — say both

This unit gets 1 of the 4 grain it can have here, because your units in this region are competing for it — and its earlier orders have taken 3 of that.

c — leave it alone

This unit gets 1 of the 5 grain this market has, because your units in this region are competing for it.
