ah-lauy — round 2: the counts, the shared share, and which cause wins

Option B is chosen, and the two sentences are agreed in their simplest form:
  hover — “This unit buys no grain: an earlier order of its own has already bought all 5 this market has.”
  finding — “an earlier order of its own has already bought all 5 grain this market has, so this buys nothing”
Those words are exactly right for one case: the unit had the whole line to itself and its own first order took all of it. This page is the four cases where they are not.

Q1 · When the unit only ever had a share of the line

Two own units in the hex. The line is 5 grain; 2390's settled share is 3, its faction-mate holds the other 2. 2390 writes BUY ALL grain twice and is rich. The first line takes 3 — all it could ever have — and the second takes nothing. “all 5 this market has” is false here: this unit never had 5 to buy.

a — name the share

This unit buys no grain: an earlier order of its own has already bought all 3 it can have here.
nothing-left-to-buy an earlier order of its own has already bought all 3 grain it can have here, so this buys nothing

b — name the share and the competition

This unit buys no grain: an earlier order of its own has already bought all 3 of the 5 your units here are splitting.
nothing-left-to-buy an earlier order of its own has already bought all 3 grain of the 5 your units here are splitting, so this buys nothing

c — keep the shipped competition sentence, say nothing about the earlier line

This unit buys no grain: your other units in this region have claimed all 5 this market has.

Today's sentence, unchanged. Cheapest, and the only one of the three that does not tell the player which of their own lines to delete.

And the case that is not in doubt: 36 silver against a line of 5 — the first line buys 3 and stops for want of silver, the market still has 2. Nothing was bought twice, the shipped silver sentence stands, and no finding fires. Both options agree there.
