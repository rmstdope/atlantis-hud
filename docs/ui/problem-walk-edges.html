The problem walk — the awkward cases

Settled: a counter between the arrows in the Orders pane header, and the walk keeps its place by identity across a re-validation rather than jumping back to the start. What is left is what "keeps its place" means when the problem you were standing on is the one you just fixed, what the counter says when there is nothing to count, and what gives way when the window is narrow.

1 · You fixed the problem you were standing on

You were on problem 3 of 7. You fixed it. Validation re-runs and there are now six. Where does the next F8 take you?

K1The next one down the document

The walk remembers where it stood in the document. Its problem is gone, so it resumes at the first problem at or after that position — the one that would have come next anyway.

  • Fix one, step on, and you land exactly where you would have without the edit
  • Nothing is skipped and nothing is repeated
  • The most rules to write and test of the three

K2Keep the number

You were on 3, so you stay on 3 — of however many there now are. The next step goes to 4.

  • Simplest rule there is: one number, clamped
  • Skips one. Old 4 slid into slot 3, and stepping to 4 jumps straight past it
  • Only correct when the problem you fixed was before the one you are on

K3Back to the top, only in this case

Identity is kept whenever the problem still exists; when it has gone, the walk restarts — today's behaviour, kept as the fallback only.

  • No "nearest" rule to define at all
  • Fixing a problem is the one moment the walk restarts, which is backwards
  • On a long turn you re-step everything you already dealt with

2 · What the counter says when there is nothing to say

Three states have no position: before the first step, when the turn has no problems at all, and when the last problem is fixed while you are standing on it. The buttons stay enabled throughout — that is deliberate and already tested.

N1Nothing at all

The counter is absent until the walk has a position, and disappears again if it loses one. The header looks exactly as it does today until you step.

Before you step it shows how many there are — 7 — and gains the position once you are walking. With none at all it shows 0.

3 · A narrow window

The header holds the title, the unit hint, and now the walk. Something has to give first.

W1The unit hint truncates

The title and the walk are fixed; the — unit 4117 hint is the flexible one and ellipsises.

  • The controls stay reachable at every width, which is what matters for a control
  • The unit id is also shown in the unit pane, so it is not the only copy
  • A truncated unit id is worse than no unit id

W2The counter drops out

Below a width the counter is hidden and the arrows remain. The hint stays whole.
