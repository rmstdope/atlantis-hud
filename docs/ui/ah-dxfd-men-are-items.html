You asked three times why a gift of men cannot be resolved. It can. This page is me finally finding the proof, and what the bead becomes because of it. Everything I said about doubt in the earlier pages is withdrawn.

The app already computes exactly this

A unit's headcount is not something the report hands over separately. It is derived — the man-tagged items, counted. crates/core/src/report/composition.rs:51-73, shipped, tested, running on every report that loads:

fn classify_unit(unit: &mut ReportUnit, ruleset: &Ruleset, unknown: &mut BTreeSet<String>) {
    // ... refuse to classify at all if any tag is unrecognised ...

    unit.men_by_race = unit
        .items
        .iter()
        .filter(|item| ruleset.is_man(&item.tag))
        .cloned()
        .collect();
    unit.men = unit.men_by_race.iter().map(|race| race.amount).sum();
    unit.men_estimated = false;
}
GIVE 1234 ALL MEN is that same filter, applied to the giver's list. Ten HUMN and three LEAD go; thirty-five SWOR and six hundred SILV stay. Nothing is unknown, nothing is estimated, and no new machinery is needed — the rule is already written down, in the file that reads the report.

One rule, and it fixes everything at once

Men are the man-tagged items. Count them wherever the items are right.
Both places that track a unit through its orders already keep the item list right — the units-table walker (effects.rs) and the ledger (semantics.rs). Neither re-derives the headcount from it. The units-table walker adjusts men by hand inside the GIVE arm and nowhere else, which is why a TAKE today leaves a row saying 20 HUMN in ITEMS and 10 in MEN. Re-deriving instead of adjusting fixes GIVE, TAKE, ALL MEN and ALL ITEMS in one stroke, in both places, with the rule stated once.
