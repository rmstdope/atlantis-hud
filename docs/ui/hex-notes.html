Hex notes · where they are written, how they are drawn

Issue #261 asks for free-text notes on a hex — “Build a castle here” — each with a choice of whether it shows on the map or only in the region panel. The map is SVG; unit and building marks are painted by the five themes, but chrome that belongs to the reader — the selection ring, the province outline and its atlas label — is drawn once by the map for every theme. A note is the reader's, so it is drawn that way: one layer, one look in every theme, hidden with the rest of the reader's chrome by a badge. Three questions below, then the states the happy path hides.

Chosen (2026-08-15): A — the Notes section in the region panel is the only editor. On the map, a pin (from 1/2), which when clicked selects the hex and opens a stack of tags (round 2, a) showing every map-visible note on that hex; one open at a time, closed by a click elsewhere, Escape, or the pin again; keyboard users read the panel. The map-wide switch is a Badges menu entry, “Notes”. Many notes per hex, a new note starts on the map, stamped with the turn it was written on, multi-line up to 500 characters. Stored in the game's own store, like order drafts, so notes travel with a backup. All the words are as drawn.

Question 1Where a note is written and edited

Right-click on the map already means “centre here without selecting” (ah-t2i), so a context menu is not on offer. Both candidates put the editor in the region panel, which is where the selected hex is already described and which is keyboard-reachable; they differ in whether the map itself is also a way in.

AA Notes section in the region panel, and only there

chosen

A Notes section at the foot of the region panel — after Structures — lists the hex's notes. Each row is the text, a Map chip that says whether it is drawn on the map, and a remove button. Add note opens a textarea in place. Pins on the map are paint only: clicking one selects the hex, exactly as clicking the hex does, and the panel shows the notes.

Question 2How a map-visible note is drawn

Drawn by the map, once for every theme, screen-constant like a label, in the note colour (--note, a warm paper yellow that no theme uses for terrain or marks). All three keep the mark off the hex centre, where units and settlements sit. Shown at mid and near zoom; the far band hides every label and would make a 9px hex mostly pin, so far hides pins too — the same rule as unit marks. Each is drawn at mid zoom (left) and near zoom (right); the third hex on each has three notes. Outcome: none of the three as drawn — the pin from 1, opening into the tag from 3 on click; see round 2 below.

1Pin only

A small paper-note glyph in the hex's upper-right, with the count when a hex has more than one map-visible note. Hover shows the text (SVG <title>). The text is read in the panel.

  • Never overlaps a neighbour, at any zoom
  • Same mark at mid and near — nothing to learn twice
  • The note itself is one hover or one click away

2Pin at mid, pin and text at near

The same pin; at near zoom the first line of the note is set beneath the hex in the label style the settlement name uses, truncated to about 22 characters with an ellipsis. Several notes on a hex: the newest map-visible one is the label, the pin carries the count.

  • The note is read on the map when there is room to read it
  • Follows the settlement-name rule the player already knows: text only when near
  • A label under a hex that also has a settlement name — two labels stack (shown on the middle hex)

3Sticky tag

A small yellow tag with the note's first words, hung off the hex's north-east edge like a tab on an atlas, at mid and near alike; several notes fan as stacked tags.

  • The most legible from across the room; reads as a note, not a badge
  • At mid zoom the tag is wider than the hex and hides the neighbour to its NE
  • A second colour system on top of terrain and marks, in a solid block

Question 2, round 2A pin that expands into a tag when clicked

Chosen in round 1: the pin from 1/2, and clicking it opens the tag from 3. So a pin is the one clickable mark on the map (it gets its own hit target above the hex polygons; every other mark stays pointer-events: none). Below, the pin on (3,3) is open and the pin on (12,6) — three notes — is open too, in each of two ways of opening. Both maps at mid zoom; the tag is screen-constant, wraps at about 28 characters, and holds at most four lines before an ellipsis.

aThe tag shows every map-visible note on the hex, stacked

chosen

One tag per note, newest on top, each with its full text (wrapped). Three notes are three tags. The hex's whole story at one click; a hex with many notes grows a tall stack.

  • Everything on the map at once
  • A stack of tags covers the hexes to the NE

bThe tag shows the newest note, and says how many more

One tag: the newest map-visible note, full text, and a last line “+2 more in the panel” when there are others. The stack never grows; the panel is where the rest is read.

  • Bounded: one tag, four lines, whatever the hex holds
  • The second and third notes are one glance away, in the panel

What is not drawn and is asked with the question: whether clicking the pin also selects the hex (so the panel shows the notes at the same time); what closes the tag — a click anywhere else and Escape, or only a second click on the pin; whether more than one tag can be open at once; and whether keyboard users get a way to open a tag or read notes in the panel instead.

Question 3Where the map-wide switch lives

Every note has its own Map chip; this is the one switch that hides all pins at once, the way Regions hides the province outlines. Precedent (region-decorations.html) chose the Badges menu for Regions, over a chip.
