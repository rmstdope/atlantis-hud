A formed unit shows its own orders

Bead ah-ty3s, round 1. You reported: “When a new unit is formed and that unit is selected in the units pane, it shall show that new unit's order, not as today that it (partially marks and) displays the creators orders, i.e. the FORM x, etc. Updating the orders of the new unit shall of course fill in those orders in the creators FORM/END block.”

Why it does that today. Nothing is broken by accident — this is ah-jw85 decision C1, which you chose: the row's Id, its ⚠ and its Problems entry all select the forming unit, because “a unit that does not exist cannot be selected, and its orders are typed in its parent's block anyway”. The rejected alternative was C2 — the new unit is selectable like any unit — and its stated cost was exactly what you are now asking for: “a unit that does not exist becomes something the rest of the app can hold selected — the keyboard walk between units, the orders pane and the map marker all then meet it.” This bead is C2, done properly.

And it can already go further than you saw. The redirect is only on the mouse: arrowing onto a formed row (UnitTableDock.tsx:689) and the pin-focus jump (:494) both select the row's own new-1. The orders pane then finds no unit new-1 block, shows an empty editor — and the first keystroke writes a real unit new-1 block into the file, which the server would reject. Whatever you choose below, that stops happening.

The hex used throughout. Tax Collector (1922) in plain (7,53) holds 500, forms NEW 1, gives it 100, and the new unit buys five Plainsmen at 40. The document reads:

unit 1922
@tax
form 1
name unit "Recruits"
buy 5 plainsmen
study combat
end
give new 1 100 silv

Question 1 yours · What the orders pane shows for the new unit

The row new-1 is selected in the units pane. Three ways to put its four inner lines in front of you. In all three the text is spliced back between the form 1 and the end in unit 1922's block, which is your second sentence and is not in question.

A1 The inner lines alone

The editor holds exactly what sits between form 1 and end — the same relationship a unit's editor already has to its own unit 1922 header line, which it also does not show. The panel's hint says whose block it lands in.
Orders— new 1, in unit 1922's block
name unit "Recruits"
buy 5 plainsmen
study combat
  • Identical in shape to every other unit's editor: no line in it that cannot be typed over.
  • The indentation the file carries is the editor's own business, exactly as it is for a normal unit.
  • Nothing on screen shows the form 1 / end the lines live inside, so the file's real shape has to be remembered.

A2 The inner lines, framed by a read-only form/end

The same editable region, with the two lines that bound it shown above and below it, dimmed and not editable — context, not text. The caret cannot enter them and typing cannot delete them.
Orders— new 1, in unit 1922's block
form 1
name unit "Recruits"
buy 5 plainsmen
study combat
end
  • The file's real shape is on screen; nobody has to hold it in their head.
  • The alias is visible, which is what give new 1 elsewhere refers to.
  • A read-only line inside a text editor is a thing this editor has never had — select-all, copy, the problem walk and Order OCD's indent pass all have to be taught about it.
  • Two lines of the five are furniture, in a pane that is often four lines tall.

A3 The whole creator's block, with the form block banded

Today's text, made deliberate: selecting new-1 keeps unit 1922's block in the editor and highlights the form 1…end run, putting the caret on its first line.
Orders— unit 1922, at new 1
@tax
form 1
name unit "Recruits"
buy 5 plainsmen
study combat
end
give new 1 100 silv
  • Nothing new to build in the editor; the whole file is reachable from wherever you stand.
  • The give new 1 100 silv that pays for the purchase is on screen beside it.
  • This is what you asked to be rid of: the pane says new 1 and shows another unit's orders.
  • Selecting 1922 and selecting new 1 give the same editable text, so the two selections stop meaning different things.

Question 2 yours · What unit 1922's own editor then shows

Whatever Question 1 decides, the four inner lines are physically inside unit 1922's block, and 1922's editor shows its block verbatim today. If they stay there, the same four lines are editable from two selections at once.

B1 Unchanged — 1922 keeps every line of its block

The editor is a view of a slice of the file, and this slice contains those lines. Both panes edit them; the last write wins, as with any two views of one document.
Orders— unit 1922
@tax
form 1
name unit "Recruits"
buy 5 plainsmen
study combat
end
give new 1 100 silv
  • Nothing is hidden: the orders file you will send is the text you can see.
  • Deleting a new unit is just deleting its form…end, where you would look for it.
  • Nothing to build and nothing to explain.
  • The same three lines are editable in two places, and the units pane offers both.

B2 Folded — 1922 shows the block as one line

The form 1…end run collapses to a single uneditable line naming the unit it forms. Clicking it selects new-1, which is where those orders are now edited.
Orders— unit 1922
@tax
form 1 — 3 orders for new 1 ›
give new 1 100 silv
  • Each line of the file is editable in exactly one place.
  • 1922's own month reads as its own month, undiluted by three units' worth of nesting.
  • A fold in a plain-text editor: paste, select-all, undo and the Order OCD indent pass all have to respect it, and it is the largest thing on this page to build.
  • Text you are about to send to the server is not on screen.

Question 4 yours · What an emptied editor does to the unit

Select new-1, select all, delete. The three inner lines go. The question is whether form 1 and end go with them — because if they do, the unit stops existing and the row you were standing on disappears out from under you.

E1 The form/end pair stays

1922's block keeps an empty form 1…end. The row stays, selected, with nothing in its editor — exactly what an existing unit whose orders you deleted does.
new 1
no orders
unit 1922
@tax
form 1
end
give new 1 100 silv
  • Deleting orders never deletes a unit; the selection you are standing on survives the keystroke.
  • An empty form 1…end is a legal, harmless order that still forms a unit.
  • Removing a new unit means going to 1922's block and deleting two lines by hand.

E2 The pair goes too, and the unit with it

Emptying the editor removes form 1 and end. The new-1 row vanishes on the next preview, and the selection falls back to the hex.
no unit
Select a unit to write its orders.
unit 1922
@tax
give new 1 100 silv
  • One gesture removes a new unit, from the pane where it lives.
  • A keystroke deletes a unit and moves the selection; the give new 1 left behind is now an order to nobody.
  • No other editor in the app destroys the thing it is editing when you clear it.

The states, whichever way it goes

These are what I would build under any of the answers above — shown so you can object now rather than at verification. Wording, sizes and colours here are mine unless you say otherwise.

S1 A form with nothing in it

Orders— new 1, in unit 1922's block
  • An empty editable pane, not a notice. There is nothing wrong: this unit simply has no orders yet.

S2 A form the file never closes

The document says form 1 and no end before the next unit line. The server would reject the file; the editor still has to show something.
Orders— new 1, in unit 1922's block
buy 5 plainsmen
study combat
  • Everything to the end of 1922's block is the new unit's, and an edit writes back into that same run. The missing end is already an order-validation error against 1922's block and is reported there, not here.

S3 Two hexes each forming NEW 1

Legal — rules/form scopes an alias to the region. The selection is already a hex plus a unit, so each hex's new-1 resolves inside its own hex and the two never meet. ah-9o0c.2 is fixing the units table's own cross-hex list separately.
  • No new decision here; recorded so it is on the page rather than discovered later.

S4 The unit whose form is inside another form

form 1 containing form 2 forms two units, and the inner one's creator is new-1 rather than a reported unit (the core already models this). Its editor holds the lines between form 2 and its end, on the same rule as everything above.
  • Falls out of the design rather than needing one of its own — noted so the plan says so and it gets a test.
My recommendation: A1, B1, D1, E1. A1 because it makes a formed unit's editor the same object as every other unit's editor — the form 1 line is its header, and no unit's editor shows its header. B1 because a fold is the one thing on this page that could hide text you are about to send to the server, and because deleting a new unit has to be possible somewhere. A1 with B1 does mean the three lines are editable from two selections; I think that is the honest description of a nested block, and it is what a text editor with two views of one file has always done. D1 because one rule beats two, and because a click that says new 1 and lands on 1922 is the report you filed. E1 because no other editor here deletes its subject when you clear it, and because the selection surviving a delete matters more than saving two keystrokes.

The pairing I would warn against is A2 with B1: read-only lines in the formed unit's editor and the same lines fully editable in 1922's, one pane apart.
