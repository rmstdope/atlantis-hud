Every check, on a unit whose skills nothing knows — round 5

Bead ah-jw85. You chose S2 — every check and I2 in round 4. S2 is not one decision but two, and the second only shows up once you look at what a formed unit actually is to a check.

The fact underneath all three options. The preview builds a formed unit as a real row, and a GIVE into it moves items and men — but never skills. There is nowhere for them to come from: the game splits a giver's skills across the men it hands over, and nothing in this app models that. So a formed unit's skill list is empty, always, no matter what its parent gives it.

You have pointed out the rule that settles this: in the game the men take their skills with them, so a unit given five Lumberjacks can produce lumber. The app does not model that for any unit, formed or not — which makes it a second question, below the first: does ah-jw85 take it on, or does a bead of its own?

The hex

Tax Collector 1922 forms NEW 1, hands it 100 silver and five of its men, and tells it to produce lumber. In the game this works: the men take their skill with them. In the app, the new unit has five men and no skills.

unit 1922 GIVE NEW 1 100 SILV GIVE NEW 1 5 PLAI FORM 1 PRODUCE LUMB END @TAX

Five men, and an empty Skills cell. That empty cell is what every skill-reading check will judge it on.

The choice

All three below are S2 — every check runs over the formed unit. They differ only in what the unit looks like when the checks meet it, and whether the checks that cannot be right are allowed to speak.

E1Every check, on a bare unit

The formed unit is the husk the FORM line creates: no men, no stock, no skills, whatever its parent gives it. Every check runs.

Problems

  • ⚠new 1short $100: this unit can have $100 and its orders spend $200
  • ⚠new 1cannot produce lumber: needs Lumberjack 1, has no Lumberjack
  • Simplest to build and to explain: one rule, no exceptions.
  • The idle check stays quiet by itself — it already exempts a unit with no men.
  • The red line is wrong, and the player cannot make it go away. They did give the unit five men who know the skill.
  • The row also shows the unit with no men and no stock, contradicting the Items cell the table already fills in.

E2Every check, on the unit as its parent leaves it

The formed unit carries the men and stock its parent's GIVEs hand it — the reading the table already shows. Skills are still empty, because nothing models them.

Problems

  • ⚠new 1short $100: this unit can have $100 and its orders spend $200
  • ⚠new 1cannot produce lumber: needs Lumberjack 1, has no Lumberjack
  • ⚠new 1has no order that spends the month
  • The unit the checks judge is the unit the table shows — no second, poorer reading of the same row.
  • Strictly worse than E1 for false warnings. Giving the unit men switches off the "no men" exemption that was silencing the idle check, so a formed unit now collects that line too.
  • The skill line is unchanged — men move, skills never do.

E3Every check, except those that read a skill it cannot have

Every check runs, on the unit as its parent leaves it — but a check whose judgement rests on the unit's own skills skips a unit whose skills the report never stated. Exactly the exemption the idle check already makes for a unit with no men.

Problems

  • ⚠new 1short $100: this unit can have $100 and its orders spend $200

A BUY the market will not sell, a GIVE to a unit that is not here, a reused FORM alias — all still warn. Only the skill-resting ones stay quiet.

  • Everything a player can act on is caught; nothing they cannot is said.
  • Uses a rule this codebase already made, for the same reason, rather than a new one.
  • A real mistake — forming a unit, giving it no men and telling it to produce — goes unwarned until the silver runs short.

The precedent E3 leans on

This is not a new idea. check_idle_units already carries it, in the codebase, today:

// A unit with no men cannot spend a month on anything, so there is no order the player
// could add that would satisfy this - the advice would be unfollowable rather than merely
// unwelcome (ah-udff, revising ah-dwk6's "no exemptions").

ah-dwk6 shipped "no exemptions" and ah-udff reversed it, for the same reason E3 exists: a warning nobody can act on is worse than silence. E1 and E2 are "no exemptions" again, on a different check.

The second question — the skills themselves

Modelling skills-with-men is not a FORM feature. It changes the Skills cell for every GIVE of men between any two units in the game, formed or long-standing, and it needs its own reading of the rules: skill knowledge is held in man-days, so moving five men out of a unit of eight moves a share of its learning, and both units' levels can change. That is a modelling decision with its own screen consequences and its own verification.

B1A bead of its own

ah-jw85 ships the column, the mark and the naming, with E3's exemption holding the false warnings back. A new bead models skills-with-men, and lifts the exemption as it lands.

  • The P1 column fix ships now rather than behind a rules-modelling exercise.
  • The skills work gets its own verification, which it needs — it changes every unit that gives men away.
  • The exemption is scaffolding, and scaffolding sometimes stays up. The new bead has to name it as the thing it removes.

B2The same bead

ah-jw85 grows to include skills-with-men, so E2 becomes correct and no exemption is needed at all.

  • Nothing temporary is built, and no warning is ever wrong on screen.
  • A bug about one column becomes a change to how every transfer of men is modelled. Much the largest thing in the bead, and the last to be verified.
  • The column fix waits for it.

B3A bead of its own, and ah-jw85 waits for it

Split the difference: file the skills bead, block ah-jw85 on it, and ship both together with E2 and no exemption.

  • Two verifications instead of one big one, and still nothing temporary.
  • The P1 column fix is now blocked behind a bead nobody has ranked yet.
Chosen: E2 with B1 — and E2 was then refined, in the same discussion, by a principle that supersedes the three cards above: a newly formed unit is treated as far as possible as a normal unit, with the one exception that at the start of the turn it has no items and no skills. So the checks and the arithmetic read it as 0 men, no items, no skills, and anything given to it arrives as income and intents exactly as it does for a unit the report shows. No exemption is built (that is E3, not taken); the men == 0 exemptions the checks already carry apply to it of their own accord. The wrong cannot produce lumber line does ship, and ah-z73s is what removes it.

The recommendation was E3 with B1. It is the S2 you chose — every check sees the unit — with the one exemption this codebase has already paid to learn. E2 is the option I would most warn against on its own: it looks like the most faithful reading of the row and produces more false warnings than doing nothing, because it removes the protection E1 gets by accident. E2 only becomes right once the skills come with the men — which is B2 or B3.
