ah-sefq — a SAIL step between two land hexes

Unit 3493 (Farmers) stands in mountain (36,4). Every one of its six exits is land — five mountain and a volcano. The player writes SAIL N. Nothing is said. No mark in the gutter, nothing in Problems. The turn is sent, and the fleet spends the month exactly where it started.

rules/movement_sailing, verbatim: “A fleet can move from an ocean region to another ocean region, or from a coastal region to an ocean region, or from an ocean region to a coastal region.” So at least one end of every step must be ocean. Land to land is never legal, whatever the fleet, its capacity or its crew.

Question 1 — answered: A Problems only; the map half is filed as its own bead

The map already dots a route from the step the game would refuse. Its rule (movement::plan::blocks) asks only about the hex being entered: land is refused unless that hex is coastal. That is right in almost every case, and wrong in exactly one — a step from one coastal land hex into another coastal land hex. The rules refuse it; the map's rule allows it. In your fixture's own hex it makes no difference (mountain 36,4's neighbours are inland), but it is a case that exists.

The navigator's addition flying ships are not bound by the water

Answering Question 2 the navigator added: “if the ship or the entire fleet consists of flying ships, like balloons, this restriction do not apply.” That is right, and it is not something the committed rules page says: rules/movement_sailing never mentions flying ships at all. The evidence in the committed sources is the catalogue — data/BALL: “This is a flying 'ship' with a capacity of 100…” — and the navigator, who plays the game.

So the warning stays silent for a fleet that may be flying, on the panel's own standing policy: accept on doubt. A false warning costs the player their confidence in every other line on the screen.

And when it cannot tell

Silence, every time. The same policy, applied to every gap.

  • A fleet with any flying hull aboard — silent, even if the rest of it floats.
  • A hull the catalogue does not carry, or no ruleset loaded at all — silent: it cannot be shown not to fly.
  • A unit with no vessel under it at all — the warning fires (Question 2, A): nothing is there to fly.
  • A neighbour the report never described — silent, as before.
