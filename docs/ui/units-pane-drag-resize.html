ah-2r3 — Drag to resize the “Units in hex” pane

Chosen: Q1 Option A — the dragged height is the height · Q2 free pixels · Q3 minimum one row (5.5rem), ceiling 70% of the map column · migration: start at the default of twelve rows, the old row-count and “Fixed pane size” settings are forgotten · handle named “Resize units pane”. Rejected: the dragged height as a ceiling (the map would jump between hexes again — the very thing gh-212 asked to stop); snapping to rows (22px jumps under the pointer, unlike every other pane); a three-row floor; keeping the 45% ceiling; converting the saved row count into a height once. The full interview is recorded in bead ah-2r3's design field.

The pane at the bottom of the map gets the same grip pill the unit/orders split has (ah-13o), on its top edge; the row-count stepper leaves the pane's header and the “Units in hex list” slider and “Fixed pane size” checkbox leave Settings. The header keeps its title, hint and filter. Every pane below is live — drag the pill. The handle flashes amber at a limit; double-click resets to the default height; when the pane is folded to its title bar the handle is hidden, as the orders one is.

Question 1 — what happens on a hex with fewer units than fit?

Drag each pane tall, then switch the buttons under it between a full hex, a hex with three units and an empty hex. This is the ah-3cs fixed-vs-ceiling question again, now with a dragged height instead of a row count.

Option A — the dragged height is the height chosen

Units in hex— plain (7,53), 40 units filter units…▾

Like every other pane: what you dragged is what you get. A short list leaves blank pane below it; the map behind never jumps when you click between hexes. This is what “Fixed pane size” did, now the only behaviour.

Option B — the dragged height is a ceiling

Units in hex— plain (7,53), 40 units filter units…▾

Today's default: the pane hugs a short list and grows back to your height on a full one, so the map behind shifts as you move between hexes. The height you dragged is a maximum, and dragging on a three-unit hex shows nothing until you visit a fuller one.

Question 2 — snap to whole rows, or free pixels?

Rows are 22px. Free pixels can leave a partial row at the bottom edge — normal for a scrolling list, and what the region and unit panes do. Snapping keeps the edge on a row boundary but the handle moves in 22px jumps under the pointer.

Question 3 — how small, how tall?

Below the minimum the handle flashes amber and stops; likewise at the ceiling. Today's ceiling is 45% of the window height; the chosen ceiling is 70% of the map's overlay column. The minimum is the title bar plus the column header plus this many rows.
