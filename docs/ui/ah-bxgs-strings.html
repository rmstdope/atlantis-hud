Round 2 — every sentence, as it will ship

You chose A (the receiver's row moves), U1 (goods leave for a target the report cannot see, and the hover says so), R1 (refused goods stay put and the hover says why), and for Q4 the rule “anything a create … spell can make is an artefact and cannot be transported”.

Your Q4 rule measures cleanly, and it is better than any option I offered. The game has 21 skills whose name begins create; 18 of them name an item that exists, and every one of those 18 requires artifact lore. The other three — create aura of fear, create phantasmal beasts, create phantasmal demons — make no item at all, and the beasts and demons are already refused as summoned creatures. So the rule adds exactly 18 tags and removes all doubt about nine of the fourteen I asked about.

What it leaves. Refused: 105 of 171 items. Five items are still unaccounted for by any rule: PORT portal, MWAG magic wagon, GLID glider, HPOT healing potion, IENT imprisoned entity. Under your rule they are transportable. Question 5 below is whether that is what you meant.

The sender's hover — W1: departures are named   recommended

Every transport out gets its own line, in document order, so the hover reads as a list of what left and where it went. The arrival side already names its counterparty, and this keeps the two ends symmetric.

*5530Miners6mining 3120 SILV, 10 STON, 2 HORS—
hoverwas: 120 SILV, 40 STON, 2 HORS, 5 FUR Sends 30 STON to unit 6857. The game will not transport horses, so they stay with this unit. Sends 5 FUR to unit 1234, which your report does not show.
  • The player can read off every transport they wrote, and see at a glance which one did nothing.
  • One shape of sentence for both the ordinary and the unverifiable case, differing only in the trailing clause.
  • A unit with six transports has a seven-line hover.

W2 — only the exceptional lines are named

An ordinary transport out says nothing: the falling figure and the italic already carry it. Only the refusal and the unverifiable target earn a sentence.

The receiver's hover — the timing clause

rules/sequenceofevents puts transport last, after movement and production, so arriving goods cannot be spent, sold, given or carried this month. The column says this about production already. Two ways to say it here:

T1 the full clause  recommended

Exactly the shape the production sentence uses.

hover on unit 6857was: 88 SILV, 15 STON Includes 30 STON transported from unit 5530. Transport resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month.
  • A player who has met the production sentence has met this one.
  • Long, and repeated once per arriving item.

T2 the clause once, at the end

Name each arrival plainly; state the timing once, however many arrive.

hover on unit 6857was: 88 SILV, 15 STON, 4 SPEA Includes 30 STON transported from unit 5530. Includes 12 SPEA transported from unit 4670. Transport resolves last, so transported goods cannot be spent this month.
  • Scales: five arrivals cost five short lines and one clause.
  • A second wording of a sentence the column already ships, and the clause floats free of the line it qualifies.

A row that both sends and receives

A quartermaster receiving from a field unit and forwarding to a second quartermaster is the ordinary middle of a supply chain, and the game runs all three phases in one month. Both kinds of line appear in one hover, arrivals first:
