Q7 — where the transport lines sit among the ones already there

The hover already has a fixed reading order: what the report said, what was taken, what will be produced, what a BUY ALL settles at, what a cast will bring, the production cap, what a BUILD spends, the cast cap, and last of all what could not be counted.

H1 gains with the gains, losses just before the end  recommended

What arrives joins the other Includes… lines. What leaves, and what refused to leave, go together after everything the unit gained — still ahead of the cannot be counted line, which stays last always.

hoverwas: 88 SILV, 15 STON, 2 HORS, 5 FUR Includes 12 STON this unit will produce. Production resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month. Includes 12 SPEA transported from unit 5530. Transport resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month. Sends 30 STON to unit 16340. The game will not transport horses, so they stay with this unit. Sends 5 FUR to unit 4670.
  • Everything the unit gains reads as one block, everything it loses as another. The two transport ends never interleave with each other.
  • The three transport lines that came from the player's own block stay in the order the player wrote them.

H2 all five transport lines together, at the end

Transport is one subject, so it reads as one paragraph — arrivals first, then departures and refusals in document order.

hoverwas: 88 SILV, 15 STON, 2 HORS, 5 FUR Includes 12 STON this unit will produce. Production resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month. Includes 12 SPEA transported from unit 5530. Transport resolves last, so they cannot be spent this month. Sends 30 STON to unit 16340. The game will not transport horses, so they stay with this unit. Sends 5 FUR to unit 4670.
  • On this unit H1 and H2 read identically — and that is the point of showing it. They only differ when a BUILD or a cast cap is also present, where H2 would push those two above the arrivals and H1 would leave them below.
  • Pulls the transport block away from the Includes… family it belongs to grammatically.

Q8 — how the refusal sentence names the item

You approved “The game will not transport horses, so they stay with this unit.” There is a catch worth one question: the plural comes from what your report happened to print, which follows the amount the unit holds, not the amount being transported.

P1 the report's own word

Reads naturally when the unit holds several.

holding 2 horsesThe game will not transport horses, so they stay with this unit.
holding 1 horseThe game will not transport horse, so they stay with this unit.
  • The sentence you approved, unchanged, in the common case.
  • Ungrammatical whenever the unit holds exactly one.

P2 the tag  recommended

Every other sentence in this hover already names items by tag.

holding 2 horsesThe game will not transport HORS, so they stay with this unit.
holding 1 horseThe game will not transport HORS, so they stay with this unit.
  • Correct at every amount, and matches Includes 30 STON… and Sends 5 FUR… in the same hover.
  • The tag is what the player typed in the order.
  • Reads as jargon rather than English.

P3 the amount and the tag

Says how much did not go, matching the Sends… lines exactly.

holding 2 horsesWill not send 2 HORS to unit 16340: the game does not transport them.
  • Names the target too, so a unit with several refusals says which order each one was.
  • A longer sentence, and it leads with a negative.
