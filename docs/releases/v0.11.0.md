What's new for you in Atlantis HUD (v0.11.0)
============================================

## Your units' silver

The units-in-hex list has a new **Silver** column showing what each unit will have
left at the end of the month, once its orders have been carried out.

- The column counts what a unit earns — working, taxing, entertaining, selling,
  claiming from your faction's unclaimed fund, gifts from your other units, and
  magic that pays, including Earth Lore
- It counts what a unit spends — buying, studying, giving silver away,
  withdrawing, and spells that cost money
- Wages are treated the way the game treats them: they arrive too late in the
  month to pay for a purchase, but in time to pay upkeep, so a unit funded only
  by this month's wages is no longer shown as able to afford something the game
  will refuse
- Monthly upkeep can be counted against the figure or left out of it, from
  Settings
- Units set to CONSUME are fed from food instead of silver — their own food
  first, then their faction's food in the same hex — and each unit says how much
  of its upkeep the food covered
- Hover a unit's silver to see the whole calculation broken out: held now, coming
  in in time, coming in too late, going out, and the month-end figure
- You are warned when a unit cannot afford its orders, when the units in a hex
  are short between them, and when your units claim more silver than your faction
  actually has

## Map

- The world now repeats across the wrap seam, so panning east or west runs
  continuously into the next copy instead of into emptiness
- The ruler and the arrow keys speak the map's real coordinates at the seam,
  so panning past the edge labels hexes you actually have
- When you start a new game you set the map's width, height and whether it wraps,
  prefilled with the right defaults for the variant you chose
- The form refuses a wrap that an odd map size cannot support, where the two
  edges genuinely would not meet
- Lairs, caves, ruins, crypts, ice caves, demon pits, whirlpools, gateways and
  the rest are drawn as what they are instead of as anonymous buildings
- Fleets are drawn as ships, and the Ships badge counts the vessels moored rather
  than the number of fleets
- The badges selector has moved next to the zoom buttons, so every map control
  sits in one corner

## Writing orders

- Order OCD indents nested FORM and TURN blocks one level per depth as you type
- END and ENDTURN line up with the block they close, as soon as you press Enter
- Each unit's orders end with exactly one blank line, so clicking below the last
  order always puts the cursor on a fresh line ready to type
- A unit with no men left is no longer warned that it has no month-long order —
  there was no order you could have given it

## Reading your turn report

- Units and factions whose names contain a comma, or an unclosed bracket, are
  read properly instead of being dropped or attributed to the wrong faction
- Any line of the report the app cannot read is now reported, with a count and
  the raw text, instead of disappearing silently
- Units in your own faction are no longer marked as estimates — their numbers
  come straight from the report and are exact
- A unit's skills show their study points as well as their level — "MINI 2 (90)"
  rather than "MINI 2" — so you can tell two units a month apart in study

## Game data reference

- The game data can be opened from the command palette or by keyboard shortcut,
  so you can browse it without knowing what you are looking for
- Clicking a fleet gives you one link per vessel type — Galleons, Galleys,
  Balloons — each opening that ship's entry, instead of one search for the whole
  fleet summary
- The dialog stops short of the bottom of the screen, and moving down its list
  with the keyboard scrolls to keep your selection in view

## Elsewhere

- Settings > About says what Atlantis HUD is, what it needs in order to work,
  which Atlantis variant it supports, and where to report a bug
- Opening a header popover announces itself and takes focus, and closing it puts
  focus back where it was
