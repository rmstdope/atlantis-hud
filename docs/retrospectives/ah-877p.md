## A correct owner rule on a coast was wrong at sea, and the suite could not tell

**What happened.** The first fix made `fleet_owner` (crates/core/src/movement/fleet.rs) hand a hull
to the next unit aboard when its owner LEAVEs. It came out green on every suite and on the fast gate.
The cold review then showed it was wrong at sea: the engine refuses LEAVE in water from a unit that
cannot swim or fly, or that won't cross water (`Do1EnterOrder`; `rules/leave`, `rules/nocross`). The
old fallback to the departed owner had been right there by accident. The fleet.rs owner tests the fix
added or corrected all ran on an ocean scene, so they asserted engine-wrong answers. The follow-up
delta review then found that the first attempt at modelling the refusal left out NOCROSS, which every
new unit starts with set.

**Why.** `standing_after_in` (orders/standing.rs), the one rule every reader asks for "where does
this unit stand after ENTER/LEAVE", does not model a refused LEAVE at sea. The gap is written down,
but only in `orders::semantics::walk_after_boarding`'s doc, which the owner rule never pointed to.
Nothing at `standing_after_in` or `OrderedUnits::structure_of` says it. Separately, the reproduction
scenes were built on whatever fixture the nearby tests used (`owner_scene`, an ocean hex), with no
check of whether its terrain changes the rule being tested.

**Cost.** Two extra review rounds and two extra commits (about forty minutes), one scope question to
the navigator, and a follow-up bead (ah-djst) for the surfaces still affected.

**Prevent by.** State the at-sea LEAVE gap at `orders/standing.rs` `standing_after_in`, where every
reader of a unit's standing looks, until ah-djst closes it. A candidate entry for `.cerebro/traps.md`:
"a LEAVE at sea is refused for a non-swimmer or a won't-cross-water unit, and `structure_of` does not
model it. Any test of LEAVE belongs on a coast hex unless it is about the sea."

**Seen before.** ah-ssd. Its second section recorded "a unit that writes SAIL SE and then LEAVE still
gave that order — the server reads the SAIL line before running the LEAVE" as established. The engine
says otherwise: `RunEnterOrders` runs before `RunMovementOrders`, which takes the owner as it then
stands. This bead corrected that belief and the test it produced (`a_captain_that_also_leaves_*`).
Both times, a belief about turn order passed every suite because the tests encoded the same belief.
